Distance into rate

A point in the depth world is a distance — how far behind the picture plane a thing sits. The rate square does not plot that distance; it plots its reciprocal, one per slit: ru = k/(z − Zv) and rv = k/(z − Zh). Each axis is not “distance” but “one over distance from this slit,” because 1/distance is the natural unit of how fast an image feature moves as you change depth: near things swing quickly as you dolly, far things barely move. Drag the point Z through the depth world on the left and watch where it lands on the rate square at right.

The depth world. The two slits sit at Zv = 150 and Zh = 355; the point Z is your draggable object. The two coloured bars are the distances z − Zv and z − Zh — plain lengths. Drag Z toward a slit and its bar shrinks toward zero; drag it far and both bars grow without limit.

The rate square. The same point, plotted as (ru, rv) — the reciprocals of those two distances. Far depth lands near the origin (both rates → 0, the horizon); near a slit sends one rate racing to the edge of the square (r → ∞). The diagonal is ru = rv; the axes are the edges r = 0. Those three lines are the seam — and the point of this plate is that they do not move when you switch the gauge.

What the gauge toggle shows

The constant k is a gauge: a free choice of unit for the rate axes. Switch between k = 1 and k = 100 and watch what happens. The point's numbers change — every ru, rv scales by 100 — and the square's tick labels change with them. But the picture does not: the point sits in the same place relative to the diagonal and the edges, because both axes rescale together. The gauge-invariant fact is the ratio ru / rv = (z − Zh)/(z − Zv), which the readout holds fixed across the toggle. The seam lives on equality (the diagonal) and vanishing (the edges) — both blind to k — which is exactly why it is a law and the tick marks are only a convention.

Conventions

Judgment calls flagged: the point Z is held behind the near slit (z > Zv) so both distances are positive and the reciprocals stay in one quadrant — the full rate square carries all four sign combinations, but one quadrant is enough to see distance become rate; the rate axes are clipped at a finite reach with an arrow where a rate runs past the edge toward its slit (r → ∞); the two gauges shown are k = 1 and k = 100, a plain common rescale — any nonzero k would do, and the invariance is the same. The reciprocal calibration is r = k/(z − Z), the sign convention of the engine.

Glossary

depth world
The scene measured in plain distance behind the picture plane; the left panel.
rate
A per-unit-depth quantity; here 1/distance, the responsiveness of an image feature to a change in depth. Its home is the reciprocal.
rate square
The plane of (ru, rv), reciprocal-depth per slit; scale-space, where the horizon is the finite origin and each slit is a point at infinity.
gauge (here, k)
The free unit of the rate axes. Changing k rescales every number and no picture; the seam is what survives it.
the ratio
ru/rv = (z − Zh)/(z − Zv): the gauge-invariant content of the point's position, unchanged by k.
