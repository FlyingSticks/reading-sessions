Counting the lines of two slits

A paraline drawing (parallel projection: lines parallel in space stay parallel on the page). Each slider is one number. The drawing shows one line at a time. The picture plane can be set at any depth.

3

Words used on this page

slit a
the straight line x = 0 at depth z = 150. It runs up and down (the y direction).
slit b
the straight line y = 0 at depth z = 250. It runs left and right (the x direction).
frame
a drawn rectangle at one depth, only to show where that depth is.
depth z
distance measured along the depth axis from the book's picture plane position, z = 0. The three means are means of the two slit depths, 150 and 250, measured this way.
picture plane
the gold frame. Its depth is set by the gold slider. The dashed gold outline stays at z = 0 for reference.
crossing
the point where the line passes through the picture plane (the hollow gold dot).
x scale, y scale
for a line meeting both slits: crossing x = x scale × (its position on slit b), crossing y = y scale × (its position on slit a).
test rectangle
the gold-filled rectangle in the picture plane: the crossings of all lines whose two numbers both lie between −100 and +100.
bearing
how far the line's direction is turned to the left or right of the depth axis, in degrees.
tilt
how far the line's direction rises above or drops below horizontal, in degrees.
miss
where the line crosses the other slit's frame: its distance from that slit, measured in the frame. Miss 0 means the line meets the slit.
complex
a family of lines that needs three numbers to pick out one line.
third line c
the blue line (fourth tab). It lies in the frame at z = 200, passes through the depth axis, and is turned from the vertical by the angle θ. It meets neither slit.
ruled surface
a surface swept by a straight line as one number runs. Each position of the line is called a ruling line.
doubly ruled surface
a surface that is ruled in two different ways: through each of its points pass two straight lines that lie wholly in the surface. On the fourth tab the green lines are one set of ruling lines and the grey lines are the other set.

Revised 13 Nov 2026: the picture plane can be moved in depth; a fourth tab shows the doubly ruled surface.
