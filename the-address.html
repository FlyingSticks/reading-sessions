The Address

The rate square, the three lines through one corner, and what only two unequal rates can show

Session 4 established three facts by audit; this page puts them under a finger. A rig is a point (ru, rv) in the rate square. The seam is not a region of that square but three concurrent lines — the diagonal and the two axes — meeting at the orthographic corner, where the 1979 miniature lives. Drag the rig and watch what the rulings do: a glide line and a camera path leave every picture-plane mark together, and either they stay one stroke or the camera peels away. The three lines are exactly where no peel happens.

Left: the rate square, washed crimson by |Δ| = |rurv(rv − ru)| and cut by the seam's three green lines — two axes and the diagonal, concurrent at the corner, where the gold ring marks the 1979 miniature. Right: each mark sends out a straight glide line and a camera path that share the mark and its tangent. On the seam the camera rides the glide line — one stroke, two graduations. Off it, the camera peels — but never on the two axis marks: the fused cross survives every rig, which is why a single-channel construction can't show the split at all. The peel is drawn at true scale.

Silence, not collision

The theorem's §5 separates the seam's two kinds of branch. On the diagonal the two channels' gauge responses are equal — both meters run, in lockstep. On an edge one channel's gauge trivializes — g ≡ 1, the readout parked at its picture-plane value for every depth, while the other channel works alone. Both are fusion; only one is a collision. Run the point down the ruling and watch the meters:

One ruling, through (1.3, 0.9); each meter tracks its channel's image coordinate as λ runs. Edge: the v-meter is parked at 0.9 — the readout a camera would print at the picture plane — for every depth; silence. Diagonal: both meters run and their fractional responses are identical at every depth; the tie is drawn when it holds. Split: both run, at different rates — and this is the only one of the three that is off the seam.

Two different animals

Session 4's other discharge deserves its own dial. The miniature's green crossing and the book's seam both go by "where things agree," and they are different kinds of object. The crossing is a depth: at a fixed rig, the one λ where the drawing's graduation matches the camera's local scale — and the drawing unit drags it up and down the line. The seam is a locus in rate space: a condition on the rig itself, and it cannot see the drawing unit at all. Two sliders, two spaces:

Left: the miniature's line — the defect between drawing gauge and camera gauge, depth by depth, with the crossing where they agree. Right: the Δ meter for the rig (ru from the slider, rv held at 0.2). Slide the unit: the crossing swims, the meter does not so much as tremble — Δ is blind to the gauge. Slide the rate: both respond, each in its own space. Agreement-at-a-depth and agreement-of-readings are different animals, and no setting of these dials will ever make one into the other.

The address, then, in one sentence each way. Where the split lives: in the mixed rulings, at rigs with two unequal, nonzero rates — anisotropy, not mere perspective. Where the first book lived: at the corner, all three seam lines at once, both channels silent, magnification 1 at every depth — the one point of the square where the quarrel cannot even be posed, and where classifying pictures by parentage is therefore complete. The present book is what the view looks like when you step off it.
