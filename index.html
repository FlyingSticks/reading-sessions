An account in eight chapters, with instruments

One Congruence, Two Readings

Glide, cross-slit, and the seam where a drawing and a camera agree

The claim

Two ways of turning depth into marks on a line — the glide draftsman's (straight depth lines, graduation free — equal steps, or harmonic with a marked vanishing point, as in Glide Projection, 1984) and the cross-slit camera's (rays through two slits at two depths, graduation welded to foreshortening) — are one line congruence read twice. The two readings attach reciprocal graduations to the same set of lines, one the inverse of the other, with product one. They agree exactly on a seam — where every drawn line is straight under both readings — and split everywhere else. The agreement is harmonic; the split is measured by a single cubic; and the whole apparatus of gauges, involutions and vanishing hierarchies is one copy of the anharmonic group S₃ per channel, acting on both channels at once, its orbits collapsing only on the seam.

That is the whole account. Every page that follows is either an instrument for checking one of those sentences or a consequence of one of them. A construction earns a chapter only if it needs both readings to make its point; what stands on one reading alone is linked, not absorbed.

Before you enter

The chapters run from the theorems up and assume nothing but geometry. Readers arriving from painting, timekeeping, maps or building may want the porch first: three old quarrels — over the canvas, the calendar and the map — each of them this book's argument in another field's clothes.

The rig

One rig throughout: picture plane at z = 0, station depth Zp = 200, slit separation D = 100, vertical slit Zv = 150, horizontal slit Zh = 250, neutral surface z* = 187.5 (the harmonic mean). Glide is drawn teal, cross-slit purple, measured results green. Terms are defined in the glossary, one entry per tap-definition across the chapters.

The chapters embed their instruments live; nothing here is a static image. The full record — every construction, essay, and check this account draws on — remains in the archives: Glide_projection, Double-slit-project, Tetra_engine_complete, Lumped-together, Overflow.

Glide Projection: Lateral Architectural Drawing, Van Nostrand Reinhold, 1984, is the point of departure throughout.
