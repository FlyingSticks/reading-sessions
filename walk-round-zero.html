Walk round zero

The same hole in two places. On a line, removing zero leaves two pieces and a wall between them: that wall is what a sign is. In a plane, removing zero leaves one piece, and the walk goes round.

What to look for

Left. The point cannot reach the other half. Everything on its side is red, everything beyond the hole is blue, and the names are permanent: nothing you do to the point changes which piece it is in. That permanence is the sign. Zero is not in either piece; it is the wall.

Right. The point goes anywhere. The colour under it is its direction from zero, a whole circle of hues, not two. Nothing is permanent: drag the point once round the hole and it comes back with the same z, and the root you were holding has become the other root. No boundary was crossed; the swap happened smoothly, a little at each step. After a second turn it is restored. A loop that does not enclose zero changes nothing, which is how you know it is the hole, not the walking, that does it.

Zero on the right is still a point you cannot stand on, and it still marks where the two roots coincide: an incidence, convention-free. But it is a post you walk round, not a wall you stand beside. That is the whole difference between a zero with a sign and a zero without one.
