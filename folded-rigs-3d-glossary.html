the rig in the round — glossary & equations

Companion sheet to folded-rigs-3d.html. Every term the plate draws or prints, defined in one or two sentences, followed by its equations with the reason each one is there. Same palette, same vocabulary; nothing new is claimed here.

The stage

slits axis (fold hinge, the λ line)
The one line shared by both channel planes. Depth is measured along it, both feet sit on it, and the fold swings the v-plane about it — hence the second name.
picture plane (the screen, λ = 0)
The plane where everything is read. It is the one depth at which both readings, in both channels, print at ×1 — the gauge there is exactly 1, so a mark on the screen means what it says.
traces
The two lines where the channel planes meet the picture plane. Each channel delivers its marks to its own trace; in the face-on view you watch this delivery happen edge-on.
the slab
The band of depths 0 ≤ λ ≤ 1, from the picture plane to the back wall — the working territory where the point lives.
back wall (λ = 1)
The dashed far edge of the slab; unit depth.
u-plane (the drawing sheet)
The channel plane that never moves. It carries the u readings, the ladders, and — in the sheet preset — becomes the flat drawing of folded-rigs-v2.
v-plane (the folding plane)
The channel plane that swings with θ. It carries the v readings; the fold lays it onto the u-plane at θ = 0.

The actors

u, v (the feet)
The positions of the two slits on the slits axis, both behind the screen (u, v < 0). Each channel runs both of its readings through its own foot. Draggable along the axis.
ℓᵤ, ℓᵥ (the slits)
The slit lines themselves: ℓᵤ vertical at depth u, ℓᵥ horizontal at depth v. A camera ray must thread both.
the point (λ, X, Y)
The scene point in rig coordinates: depth λ along the slits axis, plan coordinate X in the u channel, elevation coordinate Y in the v channel.
P = (λ, X), Q = (λ, Y) (the shadows)
The point's ⊥ shadows in the two channel planes — the two dots you actually drag. The dotted “same λ” recall line ties them to one depth on the axis.
pencils
The faint fans of rays out of each foot — each channel's whole supply of camera lines, drawn thin so the two in use stand out.
fold θ
The dihedral angle between the channel planes: 0° is folded flat (both channels on one sheet of paper), 90° is fully opened (the physical rig).

The three ways to read

camera reading (cross-slit)
Ray from the scene point to the slit, read where it crosses the screen. It pulls in: mark Xc = X·m. Equal depth steps print harmonically. Law and gauge welded by optics — the physical ray fixes both the incidence and the scale in one act.
glide reading
Ray from the footprint to the slit, read at the scene's own depth, then carried home along the level plane to the trace. It pushes out: mark Xg = X/m. Equal depth steps print arithmetically (Reach). Gauge free — the carrying-home is a level translation and imposes no scale of its own.
corner mark (the ⊥ reading)
x = X, y = Y — the perpendicular projection, i.e. the reading with both feet pushed to −∞. It is the mean proportional between the other two marks.
footprint
The ⊥ landing of the point in its channel plane — where the glide stroke starts. Drawn as an open circle on the trace.
carried home
The dashed level segment that transports the glide mark from scene depth back to the trace — same height, no rescaling.
channel gauge m
The one number a channel assigns to a depth: m = (−s)/(λ − s) for foot s. Written m_u, m_v. Both readings are this same gauge, applied in opposite directions.

On the plane: marks, prints, ladders

marks on traces
The six tick readings the traces carry: Xc, x, Xg on the u trace; Yc, y, Yg on the v trace. On each trace the three form a geometric progression — camera in, corner mid, glide out.
camera print (Xc, Yc)
The two camera marks assembled into a plane coordinate; filled dot. Not a construction: it is the strike.
the strike
Where the physical 3D ray through both slits meets the screen — checked to the decimal, strike = (X·mᵤ, Y·mᵥ), at every fold angle.
glide print (Xg, Yg)
The two glide marks assembled; open dot. It stands one reciprocal out from the corner print on each axis.
⊥ print (x, y)
The corner pair; small square. The geometric middle of the rectangle the other two prints span.
one square, two rectangles
The dashed square on the plane prints twice: the camera rectangle (area × mᵤ·mᵥ, filled wash) and the glide rectangle (area × 1/(mᵤ·mᵥ), outline). The two area factors multiply to 1. This is the v2 front-view inset promoted to the object itself.
ladders (equal depth steps)
One depth line at height X, sampled at equal steps of λ and imaged both ways in the u channel: the camera ticks crowd harmonically, the glide circles march out arithmetically.

Meters, identities, countries

r_u, r_v
Reciprocal foot distances, r_u = 1/(−u), r_v = 1/(−v) — the natural strengths of the two feet (a foot at −∞ has strength 0).
D = |u − v|
The separation of the feet along the slits axis — the rig's baseline.
AR = m_v/m_u
The aspect between the two channels' gauges at the current depth — how anisotropically the rig is printing right now.
seam meter Δ
Δ = r_u·r_v·(r_v − r_u). Zero exactly when the readings fuse (SEAM); away from zero the camera peels off the glide (SPLIT).
fuse v → u
Slide the v foot onto the u foot: Δ = 0, one center and one denominator, the prints' rectangle squares up, and the readings' drawn lines fuse into perspective.
hinge identity
Xg·Xc = x² — the corner mark is the geometric mean of the other two. The readout carries it live in both channels as Xg·Xc/x² = 1.
drafting country
θ = 0, folded flat: both channels on one sheet of paper; the glide strokes never leave this plane.
camera country
θ = 90°, fully opened: a ray must thread both slits; only here is the forward reading honest optics.
the 3D ray
The unique line through the point meeting both slits, drawn at every θ as the folded image of itself — honest optics only at 90°. It disappears when the feet fuse (no unique ray through one center).
view presets
¾ round (the orbit's home); face the picture plane (depth axis projects to a single point, both channel planes arrive edge-on, prints at true coordinates); sheet (u-plane flat-on, recovering v2's drawing). The orbit itself is a plain orthographic turn — drafting rules, no vanishing tricks.

The equations

m = (−s)/(λ − s) m_u = m(u, λ) m_v = m(v, λ)
The channel gauge: what a foot at s does to material at depth λ when it is brought to the screen. At λ = 0 it is exactly 1 (the ×1 picture plane); it shrinks as the point goes deeper.
Xc = X·m_u Yc = Y·m_v (camera — pull in) Xg = X/m_u Yg = Y/m_v (glide — push out) x = X y = Y (corner — both feet at −∞)
The three readings of one point. One gauge, used three ways: multiplied, divided, ignored.
Xg·Xc = x² Yg·Yc = y²
The harmonic hinge: (X/m)(X·m) = X². Equivalently Xc, x, Xg is a geometric progression with ratio 1/m — the reciprocal pair with the corner as mean proportional.
strike = (X·m_u, Y·m_v) = camera print
The physical ray through both slits lands on the screen exactly at the camera print, at every fold angle — the plate's central check, and the reason the print is drawn filled.
r_u = 1/(−u) r_v = 1/(−v) D = |u − v| Δ = r_u·r_v·(r_v − r_u)
The seam meter and its ingredients. Δ = 0 exactly when the feet coincide (or a foot retreats to infinity, killing its strength) — the zero set where camera and glide stop being distinguishable and perspective takes over.
(λ, X, Y) ↦ (λ, X + Y·cos θ, Y·sin θ)
The fold map, sending rig coordinates to space at fold angle θ. It is linear at every θ, so straight lines stay straight and every incidence the flat plate drew survives the orbit untouched. Its height direction is h(θ) = (0, cos θ, sin θ); at θ = 0 the v-plane lies in the u-plane, at 90° it stands perpendicular.
λ_k = k·λ/N, k = 1…N camera: X·m(u, λ_k) = X/(1 + λ_k·r_u) — harmonic glide: X/m(u, λ_k) = X·(1 + λ_k·r_u) — arithmetic (Reach)
The ladders. Equal steps of depth print as a harmonic progression under the camera (its reciprocals are equally spaced) and as an arithmetic progression under the glide, with common difference X·r_u·λ/N — the foot's strength r_u appearing as the glide's slope.
camera area = q²·(m_u·m_v) glide area = q²/(m_u·m_v) product of factors = 1
The dashed square of side q printing to its two rectangles: camera in, glide out, areas in exact reciprocal. Fusing the feet makes m_u = m_v and both rectangles square up.
t = (λ − u)/(v − u) ray ∩ ℓ_u = (u, 0, Y/(1−t)) ray ∩ ℓ_v = (v, X/t, 0)
The 3D ray's two slit crossings, parametrized by how far along the baseline the depth λ sits. It needs u ≠ v — with the feet fused there is no unique ray, which is why the plate hides it.
