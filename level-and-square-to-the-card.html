Level, and Square to the Card

Two conditions that are easy to confuse. A line at a right angle to slit a is level, and its bearing is still free. A line square to the card has no freedom left. Slit b at infinity is the first kind of thing, taken all together, not the second.

Addendum · sign

One point at infinity, two senses

A level ray has one point at infinity, B, yet it can be followed two ways. The two ways are the two senses of the line. Parallel lines share a direction but not a sense, so B does not record which way you went. Calling one end plus is a choice; incidence does not supply it. The pairing of P with P′ swaps the two senses, and it can be made in two ways that differ in sign.

Terms

card
The flat reference surface that carries slit a, with its two ruled directions ACROSS and UP.
slit a
A straight cut in the card, here parallel to UP.
level
At a right angle to UP, and so at a right angle to slit a. Said of a line, a direction or a plane.
level plane
A plane at a right angle to slit a. There is one at every height.
level direction
A direction lying in the level planes. There is one for each bearing.
z′
The direction square to the card: at a right angle to both UP and ACROSS. It is the level direction with bearing 0.
square to the card
In the direction z′. Used for nothing else on these plates.
bearing
The angle, measured in a level plane, from z′ to a level direction.
height
The position of a point along slit a, measured in the direction UP.
point at infinity
What a family of parallel lines has in common: one direction. It has no place on a paraline and is shown by an arrowhead.
horizon
The collection of all level directions. In a perspective view, the line where all level planes appear to meet. In a paraline it has no place, and shows only as the fact that every level plane carries the same fan of directions.
slit b at infinity
The horizon, used as the second slit.
tilt
The angle between a ray and the level plane through the point where it crosses slit a.
sense
One of the two ways of following a line. A direction has two senses. Which one is called plus is a choice.
mirror with point A
On a line: the pairing of each point with the point at the same distance on the other side of A.
half-turn about slit a
The turn through 180° about slit a. It is the product of two mirrors whose planes meet at right angles along slit a.
sign
+1 for a map made of an even number of mirrors, −1 for an odd number.
P*
The point P is carried to by mirror 1 alone, before mirror 2 is applied.
congruence
A two-number family of lines in space (first meaning).
