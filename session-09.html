Is the glass transparent?

Read this session: the question that governed the week — answered in the negative for the surface, and then answered again, unexpectedly, by the range itself

Filed 13 September from the working record. The session opened on the means sphere with the reader's observation that the three-means arrangement is probe-independent, and his question: is the glass transparent? It seems to operate more as an image plane than a picture plane.

Answered Image plane, not picture plane

No — not in the sense the word promises. The glass is terminal: rays land on it and stop, the mark inverted, the anatomy of a camera back and not a window. The structural reason cuts deeper than the positional one: transparency needs somewhere to stand, and the rig has two slit-lines and no centre — no station from which the glass reads as a section of sight. The glass is transparent to depth along a ray (the probe's irrelevance, seen from the other side) while opaque as a surface; and the means arrangement ignores the probe because it is rig geometry — calibration, not scene. The profile figure was recognized as the classical station-point construction wearing the rig's clothes: the semicircle on two horizon points, the right angle on the circle, the altitude equal to the geometric mean. One window reading was licensed, and only one: the sixth check's equivalent pinhole at the neutral surface.

Landed The virtual centre

The comparison that struck the reader — the computed centre against the vanishing points of perspective — was built as Two pivots, one virtual centre: one drawing containing two pivots you can thread and one centre you can only compute. Its check ran first, 88 assertions across four rigs: C from the polar formula against C solved from the cross-ratio condition; (B, D; A, C) = −1; the threaded channel's mark invariant along the pivot line with both real channels asserted non-invariant along the C-line; rate closure; every mark inverted. The plate went out in SVG rather than canvas — a flagged departure, for label crispness on the iPad. And a line worth keeping from the same sitting: hand someone the glass alone and they cannot distinguish the two-slit camera from a pinhole at C — sizes can be forged by a fiction, incidence cannot; from a single frame, "which camera took this?" is answerable only up to harmonic conjugacy.

Found Three answers to "conjugate of what?"

The sitting's deepest return: the three means are three answers to one question. HM is the harmonic conjugate of the glass with respect to the slits; AM is the conjugate of infinity; GM is the point the swap leaves alone. The polar plane, restricted to the axis, collapses to harmonic conjugacy — the sphere's whole tangent apparatus done by the pair {B, D} in one dimension fewer. And since the fourth harmonic point is straightedge-constructible, the horizon carrying A, B, D projectively determines the equivalent pinhole before any sphere is drawn: the range came with its own station point folded inside. Transparency, when it finally arrives, arrives as the glass's own reflection through the slit pair. One arrow of the working figure was corrected along the way — the record notes the repair without re-arguing it.

Open at close of session

The reader annotated the means-sphere profile — the far slit dragged to 355, a two-point square constructed on the range, and the question "equivalent pinhole plane?" written beside the gold line — and those annotations became the commission that opens session 10. The sitting closed the morning of 12 September with the question it had raised still warm.
