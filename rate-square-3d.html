The rate square, in the round

The abstract square (ru, rv) put back on the machine it describes. Drag the point and the slits move; drag the scene to orbit; slide the probe and watch one ray thread both slits.

The square is the camera's law-space: two numbers the slits are free to set. Each point of it is a rig. On the gold lines the camera's image of a depth line is straight — inside the draftsman's class — and Turn welds tick to mark. Off them the dark curve bows, and no ruler on the teal line can follow it.

What to look for

The crimson bar is the vertical slit, which owns the u-channel; the purple bar is the horizontal slit, which owns v. The dark ray from the probe is the unique line through both — its crossing point rides each bar as the probe moves, which is the whole optics of the rig. Where the ray pierces the glass is the mark, and the dark curve is the trail of marks the depth line leaves: the camera's image of a straight line.

On the diagonal the slits coincide — a pinhole — and the curve is straight. On either edge one slit has gone to infinity — a pushbroom — and the curve is straight again. Everywhere else it bows, and the teal line (the draftsman's law: straight from the footprint) can carry ticks under any ruler you like without ever landing them on the marks. Drag the point off the seam and watch the residual in the readout refuse to close; snap back and turn the ruler to Turn, and the weld returns.

At the orthographic corner both rates are zero, both channels fall silent, and the mark never leaves the footprint: depth unread. That corner is where the 1984 board re-introduces depth by hand — the reason glide exists.

Conventions. Chapter 2 throughout: mark M(z) = (x₀/(1+ruz), y₀/(1+rvz)); slit depths are the reciprocal rates, Z = 1/r; the cross-slit preset is the book rig, vertical slit crimson at depth 150, horizontal purple at 250; pinhole at 200. Glide G(μ) = F + μd with d the camera's starting direction; Turn μ = z/(1+rz) with r the welded channel's rate, Reach calibrated to agree with Turn at the first drawn level z = 100 (as in the window-and-floor plate). Judgment calls flagged: off the seam Turn is drawn with the u-channel's rate (no choice welds there, which is the point); the camera's reading is drawn in dark neutral rather than the flat pages' purple, since purple here belongs to the horizontal slit; slit bars are drawn at their true depths up to 560 and annotated beyond; depth compressed 0.55× for display, affine only.
