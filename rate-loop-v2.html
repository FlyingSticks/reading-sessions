Reading the plate

Provenance. This is the unresolved kj sketch, resolved as the ground-level panel of the congruence square from "The law and the gauge." The rectangle carries two channels: the first parts (a, c) and the second parts (b, d). Each channel has its rate — r₁ = c/a, r₂ = d/b — and the congruence square inset plots the pair (r₁, r₂), seam in gold on its diagonal, exactly as the seam page plots (rᵤ, rᵥ).

Two laws share the letters, and the sketch needed them pulled apart. The round-trip law — the seam page's convention "product of factors 1 per channel" — says k·j = 1 within each channel: quote the widths in height-units and the heights in width-units and the loop must close. It is bookkeeping, true everywhere on this plate, and it is why both channel points always sit on the unit hyperbola uv = 1, the product form's unit conic. The common-rate law is the event: a single k with c = ka and d = kb — one rate serving both channels — which holds exactly when P lies on the rectangle's diagonal, since one k means c/a = d/b, which is ad = bc, which is I.43. On the seam the labels ka, kb, jc, jd are legitimate as written; off it they are impossible, and the plate withholds them.

The defect and the hook. Off the seam the disagreement is the determinant in rate denomination: ad − bc = ab·(r₂ − r₁), weights times spread — the same grammar as the seam page's law gap Δ = rᵤrᵥ(rᵥ − rᵤ), each a product of weights with the channel spread. The red hook is its per-height price: the horizontal gap from P to the diagonal at P's own level is (bc − ad)/(a + b), the defect divided by the rectangle's height. Hook zero, seam reached, laws agree.

The weld. On the seam, and only there, the boost (x, y) → (jx, ky) — an isometry of the product form, parameter on the unit hyperbola — carries the rectangle to its transpose: width and height exchange, P = (c, b) lands on (a, d) on the transpose's own diagonal, and every cell area ac, ad, bc, bd is kept. That is glide's freedom exercised the way the seam page describes: the gauge can be turned to the other reading and the two weld into one map. Off the seam the weld chip parks — no common rate; the weld needs the seam — the ground-level echo of "one channel closes, the other cannot."

Upstairs. The square this plate unfolds stands in the round at The rate square, in the round, where each point of the square is a camera rig: the rates are the slits' reciprocal depths (Z = 1/r — the round-trip law wearing optics, each channel's (rate, depth) pair a point of uv = 1), and the gold seam is where the camera's image of a depth line is straight. That seam is larger than this one. Straightness survives a silent channel, so upstairs gilds the zero edges (the pushbrooms) and the orthographic corner along with the diagonal. Down here a silent channel means a degenerate rectangle — c = 0 or d = 0, the boundary the drag clamps against — so the loop's seam is the diagonal alone. The two pages draw different seams because they carry different laws: straight-iff-seam upstairs, common-rate-iff-diagonal on the ground floor. The stair between them lands diagonal to diagonal.

The reciprocal register, one currency across two projects. The seam page's weld map, written in reciprocals, is 1/μ = −1/λ − r: a translation by the rate in the reciprocal register. That is the same normal form as this suite's harmonic bookkeeping — the bounce's 1/g′ = 1/g + 1/c, the hinge's 2/HM = 1/b + 1/c, the tilt's 1/OV_D + 1/OV_D′ = 2/c. The camera's reciprocal graduations are the invariant-count crowding; the weld is the exchange desk between equal-step and crowded readings; and the rate loop kj = 1 is the smallest closed circuit in that currency. The seam page is where the Tetra Engine's law-and-gauge and the book's reach-and-turn quote the same ledger; this plate is the teller's window.

Glossary

channel
A matched pair of parts, one from each edge: channel 1 = (a, c), channel 2 = (b, d); the seam page's u and v.
rate
The width-per-height of a channel: r₁ = c/a, r₂ = d/b. The diagonal's own rate is W/H = (c + d)/(a + b).
round-trip law, k·j = 1
Within a channel, quoting each edge in the other's units must close the loop; the seam page's "product of factors 1 per channel." Always true; a condition on gauges, not on P.
common-rate law (the seam)
One k serving both channels: c = ka and d = kb ⟺ r₁ = r₂ ⟺ ad = bc ⟺ P on the diagonal. The event, not the bookkeeping.
congruence square
The (r₁, r₂) domain with the seam in gold on its diagonal; this rectangle is its ground floor — one point of the square, unfolded.
defect
ad − bc = ab(r₂ − r₁): the determinant as weights times channel spread; kin to the seam page's Δ = rᵤrᵥ(rᵥ − rᵤ).
hook
The horizontal gap from P to the diagonal at P's height: (bc − ad)/(a + b) — the defect per unit of height, drawn in red.
unit hyperbola uv = 1
The product form's unit conic; each channel's rate-pair (k, j) rides it always, and the seam is where the two points fuse.
weld (boost)
The uv-isometry (x, y) → (jx, ky), available on the seam: rectangle to transpose, P to (a, d), all four cell areas kept.
parked
The weld off-seam: no common rate exists, so no gauge turn can close both channels — "the weld needs the seam."
pushbroom edge (upstairs territory)
The rate square's seam includes the zero edges and the orthographic corner, because its law — a straight image — survives one silent channel. This plate's law does not: a silent channel is a degenerate rectangle, so the loop's seam is the diagonal alone, and the edges belong to the floor above.
reciprocal register
The currency of the whole ledger: gauge changes and crowdings as translations of reciprocals — 1/μ = −1/λ − r, 1/g′ = 1/g + 1/c, 2/HM = 1/b + 1/c, 1/OV_D + 1/OV_D′ = 2/c.
