The duality

The cross-slit rig and its dual are one figure seen through a mirror, and the mirror is the means sphere itself. Its polarity — pole and polar with respect to the sphere on the slits as diameter — carries the rig to the dual rig, the glass to the neutral surface, and every imaging ray to a ray of the dual congruence, while holding one surface fixed: the sphere. On that surface sits the station point E, self-conjugate, equal to its own polar — the one place a configuration and its dual coincide, which is why E reads the two-point drawing rather than taking a picture of it. This plate makes the exchange draggable: fold the rig into its dual and watch what moves and what stands still.

fold rig

The profile, folding. The equator is the fixed circle — the polarity's mirror; the GM circle about the glass is the separate inversion of the laws card. Drag the fold: the glass A and the neutral surface C ride a semicircle orthogonal to the equator and trade places; the slits B and D ride the equator itself and swap; the geometric mean and the touch point T stand still; and E, on the equator, is carried along the equator to the summit and back — never leaving the fixed surface, because it is the fixed surface's own point. What the fold performs on the axis is the inversion x ↦ AM + R²/(x − AM); what it performs on the rig is the exchange of the two readings.

What swaps, what stands

What to look for

The duality is an involution: fold all the way to the dual and the same fold carries it back, because the polar of the polar is the pole. Three things never move under it — the geometric mean GM (the tangent length from the glass, fixed by the GM-circle inversion of the laws card), the touch point T where the equator meets the GM circle, and the whole equator as a set, though its points slide along it. Everything else pairs off: glass with neutral surface, slit with slit, arithmetic mean with harmonic mean, and — the closing law read as a single sentence — the product of a point's distance from AM with its conjugate's is R², so AM × HM = GM² is the pole–polar pairing of the glass with the neutral surface, not a coincidence of three means but the duality acting on the axis.

E is the plate's quiet centre. It sits on the equator, so it is its own polar — fold as far as you like and E only travels along the fixed circle, never off it. That is the exact sense in which the station point reads: a reader stands where the drawing and its dual agree, and the only such places are the sphere's own points. The two-point perspective on the horizon is precisely the content that survives the exchange — the self-dual part — which is why the horizon carries a true reading while the volume, split into the dual pair, carries none.

The laws card, the GM mirror, and the dual rig are three faces of one polarity. The means sphere is not a picture of the rig; it is the mirror in which the rig and its dual are the same object, and E is the point on the mirror where looking and being looked at coincide.

Conventions

Judgment calls flagged: the fold is a visual interpolation of each conjugate pair along its orthogonal semicircle, not a physical motion of the rig — the endpoints (fold 0 and 1) are the rig and its dual; the between is a drawing aid. The equator inversion (radius R about AM) is the polarity's trace on the axis; the GM-circle inversion (radius GM about the glass) is the distinct symmetry of the laws card, drawn in green for contrast — the two share the touch point T but no other point. The level set at the neutral surface is a plane and is called one.

Glossary

polarity
A duality equal to its own inverse — pole and polar with respect to a conic or quadric. Here, with respect to the means sphere.
pole–polar
The pairing a polarity makes: each point (pole) is carried to a line or plane (its polar), and back. On the axis it is the inversion in the equator.
self-conjugate
A point on its own polar — equivalently, a point of the sphere. E is self-conjugate; the reader stands on the fixed surface.
fixed surface
The sphere itself: the locus the polarity holds pointwise-as-a-set, where a configuration and its dual coincide.
dual rig
The rig with its two slit directions exchanged, depths kept — the image of the rig under the polarity.
self-dual content
What survives the exchange unchanged: the two-point reading on the horizon, which both rig and dual rig read the same way.
