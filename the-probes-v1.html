Every plate of the thread watches a figure, the probe, to see what a map has done. This side plate states the rule that governs what a probe can and cannot show, and why the thread uses the L.

A figure detects only what moves it

This is the principle the pin sketches were built on, stated outright. A figure can distinguish two congruences only if their difference moves it. (The difference of maps A and B is B⁻¹A; if that fixes every point of the figure, the figure cannot tell A from B.)

The half-turn about a point P is the product of two perpendicular mirrors through P: the mirror in the vertical line, then the mirror in the horizontal line. Their difference is the horizontal mirror. A pin lying on the horizontal line is fixed point by point by that mirror, so on that pin the half-turn and the vertical mirror are the same map; this is the “perpendicular” block of the sketch, where both pictures came out alike. A pin parallel to the vertical line but off the horizontal one does feel the horizontal mirror, as head-up becoming head-down; that is the “parallel” block, where the pictures differed. A single pin is a vector and has no hand; only a figure that spans the plane (the L) detects the sign under every placement.

Glossary for this plate

Terms not listed here are as on the One Generator page. Mathematical terms first, then drawing terms.

probe
A figure used to test a map by looking at its image. A probe distinguishes two maps only if the difference of the maps moves the probe. A single pin is a weak probe (it is a vector, with no hand); an L is a complete probe in the plane.
difference of two maps
For maps A and B, the map B⁻¹A. If it fixes a figure point by point, A and B agree on that figure.
half-turn
Rotation by 180°: the product of two perpendicular mirrors. Even. The in-plane operation drawn with an arc in the sketch.
hand
The property of a figure that a mirror reverses and an even congruence preserves. “Opposite hand” means mirror image.
pin
Drawing term. A segment with a head at one end, drawn as a black stroke with a red disc. It represents an oriented segment: a vector with a position.
head
Drawing term. The red disc marking which end of a pin is which.
L
Drawing term. The figure used as a probe with a hand: a short bar and a long bar meeting at a right angle, unequal so that its mirror image cannot be rotated onto it.
