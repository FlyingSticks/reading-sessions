Beneath the Involution

In geometry the involution is not the first thing. It is built from incidence: joining two points and meeting two lines. This plate follows the construction, and shows why it needs no measure. The general idea of an involution is on the plate before this one, Correspondences and Circuits.

The order of construction

step 1Incidencejoin two points; meet two lines
step 2The harmonic relationthe value −1, reached by joins and meets alone (figure 1)
step 3The involutionthe harmonic relation applied to every point (figure 2)

Audit. Audited outside on 19 November 2026 and amended. The audit found that the plate credited incidence alone with the midpoint, when the point at infinity has to be given first; that "nothing cancels and a measurement is left over" was wrong, since the unit still cancels and what is left is a plain number; and that the construction needed its conditions stated (three different points, T other than S and P, parallels meeting at infinity). It confirmed the attribution to Desargues' theorem and all the arithmetic.

Terms

The plain words are the plate's own. The standard name, for looking things up elsewhere, follows each in grey.

incidence
The relation "this point lies on that line". Its two operations are join (the line through two points) and meet (the point on two lines). Standard: incidence; join and meet.
harmonic partner
The point P′ reached from A, B and P by the construction of figure 1. Standard: the harmonic conjugate of P with respect to A and B. The four points form a harmonic range.
complete quadrangle
Four points, no three on one line, with the six lines joining them in pairs. In figure 1: S, T, U, V. Standard: complete quadrangle.
stays
Said of a point that is its own partner. Standard: a fixed point.
stretch XY
The length from X to Y along the line, counted plus in the chosen sense and minus against it. Reversing the chosen sense changes every sign and no ratio. Standard: a directed, or signed, length.
divides A B in the ratio
Said of a point X on the line: the ratio AX : XB, plus when X is between A and B, minus when X is outside. Standard: the division ratio.
the −1
The first division ratio divided by the second, with the four points taken in the order A, B, P, P′. Standard: the cross-ratio (A, B; P, P′), which always equals (P, P′; A, B). −1 is its harmonic value; for four points in general it takes other values, as in the third row of figure 3.
picture line
The line onto which the four points are projected from the eye in figure 4. Standard: the image line under a central projection. The projection itself is called a perspectivity.
involution
A reversible correspondence, other than the identity, whose backward rule is its forward rule; see the previous plate. Standard: involution.
