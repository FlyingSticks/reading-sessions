The three means, in the round

Two slits at depths Zv and Zh. Put a sphere on them as diameter and reach for it from the glass. The tangent length is the geometric mean, the centre is the arithmetic mean, and the circle where the tangents touch stands at the harmonic mean — the neutral surface. Drag to orbit, drag the marks along the axis, slide the probe and watch the same rig thread a ray and bow a line.

What to look for

The sphere is drawn true: no depth compression here, so its outline is a circle from every angle, profile included. Your assumption about the interior circles is right, and it splits into two families. The transverse rings — the latitudes, and with them the gold circle of tangency — show as true circles only in the front view, open into ellipses as you orbit, and close to line segments in profile. The meridians do the opposite: in profile the one facing you is a true circle and the rest are ellipses, while in the front view every meridian collapses to a diameter and the sphere reads as a wheel of spokes. Only the silhouette is a circle in all views, which is the orthographic projection's one gift.

Front view is where the tangency reads best. The tangent cone collapses onto the gold ring, every tangent foreshortened to a radius, and you are looking straight down the axis at the neutral surface as a surface — flat, transverse, standing at z*. Profile view returns the flat figure exactly: circle, tangent, altitude, three marks on the axis.

Drag either slit and the three marks separate; bring them together and they collapse onto the pinhole, where one depth serves for all three. Drag the glass and the means follow, because they are means of the distances a and b from the glass, not of the absolute depths. Push the glass inside the sphere and the tangent has nowhere to land: the power of the point goes negative, GM leaves the reals, and the page stops drawing rather than fake it.

The rate square, alongside

The inset is the rate square from the companion page, live. The rig is the point (ru, rv) = (1/Zv, 1/Zh); the gold seam is the diagonal and the two edges. What the sphere adds is three marked pinholes on that diagonal — the rigs whose single depth is the harmonic, geometric and arithmetic mean of the rig's two — and they turn out to sit at three different constructions.

The harmonic mean is the perpendicular foot. Dropping (ru, rv) onto the diagonal gives ((ru+rv)/2, (ru+rv)/2), whose depth is 2/(ru+rv) = HM. The arithmetic mean is a perpendicular foot too, but in the depth square rather than the rate square — in rate coordinates it arrives as a harmonic mean instead, marked by no perpendicular at all. The geometric mean is the hyperbolic foot, where rurv = constant meets the diagonal, and it is the only one of the three that is self-dual: √(rurv) is the rate of the depth √(ZvZh), so it reads the same in both squares. Reciprocation swaps AM and HM and fixes GM, which is why the sphere's three marks and the square's three marks are the same three facts in opposite order.

The threaded ray and its trail are the companion page's objects, put in the same scene. The probe's ray is the unique line meeting both slits; where it pierces the glass is the mark; and the dark curve on the glass is the trail of marks left by a depth line, bowed in general and straight only on the seam. Snap to pinhole and the bow goes out of it while the three means fuse — one event, seen twice.

Conventions

Chapter 2 depths; the book rig is crimson vertical at 150, purple horizontal at 250, as on the rate square. Sphere centre c = (Zv+Zh)/2, radius R = |Zh−Zv|/2. With a = Zv−O and b = Zh−O, the power of the glass is (c−O)² − R² = ab, so the tangent length is √(ab) = GM, and the polar plane of O meets the axis at O + 2ab/(a+b) = O + HM. Judgment calls flagged: depth is drawn at true scale here, unlike the rate square's 0.55× compression, because a compressed sphere reads as a lens and the sphere is the whole argument — the cost is a longer scene, absorbed by auto-fit. Light runs from the probe through the slits to the glass, so the scene sits beyond both slits and the mark is inverted; the channel scale is (O−Z)/(z−Z), which is the companion page's 1/(1+rz) with depth measured from the glass outward rather than inward. The sphere is a wireframe of transverse rings and meridians rather than a shaded ball, since shading would hide the tangency it exists to show. The probe rides a single depth line at a fixed transverse offset, so the trail is that one line's image and not a family.

Checks

The page re-runs a live micro-check on every interaction: that the tangent from O is perpendicular to the radius at the touch point, that its foot lands on the harmonic mean, that the drawn touch point satisfies the polar relation (X−c)·(O−c) = R², that AM × HM − GM² closes, and that the threaded ray built only from slit incidence lands on the closed-form mark. A sixth line checks a claim this page makes and the flat figure could not: the arithmetic mean of the two channels' reciprocal scales equals the reciprocal scale of a pinhole at the neutral surface, at every probe depth and for every rig. All are computed from the drawn geometry rather than from the closed form they confirm.
