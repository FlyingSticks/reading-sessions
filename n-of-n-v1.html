Reading the sheet

Givens: a horizontal line carrying marks 0 … n, and one point O placed below mark 0. Joining O to each mark makes the pencil — the reach of the page. The join O–0 is ray 0, and it doubles as the left edge. Through mark 1 runs the one parallel the construction starts with: a line parallel to ray 0. It meets the last ray at height 1/n — not by choice but by similar triangles — and the horizontal drawn there, the free horizontal, is a copy of the whole line at 1/n scale. The pencil that made the coarse marks already cuts this copy into n equal parts: rays 1 … n−1 pass through it at exactly its own division points. Nothing new is purchased.

Only two maps ever act, and each is honest about intervals. Projection from O between two parallel horizontals is a pure scaling — it multiplies every length by the ratio of heights and moves nothing sideways. Parallel transfer between the same two horizontals is a pure translation — it slides every mark by the same amount and stretches nothing. Reach scales; turn shifts. The multiplicative and the additive register meet on one page, each doing the one thing it is licensed to do.

The turn families do the addressing. Family T₁ transfers the free horizontal's marks straight up (B = 0) into the first coarse cell; T₂ carries the same marks one cell over; T₃ two cells; the ratio B : A names the direction. A fine mark is therefore a pair (turn, reach): x = turn + reach/n. Nine marks from two three-valued digits — n of n = n² is a two-digit numeral in base n, drawn instead of written.

Adjacent families share their boundary marks: reach n of one family lands exactly where reach 0 of the next begins — two transfer lines meeting at one point. That meeting is the carry of positional arithmetic, performed by incidence.

The turn directions are not imported. T₂ is the join of the free horizontal's zero to coarse mark 1; T₃ the join to mark 2; T₁ is ray 0's own direction. And the machine recurses before being asked: the pencil already cuts the horizontal at height 1/n² into n parts (switch on next level), so a third round of parallels would deliver n³. Each level of the triangle is a digit place; the figure is a numeral system with a vanishing point.

Glossary

gauge n
The count chosen once; everything else follows from it.
pencil
The joins from O to the coarse marks; the page's reach.
ray 0
The join O–0; also the left edge, also the direction of turn family T₁.
free horizontal
The horizontal at height 1/n; a 1/n-scale copy of the marked line, cut into n parts by the pencil at no extra cost.
reach
Equal-interval count along a line; here, position within a cell. Scaling's register.
turn
Choice of direction, named by a ratio; here, choice of cell. Translation's register.
turn family Tk
The parallels of one direction, carrying the free horizontal's marks into coarse cell k.
B : A
Run per rise of a turn family: B₁ = 0, and in general B : A = n(k−1) : (n−1). B is counted in the line's units and A in the height's units, so the drawing may stretch its axes independently — every parallel stays parallel.
coarse cell, fine mark
The n parts made by the pencil; the n² parts made by pencil and parallels together.
address
The pair (turn, reach) with x = turn + reach/n; a two-digit base-n numeral.
carry
The shared boundary mark where reach n of one family coincides with reach 0 of the next.
next level
The horizontal at 1/n², already cut by the pencil; the standing offer of n³.
