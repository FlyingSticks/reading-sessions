The defect, in the round

The count out in the world, the account in crimson, and the placement morph — the flat page's bottom strips, stood up in the scene they were strips of. Drag to orbit; stop the count; slide the placement.

The crimson is the account: everything past the count, fixed in the world before any ruler is chosen. The ruler only chooses its address on the glass. Slide the placement to Reach and the account is piled past the open end, no mark anywhere. Slide it to Turn and infinity is seated at the crimson ring, the payment graded into every crowding interval. The numbers ride the whole way unchanged — the count survives; only the placement moves.

What to look for

The green run of the depth line is the counted part of the world — beads at whole steps, numbered. The crimson beyond the last bead is the account: the rest of infinity, the part every closure test fails on. Move the count stop and the boundary moves; nothing a ruler does can shrink what lies past it.

On the glass, the teal ticks carry the same numbers. At the Reach end their steps are equal and the line stays open — always one more step available, the end never an object — so the crimson runs off along the dashed extension to no address at all: the account piled at the unreachable end, the wolf at the end of the line. Slide toward Turn and watch the numbers crowd without ever exchanging places, until at pure Turn infinity snaps to a finite seat: the crimson ring closes around the gold diamond, and the account becomes a visible sliver from the last tick to the ring — shrinking at every stop of the count, nonzero at every stop. That snap happens only at the very end of the slider: any blend short of pure Turn still leaves infinity unmarked, which is Theorem 0 watched along a whole family of rulers, not just its two endpoints.

The dark dots never move. They are the camera's marks at the counted depths — welded to the graded placement by the optics, holding the Turn positions while the teal ticks travel to meet them. The camera runs no slider: it is the one party in the scene with no policy of its own. Turn the sight lines on to see the fan that welds it.
