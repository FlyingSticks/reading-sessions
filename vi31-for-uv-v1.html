Reading the plate

What was owed. The lower identity had been priced as a reading: it behaves like a Pythagorean theorem for the product form Q(x, y) = xy, whose null directions are the rectangle's own edges. The stated redemption terms: draw the form's conic over the rectangle, produce a Thales-analogue certifying a right angle, and derive the identity as Euclid VI.31 — similar figures on the three sides of a right triangle, areas adding as the squares do. This plate pays.

The Minkowski circle. Fix the segment O–B, the hypotenuse whose Q-square is Q(OB) = b·1 = b. The locus of points E with (E−O) Q-orthogonal to (E−B) — the Thales circle of the form — is the rectangular hyperbola 2xy − x − by = 0: centered at the midpoint of O–B, asymptotes along the null directions, passing through O and B. It crosses the rectangle's right edge at exactly one point, P₂ = (2, 2/(4−b)) — and that height, 2/(4−b), is the hinge height already on the books. By hyperbola-Thales, the angle O–P₂–B is a Q-right angle. The certificate can also be read optically: Q-orthogonality for the product form means equal and opposite slopes, ±1/(4−b) here, so P₂ is the point on the right edge at which a ray from O reflects off a horizontal null mirror and arrives at B.

Signed Pythagoras. For any symmetric form, Q(OB) = Q(OP₂) + Q(P₂B) + 2⟨OP₂, P₂B⟩, so a Q-right angle at P₂ gives Q(OP₂) + Q(P₂B) = Q(OB) — identically in b, as the caption's ledger shows. But signature (1,1) adds its signature clause: the two legs at a Thales apex always have opposite causal type. Here Q(OP₂) = 2t > 0 while Q(P₂B) = (b−2)(1−t) < 0. The form does not sum two positives; it trades a positive against a negative. Euclid's circle never shows this because x² + y² has no negatives to hide.

Squares into triangles. The square on a segment, in this geometry, is the axis-aligned rectangle having the segment as diagonal — signed area Q — and its half-square is the triangle cut by that diagonal. All axis rectangles are Q-similar (diagonal maps scale Q), the sign-reversing ones included, exactly as Euclid admits reflections. Now read the page: the half-square on O–B is the tinted triangle OAB, vertex for vertex. The half-square on O–P₂ is the triangle O–P₂–2; the half-square on P₂–B is the triangle B–P₂–D — its rectangle owns the corner D outright. One slide finishes it: the apex P₂ rides the null rail y = 2/(4−b) to the hinge P, and by I.37 — equal bases, apexes on one parallel — both half-squares keep their areas while becoming the tinted OP2 and BPD. Then the negative square crosses the equals sign: ½Q(OB) = ½Q(OP₂) + ½Q(P₂B) rearranges, unsigned, to OAB + BPD = OP2. That is VI.31 for uv, drawn.

The hinge, re-identified. P was met twice before: as the meet of B–2 with O–D, and as the trapezoid diagonal crossing at height 2/(4−b) with its harmonic chord. It is now met a third time, from inside the form: P is the null corner between the apex and its altitude — P₂ to P runs horizontal, P to the Q-altitude foot F on O–B runs vertical, and Euclid's lemma holds in Q throughout: Q(OP₂) = Q(OB)·(foot fraction), and Q(P₂F) = Q(OP₂)·Q(P₂B)/Q(OB), the form's geometric-mean relation, negative because the altitude has the opposite type of the hypotenuse — the same office the altitude B′B holds upstairs, where the circle makes it √(AB·BD).

Settlement. Every load-bearing word of the naming now has a theorem behind it on this one page: the conic, its Thales, the right angle, the squares, the similar figures, the lemma, the mean. The word "Pythagorean" is redeemed; the claim is priced as theorem, and the two certificates hang over one rectangle — the circle for x² + y², where all squares agree in sign and the theorem sums, and the hyperbola for xy, where the legs must disagree and the theorem trades. One congruence, two quadratic banks; the page clears in both.

Glossary

product form
Q(x, y) = xy on the rectangle's coordinates; rotated 45° it is (x² − y²)/2, the Minkowski plane's form in null coordinates.
null direction
A direction of Q-length zero: here horizontal and vertical, the rectangle's own edges; the form's degenerate conic Q = 0 is this line pair.
Q-orthogonal
⟨u, v⟩ = (u₁v₂ + u₂v₁)/2 = 0; for the product form, equal and opposite slopes — the mirror law off a null direction.
Minkowski circle
The Thales locus of a segment: all E with (E−O) ⊥ (E−B). A rectangular hyperbola with null asymptotes, centered at the segment's midpoint.
hyperbola-Thales
E on the Minkowski circle of diameter O–B ⟺ the angle O–E–B is Q-right; the circle-Thales sentence with the conic exchanged.
apex P₂
The Minkowski circle's unique crossing of the right edge, (2, 2/(4−b)); the certified Q-right angle of the plate.
signed square
The axis rectangle on a segment as diagonal, area Q with sign; its half-square is the triangle the diagonal cuts.
negative square
A square of Q < 0 (segment of the other causal type); rendered crimson; it crosses the equals sign to appear as a positive area.
Q-similar
Related by a diagonal map (λx, μy), which scales Q by λμ; includes sign-reversing anti-similarities, as Euclidean similarity includes reflections.
null shear
Sliding an apex along a rail parallel to a null base — I.37; area-preserving, and the move that carries both half-squares onto the tinted triangles.
hinge P
Meet of B–2 and O–D; trapezoid diagonal crossing; and now the null corner between apex P₂ and altitude foot F — three offices, one point.
altitude foot F
The Q-perpendicular from P₂ onto O–B; foot fraction Q(OP₂)/Q(OB) along the hypotenuse — Euclid's lemma in Q.
Q-geometric mean
Q(P₂F) = Q(OP₂)·Q(P₂B)/Q(OB), the altitude relation; upstairs the same office is B′B = √(AB·BD).
settlement
The conversion of a priced reading into theorem by exhibiting its redemption terms; what this plate performs for the word "Pythagorean."
