The station-point locus of the two-slit horizon

Companion plates: The three means, here · The GM mirror · The three means, in the round. Every displayed equality below is asserted in the two check scripts; the map from propositions to assertions closes the addendum.

The anatomy of the two-slit camera and the classical setup of a two-point perspective are the same figure. The means sphere's equator is the draftsman's station-point locus; the glass and the neutral surface are the diagonal points of the square; and the whole table of laws is the orbit of one inversion.

0 · Setting and names

Glass (image plane) at O; vertical slit at depth Zv, horizontal at Zh; distances from the glass a = Zv − O, b = Zh − O, with the glass outside the slab (ab > 0). Means of a and b: AM = (a+b)/2, GM = √(ab), HM = 2ab/(a+b); means sphere on the slits as diameter, centre at AM, radius R = |b − a|/2; its equator is the profile circle on BD. The range on the axis: A the glass, B and D the slits, C the harmonic-mean point z* — in the profile, the foot of the tangent's touch point T. All statements are made in the profile plane and read on the axis as a horizon; the three-dimensional claim is deliberately not made (Remark 3).

1 · Propositions

P1 (Harmonic horizon). (B, D; C, A) = −1: the harmonic-mean point is the fourth harmonic of the glass across the two slits.
The power of the glass with respect to the equator is ab, so the tangent length is GM and the polar of O meets the axis at O + 2ab/(a+b) = O + HM. The polar construction is the harmonic-conjugate construction; the cross-ratio computes to −1 directly. ∎
P2 (Parallelogram licence). Read the axis as a horizon. A quadrilateral whose sides vanish to B and D and whose diagonals vanish to C and A exists through any starting vertex, and its fourth ray lands unaided; conversely, the side and diagonal vanishing points of any parallelogram image form a harmonic range.
Directions u, v, u+v, u−v have cross-ratio (0, ∞; 1, −1) = −1, so a parallelogram's four vanishing points are harmonic. Given the harmonic range, construct sides toward B, D and one diagonal toward C from any vertex; the second diagonal meets the axis at the harmonic conjugate of C across B, D, which is A by P1 and uniqueness of the conjugate. ∎
P3 (Station-point locus). The eyes from which the two slits subtend a right angle fill the circle on BD as diameter — and this is the means sphere's equator. The classical 90° arc over the two vanishing points, by which the draftsman sites the station point of a two-point perspective, is therefore drawn by the rig itself.
Thales on the diameter BD; the equator is that circle by the sphere's definition. ∎
P4 (The square and the sphere). The eyes from which A and C also subtend a right angle fill the circle on AC; the two circles are orthogonal, hence meet, at E above the horizon and its reflection below; from E the quadrilateral of P2 is the image of a square. The eye that squares the range stands on the sphere. The diagonal points of that square are the glass and the virtual centre.
A range of four collinear points is harmonic exactly when the circles on its two separating pairs as diameters are orthogonal; orthogonal circles with real radii meet. At a crossing, sides subtend 90° (P3's circle) and diagonals subtend 90° (the AC circle): perpendicular sides make the parallelogram a rectangle, perpendicular diagonals make it a rhombus, together a square. ∎
P5 (Means as feet on the horizon). From the glass: the centre of the equator stands at AM, the tangent has length GM, and the tangency's foot stands at HM, with AM × HM = GM². The classical mean-proportional semicircle, drawn over the two vanishing points, therefore constructs the neutral surface on the horizon: the camera's zero-disparity depth is a ruler-and-compass point of the perspective setup.
Power of the point for GM; the polar relation (T − M)·(O − M) = R² projects to the foot at HM; the closure is HM · AM = (2ab/(a+b))·((a+b)/2) = ab = GM². ∎
P6 (The GM mirror). Inversion ι in the circle of radius GM about the glass — x · x′ = ab — swaps B ↔ D and AM ↔ HM, trades the glass with the ideal point, and fixes GM and T. It preserves cross-ratio; it carries the equator to itself (the orthogonality AM² − GM² = R²), with the equator crossing the mirror at T; it carries the eye's circle on AC to the transverse plane through the centre, sending E to the equator's summit. Every conjugate pair (x, ab/x) rides a circle orthogonal to the mirror, the pair (B, D) riding the equator itself. Reciprocation on the rate square is ι in the rig's own unit: the means of (1/a, 1/b) are the reciprocals of the swapped means of (a, b), and ι is the reciprocal map rescaled by the power ab.
Each swap and fixity is one line of x · x′ = ab; ι is a Möbius involution of the axis, hence preserves cross-ratio. A circle is orthogonal to the mirror iff the power of O with respect to it equals GM², which for the circle on [x, ab/x] as diameter is ((x+x′)/2 − O)² − ((x′−x)/2)² = x·x′ = ab; for the equator the same computation is AM² − R² = ab. A circle through the centre of inversion maps to a line, and the antipode C of O on the AC circle maps to O + AM, fixing the line as the transverse plane there; E, on both circles, maps to a point of the (invariant) equator on that plane — its summit. ∎
P7 (Degeneration at the pinhole). As the slits fuse at P, blow up the axis by the half-gap: the range (B, D; C, A) tends to (−1, +1; 0, ∞) — the conjugate of infinity is the midpoint — and the two-point square degenerates to a one-point parallelogram with a diagonal parallel to the horizon. The station-point circle dies into the pinhole; the eye's circle survives as the circle on glass–pinhole; Thales keeps the right angle at F, the foot of the perpendicular from the glass onto the collapsed pencil's ray. The slits fall onto the mirror and stop trading.
C − P is second order in the half-gap while B − P and D − P are first order, giving the rescaled range; the quadrilateral's construction lines all pass to the pencil at P, its diagonal's meeting point halting at a parameter fixed by the free start (a gauge, not a fact). Any point of the surviving circle sees its diameter A P at 90°; the crossing of the collapsed ray with that circle is the perpendicular foot. At a = b the slits lie at distance GM = a from the glass, on the mirror. ∎

2 · Remarks

R1 (One symmetry). The laws card of the means plate is the orbit of P6: the closing law AM × HM = GM² is x · x′ = GM² evaluated at one pair, the orthogonality is the same power computed at the equator, and the rate square's duality is the same map with the unit renormalized. What the plates present as seven equalities the mirror presents as one membership: being a conjugate pair.

R2 (Context and standing). The individual ingredients are classical — power of a point, polars, harmonic conjugates, orthogonal circles, the mean-proportional semicircle — and the 90° arc over the two vanishing points is standard studio method for siting the station point. What appears to be new is the identification: the same circle doing camera duty (neutral surface, tangency, channel averaging) and drawing-board duty (station-point locus, diagonal transfer). A literature pass is pending; no priority claim is asserted here.

R3 (Scope). Everything above is the horizon reading of the profile. No claim is made that the three-dimensional cross-slit image seen from E is itself a two-point perspective — cross-slit images are not perspectives in general — and that question is left as the open item, to receive its own check script before any prose.

R4 (A caution on names, for the reader). The glass and the virtual centre are the diagonal points of the square of P4 — the vanishing points of its diagonals, which suffice to transfer equal depth modules along the horizon, and that is how they are named and used throughout. A reader trained on the two-point method may reach for measuring points; that is a different construction, lying at the eye-distance from each vanishing point, and the two figures do not coincide: |BA| ≠ |BE| already at the sketch rig (150 vs. ≈ 79.8; the scope guard below asserts the inequality).

R5 (Judgment calls flagged). The addendum number is left open for the register. "Diagonal points" is used in preference to "distance points" throughout, per R4. The blow-up in P7 is stated for the symmetric approach B, D → P at equal rates; an asymmetric approach rescales the limit range without changing its harmonicity.
