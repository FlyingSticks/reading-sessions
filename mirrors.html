Every congruence of the plane is a product of mirrors. The sign of a congruence is the count of its mirrors, mod 2. The measure of a congruence is the separation of a pair of mirrors. A single mirror has sign and no measure.

The pin sketches asked a question the textbook does not start from: what can a figure tell about the map that was done to it? This page keeps that question and puts the standard structure under it. Three steps, each with a diagram you can operate, then the complete list of plane congruences, then a glossary. All terms used here are defined in the glossary; a drawing word (mirror line, L) is never used for a mathematical object, and a mathematical word (mirror, half-turn, sign) is never used for a drawing.

1. One generator: the mirror Cartan–Dieudonné

A mirror is a map of the plane determined by one line m, its mirror line: every point of m stays where it is, and every other point P goes to the one point P′ for which m is the perpendicular bisector of PP′ — the same distance away on the far side, along the perpendicular. Nothing travels; the map is the finished pairing of each point with its partner, and doing it twice gives every point back. The textbooks call this map “reflection in m” (British usage) or “reflection across m” (American). The preposition is an idiom naming the line that does the reflecting, as in “your image in the mirror”; it does not describe a direction. On this page the map is called the mirror with line m, or as a shorter name, the mirror in m. The theorem of Cartan and Dieudonné says that every congruence of n-dimensional space is a product of at most n mirrors; in the plane, at most two — three if translations are included in the count, as they are below. So the list of binaries on the sketch (plus/minus, odd/even, left/right versus front/back) is replaced by one object. Everything else on this page is built from it.

2. Sign = count of mirrors, mod 2

Apply mirrors one after another and count them. An even count returns the figure to its own hand; an odd count gives the opposite hand. The parity does not depend on which mirrors were used, only on how many: it is the one invariant every congruence carries without ambiguity. Plus/minus (the determinant, +1 or −1) and odd/even are the same fact in two notations.

Which mirror is not an invariant. Any odd congruence is any chosen mirror followed by some even congruence; the choice of mirror is free, the parity is not. This is the whole of the left/right versus front/back puzzle: a mirror reverses the axis normal to it (front/back). We say “left/right” because we silently compose with a half-turn about the vertical to line ourselves up with the image. The named axis is a choice of even companion; the sign is intrinsic.

3. Measure = separation of a pair of mirrors

Two mirrors give an even congruence, and the even congruence carries a number. Two mirror lines crossing at angle θ compose to a rotation by 2θ about their crossing point. Two parallel mirror lines at distance d compose to a translation by 2d, perpendicular to them. Perpendicular mirror lines (θ = 90°) give the half-turn, the case in the pin sketches.

So measure sits on the even side and comes from the pair; sign comes from the count. A single mirror has a sign and no measure: there is no half a mirror in the plane. The continuous arc in the sketch belongs to the rotation, that is, to the pair.

The complete list of plane congruences

With translation (two parallel mirrors) and the glide reflection (three mirrors: a mirror followed by a translation along its own line) added to the sketch, the list closes. Every congruence of the plane is one of these five. Later plates take the rows one at a time: the pair (crossing lines), the parallel pair, the glide; the third-mirror plate shows exactly when three mirrors make a glide and when they make a single mirror.

Glossary

Mathematical terms first, then drawing terms, then terms retired from the sketch. The terms of the three side plates (fold, trace, chiral, probe, side, frame and the drawing terms that go with them) are in those plates' glossaries.

congruence
A map of the plane (or of space) to itself that preserves every distance. Also called an isometry. Every congruence is a product of mirrors.
mirror
The map of the plane determined by a line m: each point of m stays; each other point P goes to the point P′ for which m is the perpendicular bisector of PP′. In space, the same with a plane in place of the line. Called “reflection in m” or “reflection across m” in the textbooks; here, “the mirror with line m” or, as a name, “the mirror in m”. Its fixed points are exactly m; applied twice it is the identity (an involution); it preserves every distance. The single generator on this page.
“in”, as in mirror in m
A name, not a direction. Inherited from everyday speech about mirrors (“the image in the glass”), where the preposition points at the thing that reflects. Nothing goes into the line; each point goes across it to its partner. “Through” is kept for the push of a probe (a drawing word); “about” is kept for turns.
sign
The parity of the number of mirrors in a congruence: even or odd. Equivalently the determinant, +1 or −1. Invariant: the same for every way of writing the congruence as a product of mirrors.
even / odd
A congruence is even if it is a product of an even number of mirrors (it preserves hand), odd if of an odd number (it reverses hand).
measure
The number carried by an even congruence: the angle of a rotation, the distance of a translation. It is twice the separation of the pair of mirrors that compose to it. A single mirror has no measure.
rotation
Product of two mirrors in lines crossing at angle θ: a turn by 2θ about the crossing point. Even.
half-turn
Rotation by 180°: the product of two perpendicular mirrors. Even. The in-plane operation drawn with an arc in the sketch.
translation
Product of two mirrors in parallel lines at distance d: a shift by 2d perpendicular to the lines. Even.
glide reflection
A mirror followed by a translation along the mirror's own line. Three mirrors; odd; no fixed points.
hand
The property of a figure that a mirror reverses and an even congruence preserves. “Opposite hand” means mirror image.
fixed point
A point sent to itself by a map. The set of fixed points classifies plane congruences: every point (identity), a line (mirror), one point (rotation), none (translation, glide).
Cartan–Dieudonné theorem
Every congruence of n-dimensional space is a product of at most n mirrors (at most n + 1 when translations are counted, as on this page). The floor under everything here.
L
Drawing term. The figure used as a probe with a hand: a short bar and a long bar meeting at a right angle, unequal so that its mirror image cannot be rotated onto it.
mirror line
Drawing term. The drawn line of a mirror; the line of its fixed points.
flip (retired)
Used in the sketch both for the in-plane half-turn and for the out-of-plane fold, which are different maps. Replaced by half-turn and fold (the fold is on side plate A).
parallel / perpendicular (re-assigned)
In the sketch these labeled the pin's orientation relative to the axis. They are now used only for the arrangement of a pair of mirror lines (Step 3), which is what they were tracking.
helicity (removed)
A physics term, frame-dependent there, with no clean analog in the plane. The notion wanted was chirality.
left/right, front/back (absorbed)
Not primary binaries; a corollary of Step 2. The mirror reverses front/back; “left/right” names the axis after a chosen even companion is composed in.
