Minkowski product as a hyperbolic compass construction

Replace i with j, where j² = +1 and j·(x, y) = (y, x), the reflection in the line y = x. Multiplying by w = (a, b) carries the frame {1, j} to {w, j·w}. The coordinates (c, d) of z are then counted along the new frame: swing c from the x-axis along the hyperbola x² − y² = c² onto the line Ow, swing d from the y-axis along y² − x² = d² onto the line O(j·w), and close the figure. Its far corner is w·z.

The frame vectors w and j·w are no longer perpendicular; they are mirror images in the null line y = x, which is Minkowski-orthogonality. The closed figure is still a rectangle in that geometry, and its far corner is still the product. Intervals multiply and rapidities add, as lengths multiplied and angles added in the Euclidean case. The swap j·z = (y, x) is also i·z̄: conjugation followed by a quarter turn. That is where the two absolutes touch.
