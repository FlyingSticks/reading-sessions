Discharged The one-rate-zero edge

Session 2 left the pattern page's one-rate-zero regime deliberately described, not claimed — "a hybrid camera, contraction on a single axis" — pending a check against Chapter 3. Checked: the edge is on the seam. Δ = rurv(rv − ru) vanishes when either rate is zero; the mixed rulings go straight there (verified directly, alongside the control that they bow the moment the second rate wakes); and §5's gloss holds to machine precision — the zeroed channel's readout parks at its picture-plane value along the entire ruling while the live channel keeps working. Silence, not collision. The pattern page's guarded wording was never wrong, only cautious; whether its readout is now upgraded to say "seam" outright is the reader's call — that is a revision, not a repair, and the record does not make it for him. The theorem's own remaining edge business (§6's limit argument for self-duality) is the book's open item, distinct from this one, and untouched.

Discharged The crossing is not the seam — confirmed, with the reason

Session 3 left the miniature's green crossing unaudited against Chapter 3, and the page stayed in the vocabulary of "gauges agreeing" rather than claiming the seam. Correct instinct, now with its ground: the two are different kinds of object. The seam is a locus in rate space — a condition on the rig, blind to any drawing gauge; the crossing is a depth, at a fixed rig, and the drawing unit drags it up and down the line (verified: the unit moves the crossing while Δ cannot see the unit at all). No wording upgrade is available for the miniature's crossing, ever — the page's caution was not provisional but permanent, and it can now say why.

The surprise The miniature's address

Asking where the miniature lives in the theorem's terms returned three nested facts. First: the toy's single depth line is an axis ruling, and by Remark 2.1 the axis rulings fuse at every rig, seam or no seam — so a one-channel construction cannot exhibit the split at all; the split lives in the mixed rulings, where both channels act at once. Second: even the two-channel isotropic version of the toy is pinned to the seam — ru = rv fuses at every rate. Leaving the seam requires two unequal, nonzero rates; the split is not merely perspectival but anisotropic. Third, the address itself: the isometric pair is parallel projection, ru = rv = 0 — the orthographic corner, the one point where the seam's three lines concur and the cubic vanishes to order three. And at that corner both channels are silent, magnification identically 1 at every depth — which is why the 1979 defect is constant. Session 3 observed the constant defect; this session derives it from the address. The first book's territory was the corner: the deepest point of the seam, where the quarrel cannot even be posed, which is exactly the condition under which a two-column classification by parentage is complete and sufficient. The premise of 1979 was not naive about the split; it lived where the split is identically zero.

Seasoning, sharpened The building witness gains its address

The shelf still holds three items — datum-both-ways, seam-as-zero-defect, the building witness — but the third leaves this session sharper than it arrived: the first book lived at the orthographic corner, and the present book is what the view looks like when you step off it. One sentence, now with a checked derivation behind each clause. It continues to season; its candidate seats (porch building line, Chapter 1) are unchanged.
