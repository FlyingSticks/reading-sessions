Three planes, three traces, one sheet

A redrawing of the 3D sketch of 8 Nov — colour by trace, not by plane

Three mutually perpendicular planes H, V, P meet in three lines, the traces, which all pass through one corner point. To make the Monge sheet, H is turned about a and P is turned about c, each through a quarter-turn, until both lie in the plane of V. Slide the angle and watch the third trace b, which belongs to both moving planes, split into two drawn copies.

Definitions

projection planes H, V, P
Three mutually perpendicular planes in space: H horizontal, V vertical (the one that stays put), P the profile plane, perpendicular to both.
trace
The line in which two of the planes meet. There are three: a = H∩V, b = H∩P, c = V∩P. Each is a line in space, and each is perpendicular to the other two.
corner point
The single point common to H, V and P; equivalently the point where the three traces meet.
quarter-turn
Rotation of a plane through 90° about one of its traces, used to lay H and P into the plane of V. H turns about a; P turns about c. Not a fold in the sense of the mirrors page, which is a half-turn.
copy of a trace drawing word
Where a trace lands on the flat sheet. a and c are hinges, so they do not move and have one copy each. b lies in both turning planes and is carried by each, so it has two copies: along the vertical line above the corner (carried by H) and along the horizontal line to the right of the corner (carried by P).
45° line drawing word
The line through the corner bisecting the angle between the two copies of b. It carries each point of one copy to the same point of the other, which is how a depth read in the plan is transferred to the profile.
field drawing word
The region of the flat sheet occupied by one view: plan above a, elevation below it, profile to the right of c. Fields carry no signs.
image of a point drawing word
The foot of the perpendicular from the point to a projection plane, carried along when the plane is turned. Q has three: on H (plan image), on V (elevation image), on P (profile image).
ground line not used
Monge's ligne de terre: the trace H∩V in first-angle projection, where the object stands on H and the line is literally the ground, with the elevation drawn above it. These plates are plan-over-elevation (third-angle), in which H lies above the object; there the word misleads, so the line is named by its trace, a.

This plate settles the trace question and nothing more. Everything on it is at 90°, which is why it has eight maps and no measure; the continuous part of the subject begins when two mirrors meet at some other angle.
