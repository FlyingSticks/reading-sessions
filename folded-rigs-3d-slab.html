One slab, two constructions

  
Take the slab: depth 0 to 1, section the centered square of half-width q. Print it on the
  picture plane under each reading and one figure appears twice. Every depth λ prints as a frame
  — camera-wise at ×(m_u, m_v), glide-wise at the
  reciprocal — and the slab's four corner edges, which are depth lines, print as the
  connectors between frames. The whole of it is drawn on the plane; the ink wireframe behind is
  the slab itself, for reference.


  
Mediated by the red rectangle

  
At λ = 0 both gauges are ×1, so both families begin at the same rectangle — which
  is also the slab's own near face, sitting on the plane. That triple identity is the hinge of
  the figure: the red rectangle is object and image at once, and the two
  constructions attach to it from opposite sides, sharing it as their first frame. Everything
  the two readings disagree about, they disagree about starting from this common ground.


  
Opposite sides of the picture plane

  
The cross-slit projection is the view into the slab: frames
  graduated harmonically, crowding as depth grows, closed by the bold far frame — a room seen
  from its doorway. Its corner connectors bow, because a two-slit camera bends depth lines into
  hyperbolic arcs. The glide construction is the same slab represented
  on the viewer's side: frames in equal strides outward, corner lines dead straight, and no
  closing — past its λ = 1 frame the family simply continues, arrowed off the sheet.
  Graduated and closed against equal-interval and open: the harmonic and arithmetic ladders of
  the parent plates, here as whole figures. The frame areas stay in exact reciprocal, product 1
  at every graduation, and when the feet fuse the arcs straighten, the rectangles square, and
  the inward figure becomes the one-point perspective room of the drawing books.


  
What is checked, what is a reading

  
The constructions run under check-folded-rigs-3d-slab.js — 22 numbered assertions: the
  triple identity of the red rectangle (near face, ×1 print, shared frame); strict nesting,
  camera inside red inside glide, at every depth and any feet; harmonic graduation inward and
  equal strides outward; the corner connectors' endpoints on their frames, the arcs' hyperbola
  relation and their convex bow toward the origin against the glide's exact straightness; the
  far camera frame's corners as physical strikes of the slab's far corners; the area product;
  closedness (camera frames converge on the origin) against openness (glide frames unbounded);
  the floor-grid lines' membership in the same two families; straightening and squaring exactly
  at u = v; the seam meter's zero set; and containment of the drawing in all three
  view presets. The framings — "a room seen from its doorway," the glide as "the slab unfolded
  toward the viewer," and the two constructions as representing opposite sides of the picture
  plane — are readings of the sheet, offered for the seasoning shelf, not theorems.
