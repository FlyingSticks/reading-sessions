Sections of one pencil

Demote the picture plane. It is not the privileged surface of the scene but one section of the spectator pencil through the station point C — any surface cutting the rays is a picture, and the plane is merely the flat one. Once the plane is a section among sections, a small equality the drawing hides comes forward: take the picture surface as a circle through C and compare it with the arc swept about C, and at one section — the one through C's own far point — the two agree in a way that reads as a clean demonstration of the currency lesson. Same rim; the interior re-authored by the closure rule. This plate draws that section and its neighbours.

red radius R (blue held at D) R = D

The two shapes about the station C. The blue whole circle passes through C on the diameter D; the red semicircle is swept at radius R about C. Drag R. At R = D the blue circle is internally tangent to the red arc at their shared far point, and two parities snap on together: the red arc and the blue circumference are equal in length (πR = πD), and the red half-disk is exactly twice the blue circle — so the residual red, the half-disk with the blue circle removed (adjacent, no overlap in the flat), equals the whole blue circle in area. One section, both currencies settled.

The measures

What to look for

The two equalities are one condition wearing two coats. Length: the red arc is πR, the blue circumference is πD; they match iff R = D. Area: the red half-disk is πR²/2, the blue circle πD²/4, so the half-disk carries twice the blue whenever R = D, and the residual — what is left of the half-disk once the blue circle it contains is taken away — is the other half, equal to the blue. Both resolve to R = D, and R = D is nothing but the section of the pencil through C's far point: the blue circle drawn through C reaches distance D from C, the red arc swept about C reaches distance R, and they kiss exactly when those distances agree. The tangent picture you drew is the pencil sectioned at its own turning point.

This is the invariant-currency lesson made exact — the response to Russell's barber from the musing, in one figure. Perimeter is the portable scalar, the currency carried across unchanged; area is the interior, re-authored by the closure rule, and it does not travel with the rim. At R = D the rim is conserved (arc = circumference) while the closure rule reassigns the area two-to-one, and the residual bookkeeping — half-disk minus blue equals blue — is the ledger balancing. Nothing here classifies itself; two shapes hold one length between them and split one area, which is why the figure stays stable where a self-membership rule would not. A picture surface through its own station is the planar cousin of the reader E on the sphere: a section that contains the very point the pencil turns on.

The plane is one section of the pencil, and R = D is the section through the station's far point. There the rim is a conserved currency and the area is re-authored — same boundary, interior reassigned — the equal-measure fact and the invariant-currency musing meeting in a single tangent circle.

Conventions

Judgment calls flagged: "perimeter" for the red shape means its curved rim, the semicircular arc πR, matching the blue circle's curved rim πD — the diameter segment of the half-disk is the section line, not part of the swept rim, and is excluded by the same reading that excludes the picture plane's own frame; the residual-area equality is the half-disk minus the contained blue circle, an adjacency in the flat with no overlap, exactly as the sketch reads it; the blue circle is held at D and R is dragged, so the section through C's far point is the moving target R = D. The station point C is drawn but not identified with any rig point here — the pencil is the spectator pencil of the picture-plane discussion, not the imaging congruence.

Glossary

spectator pencil
The rays from the station C to the picture; any surface cutting them is a section, and a picture. The plane is the flat section.
section
A surface across the pencil. Different sections carry the same rays and so the same picture, read on different shapes.
the far point
The point of the blue circle most distant from C, at distance D; the red arc reaches it iff R = D. The tangent section.
residual red
The red half-disk with the contained blue circle removed. At R = D it equals the blue circle in area — the closure rule's reassignment.
portable scalar
A measure carried unchanged between shapes — here the rim length. The currency of the invariant-currency musing.
re-authored interior
Area, which does not travel with the rim: the same boundary can enclose a reassigned area under a changed closure rule.
