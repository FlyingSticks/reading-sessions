Halve the angle

A complex number is an arrow from the post: a length and a direction. Multiplying two of them multiplies the lengths and adds the directions. Everything about square roots follows from that one rule, applied twice and then undone.

1 · Multiply: add the angles

a: length , angle
b: length , angle
a·b: length , angle
Drag a and b. The pale triangle on base 0→1 with apex a is the same shape as the triangle on base 0→b with apex a·b: that is the drafting construction of the product, and the reason angles add.

2 · Square: double the angle

w: length , angle
w²: length , angle
Multiplying w by itself: the angle added to itself, the length times itself. Walk w once round and w² goes round twice (the purple trail is laid down twice over). So two different w, half a turn apart, land on the same w².

3 · Square root: halve the angle

z: length , angle
√z: length , angle — or
Undo panel 2: root the length, halve the angle. But z's angle can be read two ways, θ or θ + 360°, and their halves differ by 180°. Hence two roots, opposite each other. The second half-arc, dashed, shows the other reading.

Why the walk swaps the roots. Panel 3's two roots are the two halvings of z's angle. Carry z once round the post, 360°, and whichever half you were tracking has gained 180°: it is now standing where the other one stood. Nothing jumped; the halving simply delivered half of a full turn. Carry z round twice and the tracked half has gained 360°, home again. That is the whole of what the walk page shows, and it is only the rule of panel 1.
