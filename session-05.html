One identity, and its three grammars

Read this session: the section's mirror identity, proved three ways — and the ancestor figure that held all three before they had names

Filed a week after it was worked, from the working record, under the backlog ruling of 13 September; the lateness is the filing's, not the work's. The session grew out of the section: what ru and rv are (reciprocals of the slits' distances in front of the plane), what s = rλ measures (depth behind the plane in the slit's own unit), and how the three marks Xg, x, Xc on the picture plane carry two structures at once — a geometric progression when one point is read under three rigs, a harmonic progression when one rig walks equal depth steps, with the harmonic range (Xg, Xc; x, −x) = −1 as the hinge between them. The earlier "harmonic slot" description was acknowledged fuzzy and replaced. Δ = rurv(rv − ru) was located as the product of the three equations whose zero-locus is the fused/split boundary — not a formula but a conjunction.

Landed The three grammars

The mirror identity Xg·Xc = x² was asked for as a picture and returned as a taxonomy. Three proofs, three grammars: Euclid I.43, areas and a straightedge, no circle drawn and no unit declared — the anchorless grammar; the semicircle altitude, one compass stroke — the anchored grammar, the circle playing exactly the marked-conic anchor of §8.3′; and the mirror circle — the projective grammar, harmonic range and tangent witness, the inversion z ↦ x²/z wearing its own fixed points. One slider s drives all three at once.

Recognized The ancestor figure

The reader then produced an older page of his own — Overflow's harmonic area identity — and asked how it connects. It is the ancestor: the figure that held all three grammars before they had names. Its OAB + BPD = OP² theorem runs on I.37/I.43 area cancellation (anchorless); its semicircle on the fixed diameter AD is the compass gadget in its dual deployment, fixed diameter with moving altitude rather than the reverse (anchored); and its fan printing the reciprocal ladder 1/n along the top edge is the mirror circle's projective grammar in older clothes. The taxonomy was not invented this session; it was excavated.

Landed The reference page

All four figures — the three grammars and their ancestor — were combined into one reference page, One identity, three grammars, with annotations, captions, a ledger table, and eight live checks re-run on every input. Its check script passed 19/19, with a headless render pass that caught two label collisions at snap positions before delivery — the checks catching the drawing, as they usually do, rather than the geometry.

Closed The anchors made visible

The closing question — how the diagram's double fraction (Xc/x)/(x/Xg) relates to the harmonic range (Xg, Xc; x, −x) = −1 — closed the session on its cleanest sentence: they are the same theorem in different dress. The double fraction reads the geometric progression through the invisible anchor pair {0, ∞}; the harmonic range fires those anchors and replaces them with the visible fixed points {x, −x} of the inversion, which is what makes the statement projectively invariant. An anchorless grammar is one that never needed the pair at all.
