one axis, two pencils — the rigs of u and v, folded together

u and v each mark a cross-slit on the slits axis; the two slits stand at right angles in space. Here the v-plane is rotated 90° about the axis until it lies flat in the u-plane, the way an elevation is folded onto a plan. Drag u, v, and the point; work the fold.


  

    
u — foot of the vertical slit ℓu; its pencil lives in the plane of the paper and prints the image coordinate x.

    
v — foot of the horizontal slit ℓv; its pencil lives in the folding plane and prints the image coordinate y.

    
picture plane at λ = 0 — the ×1 level of both channels; in the fold, one printed line carries both rulers.

    
the point — one scene point (λ, X, Y); its two traces stand on the same ordinate λ, tied by the dotted recall line.

  






  
What the fold is

  
This is Monge’s move with an unusual ground line. The two slits ℓu and ℓv
  are perpendicular but skew — they never meet — and the slits axis is precisely their
  common perpendicular: u and v are its feet, and the slit gap is
  D = |u−v|. Each rig is a plane figure: the u-rig lives in the
  plane through the axis to which ℓu is perpendicular, the v-rig in the plane at right
  angles to that one. Rotating the v-plane 90° about the axis lays both figures on one drawing
  plane — plan and elevation folded onto a shared ground line, except that here the ground line is
  the common perpendicular of the two slits itself.


  
Two pencils, one camera

  
Seen in its own plane, each rig is a complete pencil. Every ray the camera owns, drawn in the
  u-plane, passes through the point u; drawn in the v-plane, it passes through v. So the cross-slit
  camera is not one pencil in space, as a pinhole is — it is the meshing of two plane
  pencils hinged on their common perpendicular. The fold makes the meshing visible: two centers on
  one axis, two fans overlapped on one plane.


  
How the rigs interact

  
One point takes out two loans. Its plan trace borrows x from u, its elevation trace
  borrows y from v, and the two traces stand on the same ordinate λ — the dotted recall
  line of descriptive geometry. The loans carry different interest:
  x = X·(−u)/(λ−u) but y = Y·(−v)/(λ−v).
  Same λ, different denominators, so the per-depth aspect ratio
  AR(λ) = m_v/m_u runs from exactly 1 on the picture plane and settles
  monotonically toward r_u/r_v at depth — the distortion is anamorphic,
  per-level, and bounded. That bound is the budget.


  
Degenerations

  
Drag v onto u (or press Fuse) and the two centers coincide: the pencils merge into one,
  the denominators agree at every depth, AR pins to 1, and the rig is an ordinary perspective
  camera. Send both feet to −∞ and each fan opens into a parallel bundle — the corner rig. The
  cross-slit is what lives between those two collapses.


  
Reading the folded view

  
Folded flat, the two rigs superimpose but do not intersect: they share only the axis, and the
  right angle between the slits survives only as the fold angle θ that the paper no longer shows.
  What prints as two crossing fans on one plane remains, in space, a pair of skew lines shaking
  hands along their common perpendicular. The overlap is a drawing convenience — planes adjacent in
  space, stacked on the paper — and the recall line at λ is what keeps the bookkeeping honest.
