The bones of the harmonic quadruple

Read this session: one set S, A, Q, C with cross-ratio −1, read by tangency and by area; its two circles named fixed and breathing; the plate that holds them found to hold the rig; a landing nobody asked for; orthogonality ruled the law

Three sittings over three days, all on the same four points. Filed four days after it closed; the stamp admits it.

Landed The two circles · 18 September

The reader's correction opened the session: a teal bar drawn S→Q was standing for the cross-ratio's denominator pair, which is S→A and Q→C. The 2:1 outside split at Q = 2 was found to be a coincidence of that position; the general statement is that S divides A–C outside in the ratio Q divides it inside. The two circles of a harmonic pair reads the set once by tangency on the line and once by area in two equal-area plates — the fixed circle on A–C, OQ·OS = OA² = 9, and the breathing circle on Q–S, MA·MC = MQ² — and, at the reader's request, a third plate for his quarter-turn sketch: x ↦ −1/n at the unit corner, the elliptic partner of the line's hyperbolic involution, its fixed points expelled to ±i. Smoke-tested in the sitting; no check file in this repository.

Landed The fixed square and the breathing square · 19 September

The reader's own diagram, reviewed for accuracy and then built: the fixed square and the breathing square, the two Newton products riding a draggable Q as Euclid I.43 complements about a diagonal. The reader's rulings are part of the record: one flagged correction — the printed 1.56's truncation — was declined as a signature; at revision he dropped the footnote for 1.5625 alone, halved the line weights, and asked for definitions of breathing square and Newton products. Placement ruled for the shelf, not the book's spine: two centres rather than two readings, and no camera — though kin to Chapter 3's VM·VI = VP², a Newton product of the same shape. Smoke-tested at 344; no check file in this repository.

Found The bones of the rig · 20 September

The reader saw that the plate held “the geometric bones” of the cross-slit construction, and the bones of the rig gave them their rig names: image plane at S, blue slit at A, constructed harmonic plane at Q, red slit at C, with the I.43 complements, the channel swap, and the fixed circle's tangent witness, T directly above Q. The plate made a joke on its own — the slider resting at Q = 1.535 puts S at π — verified and left in. The reader found v1 “very close to intuitively understandable”; the gap was an asymmetry, one witness where there should be two. Giving the breathing circle its own tangent in v2 surfaced what no one had asked for: T′ lands exactly above A, for every Q, the mirror of T above Q. Then v3 made the balance point Q = 6 − 3√2 the slider's right stop — MQ = OA, the circles congruent, OQ = 3(√2 − 1) and OS = 3(√2 + 1), priced in silver units — and ruled what holds everywhere else: the two circles cross at right angles at every Q, OM² = OA² + MQ², equivalent to the harmonic condition itself. Orthogonality is the law; congruence is the event.
