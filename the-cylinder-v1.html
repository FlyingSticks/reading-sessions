The cylinder

A circle at every depth, read by a split rig — the seam as the place where a cylinder is still a cone

A right circular cylinder stands along the depth axis: a circle of radius ρ on the picture plane, and the same circle at every level behind it. A pinhole sees it as a circular cone, one circle per level, all similar. A two-slit rig reads the two coordinates of every point through two different slits, at two different rates, so each level's circle becomes an ellipse — camera semi-axes ρ/(1 + ruλ) across and ρ/(1 + rvλ) up, glide semi-axes ρ(1 + ruλ) and ρ(1 + rvλ) — and the axis ratio changes level by level. Only on the seam, ru = rv, are all the ellipses circles and the cone comes back.

What to try

Press Equal depths: every ellipse is a circle, the camera's nested inward and the glide's outward, and the ratio line lies flat on 1 — a cone, twice. Press Split them: the circles flatten into ellipses whose ratio drifts away from 1 as λ grows, and the ratio line bends. Drag the slits and watch the drift change sign when v passes u. Press Pushbroom: with the v slit far off, rv → 0, the vertical axis stops shrinking altogether and the deep ellipses go long and flat — the pushbroom's signature, one direction unforeshortened.

What is being claimed

Per direction, the camera and glide semi-axes multiply to ρ² at every level: the two ellipses are inverse to one another about the true circle, which is the mirror relation Xg·Xc = x² stated for a whole curve. The axis ratio of the camera ellipse at level λ is (1 + ruλ)/(1 + rvλ), the glide's its reciprocal; both are 1 for every λ exactly when ru = rv. Off the seam the ratio is monotone in λ and tends to ru/rv as the level recedes, so the deep image of the cylinder settles toward a fixed ellipse rather than a point — the split rig has no apex. That is why the seam is the locus where a cylinder is still a cone: the cone needs all its sections similar, and similarity across levels is exactly equal rates.

Glossary

the cylinder
A circle of radius ρ at every level of the slab, the same circle throughout; the simplest object whose sections are all alike, hence the sharpest test of whether a reading keeps them alike.
axis ratio
Up over across for one level's image ellipse: (1 + ruλ)/(1 + rvλ) for the camera, its reciprocal for glide. Equal to 1 at λ = 0 for every rig, and at every λ only on the seam.
cone test
A reading sees the cylinder as a cone when all its level images are similar. Pinhole and seam pass; every split rig fails, with the failure measured by the ratio line's departure from flat.
deep limit
The ellipse the camera images tend to as λ → ∞: axis ratio ru/rv, size shrinking as 1/λ. The glide images grow without bound at the same ratio.
inverse ellipses
Camera and glide images of one level, whose semi-axes multiply to ρ² direction by direction — the mirror relation extended from a mark to a curve.
