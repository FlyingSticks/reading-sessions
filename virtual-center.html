Two pivots, one virtual center

One horizon, three candidate centers. B and D are centers by incidence: lines of indistinguishable probes pivot on them, and the drawing can demonstrate it. C is a center by computation: nothing passes through it, no pencil concurs there, yet the rig's scale law roots at it exactly, and the range A B D determines it by incidence alone. Vanishing points have always existed in precisely this mode — unoccupied, load-bearing. C shows the camera itself has one.

Their pointwise average is the gold dashed line — and its root is C, at every setting of the sliders. Below the zero line the image is inverted. B and D own the rays; C owns the average.

    What to look for

    Thread B. Five probe positions ride one line through B. Slide the probe along it, swing the line, push the probes apart: the crimson mark on the glass does not move. One line, one mark — that is what it is to be a center. The purple rays, cast by the same probes through D, were not invited: they fan, and their marks scatter down the glass. A line through B threads one channel only.

    Thread D. The same demonstration with the colors exchanged. Together the two modes show the rig has two centers, each real, each partial.

    Thread C. The same five probes, now on a line through C. The hollow gold ring is the mark a pinhole at C promises — and the promise is internally consistent: it holds still as you slide. But no real channel honors it. Both the crimson and the purple marks scatter; nothing in the optics pivots. C moves nothing. Then look down at the chart: the gold average line roots at C regardless. What C cannot do with rays it does exactly with rates.

    Fuse the slits. Drag B and D together and watch C get caught between them: at B = D the three centers coincide and C goes real — light actually passes through it. The gap you open by pulling the slits apart is the exact residue by which this rig fails to be a pinhole camera.

    Why C exists at all

    Twice over, and the two reasons agree.

    Projectively, C is the fourth harmonic point of the glass: the unique point with (B, D; A, C) = −1, constructible from A, B, D by straightedge alone. The range that carries the perspective construction already contains its own eye, folded into the other three marks the way a harmonic set always carries its fourth member implicitly. On the axis this is the polar plane of the sphere page collapsed to one dimension: the sphere meets the axis at B and D, and the polar of A crosses at their harmonic conjugate.

    Optically, C is the answer to an inverse problem. Hand someone the glass alone — marks and sizes, no rig — and let them assume a single pinhole took the picture. Solving for where that pinhole must have been yields C, uniquely, because the reciprocal scales of the two channels average to the reciprocal scale of a pinhole at the harmonic mean, at every depth. C is the principal point of the two-slit system: a point of the model, not of the optics — real as a determination, fictitious as a location. Sizes can be forged by this fiction; incidence cannot. Only ray information — the threading test above — tells the two cameras apart.

    Glossary

    mark
    The point where a probe's image lands on the glass. In this flattening each channel casts its own mark on the shared axis; the crimson and purple marks are the two coordinates of the one mark the full rig would record.
    channel
    One slit's share of the projection. Each channel acts as a one-dimensional pinhole camera: the u-channel centered at B, the v-channel at D.
    center by incidence (pivot)
    A point rays actually pass through, demonstrable in the drawing: pencils of indistinguishable probe positions concur there. B and D are centers by incidence.
    center by computation (virtual center)
    A point posited to make the arithmetic close, though nothing travels through it — the solution to an inverse problem, not a place light visits. C is one; so is the principal point of a thick lens, which no refraction happens at yet every image size obeys.
    ray-equivalence class
    All probe positions sharing a line through a center, hence sharing that channel's mark. The glass records classes, not points: it is transparent to depth along a ray while opaque as a surface.
    harmonic conjugate · cross-ratio
    Four collinear points p, q, r, s have cross-ratio ((r−p)(s−q)) / ((r−q)(s−p)), the one projective invariant of a line. When it equals −1 the pair r, s divides the pair p, q harmonically, and s is constructible from the other three by incidence alone. Here (B, D; A, C) = −1.
    harmonic mean
    HM = 2BD/(B+D). Named for the harmonic range: the point at depth HM is the harmonic conjugate of the origin with respect to B and D. The projective and arithmetic descriptions of C are the same fact.
    neutral surface
    The transverse plane at depth HM — in the round, the polar plane of the glass point, carrying the circle where the tangent cone touches the sphere on B, D as diameter. C is where it crosses the axis.
    reciprocal scale (rate)
    1 − z/Z for a center at depth Z evaluated at scene depth z: the reciprocal of the channel's magnification, affine in depth. Perspective composes in rates, not scales — which is why the honest average of two cameras is an average of reciprocals, and why it lands on the harmonic mean.
    station point · vanishing point
    The eye of a perspective, and the unoccupied points that discipline it. A vanishing point is real as a determination and fictitious as a location; every line in the drawing obeys it, nothing stands at it. C is a point of the same species, found on the camera side of the glass rather than the picture side.

    Conventions

    Glass (image plane) at A = 0; scene constrained beyond both slits, so every mark is inverted. Book-rig colors: crimson for the vertical slit's center B, purple for the horizontal slit's D, gold for the neutral surface and everything C promises. Judgment calls flagged: the drawing flattens the rig to one axis, each channel rendered as a 1-D pinhole on the shared horizon — the unique 3-D transversal threading both slits degenerates here to the axis itself, so the two threading modes exhibit the pivots separately. The probe keeps 70 units beyond the deeper slit to hold magnifications finite; marks that leave the frame are shown as open arrowheads at the glass edge. Plates in this series use canvas; this one is SVG, for label crispness and simpler pointer handling on the iPad — flagged as a departure.

    Checks

    The page re-runs the list above on every interaction, computed from the drawn geometry: C from the polar formula against C solved from the cross-ratio condition; (B, D; A, C) = −1; invariance of the threaded channel's mark along the pivot line, and asserted non-invariance of both real channels along the C-line; rate closure at the probe's depth; agreement of the drawn intersection with the closed-form mark; inversion of every mark.
