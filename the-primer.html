Lines in Space: a Primer

A staircase of short plates leading up to the title of One Congruence, Two Readings. Each plate takes one idea the book uses and sets it out as a drawing with a glossary. The plates state nothing new; they are the classical ground the book stands on, in drafting language.

plate 1 · four-numbers-for-a-line.html

Four Numbers for a Line

A straight line in space is named by four numbers measured against one fixed card: two for where it pierces the card, two for which way it points.

What is claimedStanding
A line in space takes four numbers.classical
Changing the card changes the numbers and not the count.classical
A line parallel to the card has no numbers in this scheme.classical
The three drawings are one projection of one set of coordinates.computed by the drawing code; no separate check

Toward the book. Chapter 1. The book's one object is a line congruence, which its glossary calls a two-parameter family of lines. This plate supplies the number that "two" is taken from: four.

plate 2 · pinhole-double-slit-and-four.html

Pinhole, Double Slit and the Number Four

A camera fixes two of a line's four numbers in advance and leaves two free. A pinhole fixes both on one card; a double slit fixes one on each. An addendum sets out the two meanings of "congruence".

What is claimedStanding
A point costs two numbers; a slit costs one. Pinhole: 4 − 2 = 2. Double slit: 4 − 1 − 1 = 2.classical
Through a general point there is one ray of the pinhole and one ray of the double slit.classical
A pinhole is two slits crossed on one card; the family then falls into the lines through C and the lines lying in the card.classical; not checked here
The pairing (P, P′; A, B) = −1 carries every ray onto itself, and becomes a half-turn when slit b is at infinity.check-congruence.py, 9/9
The seam pairing of Chapter 3 is this pairing restricted to a line.STRUCK: tested 12 November, check-the-two-slit-involution.js

Toward the book. Chapter 1: Line congruence, Slit, Pinhole (the book's entry: both slits at one depth, every line through one centre), Pushbroom. Chapter 8: Anamorphic dissociation says the pinhole spends its whole ray budget at one point and the double slit spends it along each slit. That is this plate's "a point costs two, a slit costs one" in the book's words.

plate 3 · level-and-square-to-the-card.html

Level, and Square to the Card

"At a right angle to slit a" (level, bearing free) is kept apart from "square to the card" (one direction). Slit b at infinity is the whole horizon of level directions. An addendum treats sign: one point at infinity, two senses.

What is claimedStanding
Slit b as one direction gives a one-number family; slit b as the horizon gives a two-number family.classical
At a finite depth the rays are tilted; the tilt goes to 0 as slit b recedes.tilts computed by the drawing code
In a paraline the horizon shows only as every level plane carrying the same fan of directions.classical
The pairing of P with P′ is one mirror on the line (sign −1) and two mirrors, a half-turn, in space (sign +1).classical; no check run

Toward the book. Chapter 1: Pushbroom, a corner of the rate square. Compare the book's Plane at infinity in Chapter 2, which is used there as a point on a depth line; the plate's horizon is a line in that plane. The sign addendum belongs with the mirror thread, not with the book.

plate 4 · correspondences-and-circuits.html

Correspondences and Circuits

What an involution is made of, one requirement at a time, and the patterns a reversible correspondence can have: closed circuits, and for infinitely many things, endless chains.

What is claimedStanding
A reversible correspondence on finitely many things is a set of closed circuits.classical
An involution is one with no circuit longer than 2 (and, for infinitely many things, no endless chain).classical
Three dots: 27 correspondences, 6 reversible. Four dots: 24 reversible, in five patterns.counts asserted by the drawing code from a complete listing

Toward the book. Chapter 4: Involution, Anharmonic group, 3-cycle. The six reversible correspondences on three dots are the six members of that group: the three pairs are its three involutions and the two circuits of three are its two 3-cycles. The plate does not yet say so. That is plate 6.

plate 5 · beneath-the-involution.html

Beneath the Involution

In geometry the involution is built from incidence. The harmonic partner is constructed by joins and meets alone, applied to every point to give an involution, and shown to need no measure.

What is claimedStanding
The construction of figure 1 yields the harmonic partner, (P, P′; A, B) = −1.asserted numerically for two constructions
The result does not depend on the choice of S and T.a theorem (Desargues); stated, not demonstrated
Applied to every point it is an involution with A and B as the only points that stay.asserted numerically for the pairs drawn
−1 means equal and opposite; a perspective view changes every ratio and leaves it alone.asserted numerically for one projection

Toward the book. Chapters 1 and 3: Harmonic cross-ratio. Chapter 4: Harmonic orbit. The mirror circle relation VM · VI = VP² of Chapter 3 is also a pairing that undoes itself, but it is not the two-slit pairing of plate 2; that identification was tested and struck.

Open items

  • Unchecked statements. On plate 2, the falling of the crossed-slit family into two pieces. In conversation only, and on no plate: that the pinhole and "all lines in one plane" are duals and the double slit is its own dual. The book's Chapter 5 treats duality in its own terms; the two have not been compared.
  • Possible overlap. Plate 5, figures 1 and 2, may repeat plate 9 of the mirror thread. Not compared.
