Index · lines in space

Lines in Space: a Primer

A staircase of short plates leading up to the title of One Congruence, Two Readings. Each plate takes one idea the book uses and sets it out as a drawing with a glossary. The mathematics on the plates is classical; it is the ground the book stands on, put in drafting language. What is the author's own is the framing: the tie between the two meanings of "congruence", and every pointer from a plate to the book.

plate 1 · four-numbers-for-a-line.html

Four Numbers for a Line

A straight line in space is named by four numbers measured against one fixed card: two for where it pierces the card, two for which way it points.

Audit. Audited outside on 19 November 2026 and amended.

What is claimedStanding
A line that pierces the card is named by four numbers; the lines of space form a four-number family.classical; audited
With α and β both strictly between −90° and +90°, each direction that pierces the card has exactly one pair.classical; audited
Changing the card changes some or all of the numbers and not the count.classical; audited
A line whose direction is parallel to the card, whether it misses the card or lies in it, has no numbers in this scheme.classical; audited (the audit added the lines lying in the card)
The three drawings are one projection of one set of coordinates.computed by the drawing code; no separate check

Toward the book. Chapter 1. The book's one object is a line congruence, which its glossary calls a two-parameter family of lines. This plate supplies the number that "two" is taken from: four.

plate 2 · pinhole-double-slit-and-four.html

Pinhole, Double Slit and the Number Four

A camera fixes two of a line's four numbers in advance and leaves two free. A pinhole fixes both on one card; a double slit fixes one on each. An addendum sets out the two meanings of "congruence".

Audit. Audited outside on 19 November 2026 and amended. Its check script was rewritten.

What is claimedStanding
A point imposes two conditions on a line's four numbers; a slit imposes one. Pinhole: 4 − 2 = 2. Double slit: 4 − 1 − 1 = 2.classical; audited
Through a general point there is one ray of the pinhole and one ray of the double slit. Left out: C; and every point of the two cards.classical; audited; sampled in check-congruence.py
A pinhole is two slits crossed on one card; the lines meeting both are the lines through C and the lines lying in the card, except, in ordinary space, lines in the card parallel to a slit.classical; audited (the exception is the audit's); sampled in the check
The pairing (P, P′; A, B) = −1 undoes itself, carries lines to lines, and carries exactly the rays and the two slits onto themselves.classical; audited; check-congruence.py, 20/20
The pairing preserves distance only when one slit is the horizon of the planes at right angles to the other. A being the midpoint of P and P′ is not enough.classical; audited (the audit's correction); oblique counter-example in the check
Wherever slit b is at infinity and AP′ = AP, the pairing preserves distance.STRUCK: audit of 19 November; false when slit b is oblique to slit a
The seam pairing of Chapter 3 is this pairing restricted to a line.STRUCK: tested 12 November, check-the-two-slit-involution.js

Toward the book. Chapter 1: Line congruence, Slit, Pinhole (the book's entry: both slits at one depth, every line through one centre; it keeps only the lines through the centre, where the plate also notes the lines lying in the card), Pushbroom. Compare Chapter 8, Anamorphic dissociation: its "∞² ray budget" is this plate's two-number family of rays: as many rays as two free numbers can name. That entry is about where the rays meet, all at one point for the pinhole and along each slit for the double slit. The plate's "a point costs two, a slit costs one" is a count of conditions. The two are neighbours, not the same statement.

plate 3 · level-and-square-to-the-card.html

Level, and Square to the Card

"At a right angle to slit a" (level, bearing free) is kept apart from "square to the card" (one direction). Slit b at infinity is the whole horizon of level directions. An addendum treats sign: one point at infinity, two senses.

Audit. Audited outside on 19 November 2026 and amended.

What is claimedStanding
Slit b as one direction gives a one-number family; slit b as the horizon gives a two-number family (ordinary lines only).classical; audited
At a finite depth the rays are tilted; the tilt goes to 0 as slit b recedes.tilts computed by the drawing code; audited
A level line receding at a fixed height tends to the horizon, whatever its bearing.classical; audited (the audit's wording)
Any level line in the second card, whatever its bearing, tends to the horizon.STRUCK: audit of 19 November; a card parallel to the first holds level lines of one bearing only
In a paraline the horizon shows only as every level plane carrying the same fan of directions.classical; audited
Sign belongs to a map, not to a pairing. The mirror with point A (−1), the half-turn about slit a (+1) and a mirror with a plane (−1) all make the same pairing on the ray.classical; audited; no check run
The same pairing has sign −1 on the line and +1 one dimension up.STRUCK: audit of 19 November; it conflated a map with its restriction to a line

Toward the book. Chapter 1: Pushbroom is the book's name for the congruence with one slit at a finite depth and the other at infinity. Its entry gives it two corners of the rate square, (1,0) and (0,1), and says both readings fuse there. Plate 3 draws one of those two corners, the one with the upright slit at a finite depth and the level slit at infinity; which of (1,0) and (0,1) that is has not been worked out here. The plate draws the family of rays only, with no graduation. The right angle between the two slits is not something the plate adds: the book's rig already has one upright slit and one level slit, so the right angle is built in, and the plate only says it out loud. Compare the book's Plane at infinity in Chapter 2: despite its name, that entry defines a point, "the fourth distinguished point of a line". The plate's horizon is a whole line of such points. The sign addendum belongs with the mirror thread, not with the book.

plate 4 · correspondences-and-circuits.html

Correspondences and Circuits

What an involution is made of, one requirement at a time, and the patterns a reversible correspondence can have: closed circuits, and for infinitely many things, endless chains.

Audit. Audited outside on 19 November 2026 and amended.

What is claimedStanding
A reversible correspondence on finitely many things is a set of closed circuits.classical; audited
On finitely many things an involution is a reversible correspondence with at least one pair and no circuit longer than 2; for infinitely many, add: and no endless chain. By convention the identity is not counted. Three dots have 3 involutions; four dots have 9.classical. The audit asked for one convention on the identity; the convention now used was chosen afterwards, to agree with the book, and the two counts have not been audited
A reversible correspondence on infinitely many things is made of closed circuits and endless chains and nothing else.classical; audited
Three dots: 27 correspondences, 6 reversible. Four dots: 24 reversible, in five patterns.counts asserted by the drawing code from a complete listing; audited
A reversible correspondence, run backwards, is a different rule.STRUCK as a general statement: audit of 19 November; true only when a circuit has length 3 or more, or there is an endless chain
The identity is simpler than an involution.STRUCK: audit of 19 November, as inconsistent with the rest of the plate; "simpler" was never defined. The convention has since been reversed (the identity is not counted), but the sentence stays off the plate

Toward the book. Chapter 4: Involution, Anharmonic group, 3-cycle. The six reversible correspondences on three dots correspond, one for one, to the six members of that group: the three with one pair to the three the book calls involutions, the two circuits of three to its two 3-cycles, the identity to the identity. Plate 4 and the book now count alike: the identity is not an involution, so there are three on three dots. They are not the same objects (the book's six are maps of a cross-ratio), and the correspondence becomes a statement about groups only when composition is brought in. That is plate 6.

plate 5 · beneath-the-involution.html

Beneath the Involution

In geometry the involution is built from incidence. The harmonic partner is constructed by joins and meets alone, applied to every point to give an involution, and shown to need no measure.

Audit. Audited outside on 19 November 2026 and amended.

What is claimedStanding
For three different points A, B, P, the construction of figure 1 yields the harmonic partner, (P, P′; A, B) = −1.classical; audited; asserted numerically for two constructions
The result does not depend on the choice of S and T.a theorem resting on Desargues' theorem; attribution confirmed by the audit; stated, not demonstrated
Applied to every other point, with A and B each paired with itself, it is an involution with A and B as the only points that stay.classical; audited
Once the point at infinity is given, incidence constructs the midpoint with no ruler.classical; audited (the audit's wording)
Incidence, which has no ruler, can still say "midpoint".STRUCK: audit of 19 November; incidence alone cannot, the point at infinity must be given first
−1 means equal and opposite: the unit cancels, then the common size.classical; audited
For a fourth point that is not the partner, nothing cancels and a measurement is left over.STRUCK: audit of 19 November; the unit still cancels and what is left is a plain number
A perspective view in general changes lengths and ratios of lengths and always leaves the harmonic relation alone.classical; audited; asserted numerically for one projection

Toward the book. Chapters 1 and 3: Harmonic cross-ratio. Chapter 4: Harmonic orbit. The mirror circle relation VM · VI = VP² of Chapter 3 is also a pairing that undoes itself. Read with V and P held still, it pairs M with I along the seam (the entry's "glide mark" and "cross-slit image"), and pairs V itself with the point at infinity. The glossary entry does not say what the four letters stand for; this reading of them is the author's and has not been checked against Chapter 3. The pairing is the classical inversion in a circle, taken along one line, but it is not the two-slit pairing of plate 2; that identification was tested and struck.

  • Audits. Plates 1 to 5 were audited outside on 19 November 2026 and amended; what was struck is shown struck in their tables. The concordance's standard names and this index's standings were audited the same day and amended. The pointers to the book were audited the same day in a second pass, against seventeen entries of the book's glossary quoted to the auditor, and amended.
  • Unchecked statements. In conversation only, and on no plate: that the pinhole and "all lines in one plane" are duals and the double slit is its own dual. The book's Chapter 5 treats duality in its own terms; the two have not been compared.
  • Possible overlap. Plate 5, figures 1 and 2, may repeat plate 9 of the mirror thread. Not compared.
  • "Toward the book" paragraphs are the author's own. Some only cite where the book uses an idea. Others identify a plate's idea with one of the book's, and those are claims. None asserts that a plate proves anything in the book. They were audited outside on 19 November 2026 against seventeen glossary entries quoted word for word. The auditor saw those entries only, not the chapters, so a pointer that depends on a chapter's argument is still unaudited.
  • Struck from the pointers to the book. (1) "Anamorphic dissociation is plate 2's 'a point costs two, a slit costs one' in the book's words": struck, audit of 19 November; the entry is about where rays meet, the plate counts conditions. (2) "The plate adds that slit b must be the horizon of the planes at right angles to the other": struck, second audit of 19 November; the book's rig already has the right angle built in. (3) In the concordance, "the horizon is one line in that plane" beside Plane at infinity with no warning: struck, same audit; the book's entry of that name defines a point. (4) Four rows of the concordance set a plain word beside a book word with no "compare" (congruence, slit, slit a, the −1): withdrawn, same audit; the book's entries are narrower, or name a different kind of thing.
  • A convention changed twice. Whether the identity is an involution. Plate 4 first treated it both ways; the audit of 19 November asked for one convention, and "it counts" was chosen; later the same day that was reversed to "it does not count", to agree with the book's Involution entry and the usual practice in geometry. Plates 4 and 5, this index and the concordance all use the last. The reworded sentences have not been audited.
  • What is not classical. The tie between the two meanings of "congruence" rests on a classical fact (the pairing is a half-turn in one arrangement), but presenting it as a tie between two meanings of a word is the author's framing. The references to the mirror thread and to the struck seam claim belong to the author's own ledger.
