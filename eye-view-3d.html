The eye and the volume

The addendum's R3 left one question standing: for the full rig with line-slits, is the view from an eye on the sphere actually a two-point perspective, or only its horizon reading? The check script ran before this page was written, and the verdict is: only its horizon reading — and the volume says so precisely. The imaging rays of the cross-slit have no common point, so there is no eye anywhere, on the sphere or off it; off the seam the image of a straight line bows, and since homographies preserve straightness, no perspective picture can be dressed up as the cross-slit picture. What the volume concedes instead is exact and threefold: each orthographic shadow of the ray congruence is central — the plan through the vertical slit's point, the profile through the horizontal's — so the horizon's two vanishing points are two projected pinholes and the two-point reading is the superposition of two one-point shadows; the least-squares eye is a scene gauge with a closed form, drifting from the near slit to a far-field limit that is exactly AM, the centre of the sphere; and the scene-free compromise is the neutral surface, the vergence midpoint of the two channels — the circle of least confusion.

What to look for

The two shadows are the heart of it. Every imaging ray meets both slits, so its plan projection passes through the point the vertical slit projects to, and its profile projection through the horizontal's — each shadow shows a central camera, and the two centres are the two depths the sketch used as vanishing points. The two-point perspective on the horizon was never a picture the rig takes; it is the drawing that superposes the rig's two central shadows, and the eye on the sphere is the certified reader of that drawing. E's jurisdiction is the profile; the profile is exactly where it ends.

The best-fit eye is the ledger's kind of finding. For a thin frontal slab at depth Z the least-squares point of the imaging rays sits on the axis at zeye(Z) = (Zv(Z−Zh)² + Zh(Z−Zv)²) / ((Z−Zh)² + (Z−Zv)²) — each slit weighted by the squared distance to the other — verified against the numeric fit to under a tenth of a unit. It is scene-bound, hence a gauge; but its far-field limit is a fact: exactly AM. The volume's compromise eye for a distant world is the centre of the sphere — inside it, never on it. The scene-free compromise is different and older: the neutral surface is the vergence midpoint of the two channels at every depth at once — the mean-reciprocal-scale law of the first plate, which optics knows as the circle of least confusion, dioptrically midway between the two line foci. And at z* alone the frontal picture is conformal — true shapes, flipped hand, since one channel has inverted and the other has not.

The sphere holds a station point for reading the drawing, not a pinhole for taking the picture. The volume has no eye; it has two central shadows, a scene-bound best fit drifting to the sphere's centre, and one scene-free surface of agreement at the harmonic mean. The two-point perspective was the horizon's way of telling the truth about a camera that has no viewpoint.

Checks

Re-run on every interaction. The rates behind the verdicts — bow linear in each offset and in the gap, non-centrality dying linearly at the pinhole, the law zeye against the least-squares fit, the far-field limit at AM — are asserted in check_eye_view_3d.js across decades of the parameters.

Conventions

Judgment calls flagged: the profile is the y–z plane, as in the sketch (vertical slit in true length, horizontal slit as a point), so E lifts to E′ = (0, yE, zE); the box's half-depth is fixed at 45 so the two sliders stay legible; the box is kept beyond both slits with clearance, since the v-channel diverges at the slit itself; zeye is the paraxial least-squares form for a thin frontal slab — thick boxes deviate by a few units, and the check compares like with like; the marks on the glass are inverted, as the rig inverts them. The level set at z* is a plane and is called one.

Glossary

congruence
The two-parameter family of imaging rays — all lines meeting both slits. The camera's rays, with no centre.
central camera
One whose rays share a point. The cross-slit is central only in its two shadows and at the pinhole limit.
shadow
An orthographic projection of the congruence: the plan (x–z) and the profile (y–z), each a pencil through one slit's point.
bow · sagitta
The curvature of a straight line's image, measured as maximum deviation from its chord. Zero on the seam and on frontal lines; the obstruction to perspective everywhere else.
best-fit eye
The least-squares common point of the imaging rays for a given scene — on the axis, scene-bound, far-field limit AM.
vergence
Reciprocal distance; the coordinate in which the two channels average arithmetically and the neutral surface is their midpoint.
circle of least confusion
The classical name for the plane dioptrically midway between two line foci — here, the neutral surface.
E′
The station point of the profile drawing, lifted into three dimensions in the profile plane; on the sphere; a reader, not a camera.
