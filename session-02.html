The pattern developed, and the defect named

Read this session: the porch's pattern sentence, pressed until it gave up its vocabulary

This session did not walk a chapter. It walked one sentence — the porch's four-item pattern — and kept asking it questions until a vocabulary fell out. Two companion pages carry the instruments; this page records what they carry and at what status.

Landed as a page The pattern

The four items defined and developed: a law is discovered, agreed on unnoticed, and mute — it constrains everything and marks nothing, so a law alone is not an instrument. A gauge is the graduation that makes it one, and a chosen gauge disappears — no result ever names it, so within a generation it is inherited as if it were law. The quarrel is what that invisibility costs, conducted in essence-vocabulary one level too high, and its persistence is the diagnostic: facts settle; conventions defended as facts never do. The theorem dignifies the whole — proving no gauge has everything, it makes the choice genuine, forced, and older than its own statement: Caesar obeyed the quarter-day before anyone measured it. Companion: The pattern, whose first instrument holds the template still under four cases (calendar, map, canvas, this book — the canvas theorem slot shown honestly empty, filled from next door), with a logical-versus-discovery order toggle; its second draws the title itself — one box under both readings, with cross-slit, perspective and parallel projection as slider presets, and the parallel collapse shown as the reason glide exists: parallel light and visible depth means re-introducing depth by hand.

Reader's rederivation Reach and Turn in his own words

Asked cold what to call the two ways of measuring depth from the floor plane, the reader produced them exactly: "equal intervals and counts which are open in the plane — no infinity can be drawn because it can never be reached," and "gradated depth — an harmonic sequence built up between 0 and 1." Those are Reach and Turn, re-derived from use rather than recalled from the text — the strongest kind of evidence that Chapter 2's pair carves at a joint. The plain-language glosses are worth keeping: counts open in the plane for Reach; the harmonic sequence between 0 and 1 for Turn.

Landed as a page The defect

Defect defined by the closure test: go around a loop that ought to return you; measure the miss. Three properties separate it from a mistake — provable (no craftsmanship reduces it), conserved (the carpet's bump: relocatable, never removable), and the theorem made quantitative (the number the impossibility forbids you to zero out). Three policies exhaust a gauge's options — smear, pile, grade toward a boundary — and the depth rulers run the choice on one line: Reach piles the whole account at unreachable infinity, Turn grades it into every crowding interval, the welded camera gets no choice at all. Beneath every defect, an incommensurability as cause: √2 is the archetype and is not itself a defect — the irrationality is the law's refusal; a defect is that refusal priced on a particular attempted loop. And the tools for living with an incommensurable, the continued-fraction convergents, turn out to be the historical gauges themselves: 1/4 is Caesar, 7/12 is the twelve-note octave, 17/12 and 99/70 the ancient side-and-diagonal reckonings. A gauge is where a civilization stopped the continued fraction. Companion: The defect — the closure test with three laws on a convergent slider, the conserved comma under three policies, and the count-and-placement morph.

Decomposition Gauge = count + placement

A gauge decomposes into a count (the ordinal skeleton — zero, unit, successor) and a placement (where each numbered tick physically sits). Reach and Turn share their count exactly and differ only in placement: the count is the part of the gauge the two rulers agree on; the placement is where the quarrel and the defect live. The defect is born at the joint — whole-step counting pressed against ratio-owing laws — so counting is how a law's refusal becomes a number. The morph instrument shows the count surviving while placement moves.

Reading, human terms Aristotle's two infinities

Reach is the potential infinite made into a ruler — always one more step, the end never an object; Turn is the actual infinite made into one — the totality completed and given a finite address. Projective geometry's founding act, adjoining the points at infinity, was the admission of Turn-type rulers over two millennia of Aristotelian scruple. Theorem 0 gains a reading: the two infinities cannot share a scale — count forever, or complete the count; no ruler does both. Offered on human terms, as a framing rather than a claim about the chapters.

Seasoning The seam as zero-defect locus

The cover says the readings agree on a seam and the split is measured by a single cubic. In this session's vocabulary: the seam is the zero-defect locus — rigs where the circuit from one reading to the other and back closes exactly — and the cubic is the defect-measure everywhere else, a quantity of disagreement rather than a yes-or-no, vanishing where the orbits collapse. The structure matches Gauss–Bonnet's architecture (loops failing to close, integrating the obstruction); the equation has not been audited. Held at seasoning beside session 1's datum yield. If it stands, its natural first use is a single sentence wherever the cubic is introduced; if it shifts, this page keeps the first phrasing.

Open at close of session

The one-rate-zero regime in the pattern page's instrument is deliberately described, not claimed ("a hybrid camera, contraction on a single axis") — the seam behavior of that edge awaits a check against Chapter 3. The reader signaled an analogy thread gathering ("this is bringing a whole bunch of stuff into play, by analogy") and chose to pin vocabulary first; the thread stands open. And two seasoning items now sit side by side — datum-both-ways and seam-as-zero-defect — each waiting its walking-past.
