The Gauge

Same count, three seats; one count, two placements — the sketch sheet's remaining panels, drawn live

The 11 September sketch sheet held five panels. Its area identity is already an instrument — the conservation — and its smear-pile-grade grid lives on there as the ledger's policy buttons. This page takes what remains: the three fans marked gauge, same count, and the two elevations of the glide vanishing axis, where one count is run on two placements at once. The vocabulary is the standing one — the shelf of terms governs — and the sheet's colours are repainted by the same discipline as before: the yellow of the defect and the blue of the limit both go to crimson, the colour the suite reserves for the account and for infinity's address; the harmonic marks go to teal with the suite's rulers; the arithmetic side keeps the sheet's own green.

Same count, three seats

The first panel makes the defect page's decomposition visible with almost nothing drawn. The horizontal lines are the count — levels 0 through 4, equally spaced, untouched from fan to fan. The feet on the base are the count again, written once at unit scale. Everything a fan can differ in is one point: the seat. Put the seat above the band and the rays write the count smaller on every higher level, more gently the higher the seat sits — the sheet's Type A and Type B are this one construction at two heights, not two kinds of thing. Put the seat inside the band and the fan crosses through it: below the seat's level the count reads forward, at the seat's level the write collapses to a point, above it the count comes out backwards — Type C, the count inverting through the seat. Drag the seat anywhere and watch the type follow the address.

The count survives every level — the marks stay equally spaced wherever the seat sits; only their scale changes, by s = 1 − y/v exactly (y the level's height, v the seat's). Scale is placement, so the whole panel is the defect page's sentence drawn: the gauge decomposes into a count nobody touches and a placement one point decides. Marks turn crimson where the write inverts. The Type C crossing is left unidentified with any rig object — the miniature's lesson stands: a crossing is not the seam until proven so.

The construction

The two lower panels are one construction seen twice, and it answers a question the defect page leaves standing. Theorem 0 says no single ruler carries both infinities: Reach keeps its equal steps and exiles the end; Turn seats the end and pays for it in every interval. The sheet's construction does not defy the theorem — it routes around it with two lines. Along the receding fence, the count keeps Reach's placement: equal hops, green, the end open. On the glide vanishing axis, the same count arrives in Turn's placement: teal marks crowding against a crimson ring, the fence's unreachable direction given a finite address. The seat's fan is the bridge — a projection carrying each numbered post to its numbered mark. No line does both jobs; the axis is where the transfer is legal.

And the transfer is not merely Turn-like — it is Turn exactly, and the check says why the word harmonic belongs to it: at every interior tick, the neighbours, the tick, and the ring stand in harmonic position, cross-ratio −1, the tick the harmonic conjugate of infinity's mark against its own neighbours. Equal steps have their midpoints; a projection preserves cross-ratio; a midpoint with infinity makes a harmonic four. The crowding is that classical fact, watched happening. The crimson wash between the last mark and the ring is the account, banked — the same bill the conservation's ledger weighs, here graded toward the boundary — while on the fence it is exiled past the arrow, the pile-at-infinity policy. One count, one bill, two addresses.

Slide the stop: every post the fence adds, the axis pays for — the marks crowd, the wash narrows, and the bank never empties at any finite stop. The control is the honest part of the drawing: the sheet's fence is sketched horizontal, but a fence parallel to the axis transfers with no crowding at all — equal in, equal out, no ring, no gauge. Recession is what the transfer prices; the hand's horizontal is read as shorthand for it. Crimson ring: the sheet's blue limit, repainted — infinity's finite address. Blue is spent nowhere else; the suite has no seat for it.

Terms this page adds

Three usages enter with these panels, all provisional until they earn a ruling on the shelf. The transfer fan — the projection carrying one placement of a count to another; candidate for the shelf if it recurs. The ring — the limit's mark on the axis, already drawn (crimson, closing over the seat) in the 3D defect plate; this page uses it the same way. And Type A, B, C — kept as the sheet's own labels, though the first instrument argues the list is really two entries long: A and B are one type at two heights, and the honest classification is seat outside the band against seat inside it. The words limit and crowding are already on the shelf, tagged to this sheet.
