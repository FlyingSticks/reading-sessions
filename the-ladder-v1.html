The ladder

A mirror on every rung, a half-turn on the rung above. 2 November 2026.

On every rung of the ladder of dimensions, a mirror is the pairing of a fold with two readings. The two readings are congruent — every distance and angle agrees — and from inside that rung they cannot be identified: there is no path from one to the other. Climb one rung and a path appears: the mirror below is the shadow of a half-turn above. The half-turn carries one reading onto the other and the two become one. That is the gain. The loss is the sign: once identified, the readings can no longer be told apart. Rung 3 is where we live, and there the physical world has kept the sign.

3. Where the paradoxes are

There is no contradiction in the ladder; a fourth dimension is as consistent as a third. The paradoxes sit at the border: truths of one rung that stop being true one rung up.

Incongruent counterparts

A left hand and a right hand agree in every measurement and yet no movement in space makes one into the other. Kant took this to show that space is more than the relations among the things in it: nothing inside the hand says which hand it is. One rung up the distinction is gone. Handedness is a property of an object together with the dimension it is confined to, not of the object alone.

The flip from inside

A flat figure on a Möbius strip can travel around the strip and return as its own mirror image without leaving the surface: the rung-2 flip performed from inside, by a surface that is not orientable. The rung-3 version is a non-orientable space, in which a long enough trip would bring a traveller home with heart on the right. Nothing in physics rules this out for the cosmos as a whole, and no local measurement can decide it.

Chemistry has drawn the line

Many molecules come in left and right forms, identical in every measurement. Living things are built from one hand only; the mirror form of a drug can be inert or harmful. Nature cannot perform the rung-3 flip either, so the two forms are different substances. One rung up they would be one substance.

The mirror is not a symmetry of physics

Until 1956 it was assumed that the laws of nature read the same in a mirror, so that handedness was a convention like the direction of an axis. Then the weak interaction was found to distinguish left from right: certain decays send their electrons preferentially one way. The world is not even-handed. Rung 3 is not merely unavailable to us; the universe itself can tell which side of the mirror plane it is on. The sign is kept by the laws.

4. The thread back to the fold

Every fold with two readings has a pairing that undoes itself, and a mirror is such a pairing on every rung. From inside the rung the two readings are congruent and cannot be identified; that is what makes the choice between them a sign and not a label. Going up a rung supplies the half-turn that identifies them — and in the same stroke loses the sign, because a sign is exactly what the identification forgets. The square and its two roots are rung 1 seen in the plane of roots: the half-turn about 0 is the pairing, and squaring is the climb that loses it.

Glossary

Drawing words and mathematical words are kept apart.

rung
A dimension, numbered by the space the mirror acts in: rung 1 the line, rung 2 the plane, rung 3 space. The rung above is the space one dimension larger, in which the rung's mirror becomes a half-turn.
mirror (reflection)
On rung n, the map that sends each point to its partner across a fixed set of dimension n − 1: a point on the line, a line in the plane, a plane in space. Undoes itself. Reverses handedness.
half-turn
A rotation by 180° about an axis set. On the rung above, the half-turn about the mirror's fixed set carries each point onto its partner; its shadow on the rung below is the mirror.
shadow
What a map of the higher space does to the lower space sitting inside it, read off after the fact: where points of the lower space end up, ignoring the path. The shadow of the half-turn is the mirror.
reading
One member of each pair under a self-undoing pairing. A fold has two readings; here they are a figure and its mirror image.
congruent
Agreeing in every distance and angle. A figure and its mirror image are always congruent.
identify
To treat two things as one. The half-turn identifies the two readings by landing one on the other. Identification is the fold itself, seen from above.
sign
The choice between a point and its partner under a self-undoing pairing that prefers neither. Kept while the readings are distinct; lost when they are identified.
handedness (chirality)
The property a figure has when it is not congruent to its mirror image by any movement within its own rung. A clockwise arrow on rung 2, a hand on rung 3.
incongruent counterparts
Kant's name for a figure and its mirror image: congruent in all measurements, not interchangeable by movement.
orientable
A space is orientable when no closed trip inside it brings a figure home as its mirror image. The plane and ordinary space are orientable; the Möbius strip is not. A non-orientable space performs its own rung's flip from inside.
parity
Physics' word for the mirror of space. Parity is conserved when the laws read the same in a mirror. The weak interaction violates it.
stroke with a dot, hand-shaped figure (drawing words)
The test figures on rungs 2 and 3: the simplest shapes with a handedness, so that a mirror image can be told from a turned copy.
arc (drawing word)
The dashed path of one point under the half-turn, standing for the travel of all of them. Real travel on rungs 1 and 2; on rung 3 drawn dotted as a question, since there is nothing for it to travel through.
