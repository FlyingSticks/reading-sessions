One debt, five coins

A cheat sheet for reading the comparison plate R·T-04 — the accounting words, what they mean, and what they translate to

The plate has one fact and many ways of showing it. The accounting words keep the direction of the argument visible; the Erlangen words say where each showing lives. Both are on this sheet, with the translation between them.

1 · The four words

debt
The one fact the plate is about, stated in the coin that cannot be measured: an incidence relation. On R·T-04 it is (O, R ; V_D, V_D′) = −1, the four vanishing points on the horizon in harmonic position. Fixed by the rig; untouched by every handle.
coin
A measurable or drawable quantity in which the debt can be shown — area, altitude, length, a reciprocal sum, or incidence itself. Each coin has a bank (the moves that trade in it) and each bank has a hypothesis it needs true before it trades. Coins never exchange directly; every conversion passes through the debt.
price
The equation rendering the debt in one coin. An equivalence, true if and only if the debt is — so a certificate as much as a rendering.
receipt
A price drawn at one setting with its numbers showing: what the ledger prints and the check file audits. Evidence about one configuration, not a proof of the price. Refused when the bank's hypothesis fails; the ledger then writes parked.

3 · The tilt as sorter

The tilt changes nothing about the debt. It changes which banks are open, by testing each bank's hypothesis. The four strata of the plate are the four hypotheses; parked versus standing is whether the hypothesis holds at this setting.

Fig. 3 — The sieve. Rail-borne claims are the only ones the tilt closes, because parallels are the only hypothesis it breaks.

4 · Translation: accounting → Erlangen

The accounting words are borrowed; underneath them is Klein's program, your four-rung ladder. A coin is a geometry in Klein's sense — the quantities a group preserves. The hypothesis a bank needs is the structure its group presupposes.

5 · The three means

Three of the coins are the three means. The count bank is additive, A + B = C — the arithmetic mean's register. The circle bank is the geometric mean, ab = H². The reciprocal register is the harmonic mean, c = HM(OV_D, OV_D′). The −1 is what makes the three ledgers agree; the means sphere states the same agreement as AM·HM = GM².

Fig. 5 — Three coins, three means, one agreement.

6 · Where the metaphor strains

"Paying" implies the debt comes first and the receipts after. Geometrically each price is an if-and-only-if, so any of them could be taken as the starting fact; the order debt → price is expository. The strata are hypotheses, not costs. Keep the coins for reading the sheet; translate to the ladder for anything stated as a result, where the words have owners.

7 · One-line reminders

  • One debt, several coins, one price per coin, many receipts per price.
  • Coins never exchange directly; incidence is the reserve currency.
  • A bank trades only while its hypothesis holds; the tilt tests hypotheses, not debts.
  • Rail-borne claims are the only ones the tilt closes, because parallels are the only thing it breaks.
  • The picture-plane stratum is the machine's own rung: Euclidean plus a vertical.
  • Area, altitude, reciprocal = arithmetic, geometric, harmonic; the −1 keeps them in agreement.
