Pinhole, Double Slit and the Number Four

A line in space takes four numbers. A camera fixes two of them in advance and leaves two free. In the two-card scheme used on this plate, the pinhole and the double slit differ in which two they fix.

Addendum · the word congruence

Two meanings of congruence

First meaning, the book's: a congruence is a two-number family of lines in space. Second meaning: a congruence is a distance-preserving map, the kind that carries a figure onto a congruent figure. Panels 4, 5 and 6 show how the two are tied.

Both cameras are congruences, of different kinds. Through a general point each has exactly one ray. For the pinhole, "general" leaves out only C. For the double slit it leaves out every point of card 1 and card 2: a point of a slit lies on many rays, and any other point of those two cards lies on none. A general plane contains no ray of the pinhole, because it misses C, and exactly one ray of the double slit: the plane meets each slit once, and the ray joins the two meeting points. Here "general" leaves out the planes parallel to a slit and the planes containing one. The double slit's congruence is the one in the title of One Congruence, Two Readings; the two slits are its focal lines.

Status. Panels 4, 5 and 6 state classical facts. A numerical check (check-congruence.py, rewritten after the audit, 20/20) tests on random samples: that one ray passes through a point off the cards; that the pairing undoes itself and carries lines to lines; that rays are carried onto themselves and other lines are not; that a plane parallel to neither slit holds one ray; that the pairing tends to the half-turn as a level slit b recedes; and that with slit b receding obliquely A remains the midpoint while distance is not preserved. A numerical check is evidence on the cases sampled, not a proof. One related claim does not hold: that the seam pairing of Chapter 3, VM · VI = VP², is this map restricted to a line. It was tested on 12 November (check-the-two-slit-involution.js) and struck.

Audit. Audited outside on 19 November 2026 and amended. The audit found three things of substance: that "AP′ = AP" does not by itself make the pairing preserve distance, since the right angle is also needed; that the crossed-slit statement fails in ordinary space for lines parallel to a slit; and that two assertions of the first check script tested almost nothing. It also asked for the points and planes left out by "general" to be named.

What the plate shows

  • A point costs two numbers; a slit costs one. Requiring a line to pass through a point imposes two conditions on its four numbers. Requiring it to cross a slit imposes one. On this plate the pinhole lies on a card and each slit runs along a ruling of a card, so each condition shows up as one number held fixed.
  • Both cameras leave two. That is why each produces a two-number family of rays, enough to fill a flat picture and no more.
  • A pinhole is two slits crossed on one card. A slit parallel to ACROSS at up₂ = 0.83 and a slit parallel to UP at across₂ = 0.68, both cut in card 2, cross at C. Their common opening is the pinhole. The double slit is such a pair of cuts placed on two different cards, one cut on each. Stated for the families of lines: when the two slits cross at C, the lines meeting both fall into two pieces: the lines through C, and the lines lying in the card. One exception in ordinary space: a line in the card parallel to one of the slits never meets that slit. Count parallels as meeting at infinity and the exception goes. The pinhole is the first piece.
  • The free numbers are not the same kind in the two cameras. In the pinhole both free numbers sit on one card and amount to a direction from C. (A ray through C parallel to card 1 has a direction but no landing spot.) In the double slit one sits on each card, and the rays have no common point.
  • Through a general point of space there is exactly one ray of the pinhole, and exactly one ray of this double slit. The points left out by "general" are named in the addendum.

Conventions

  • Ray. On this plate a ray is a whole line admitted by a camera: a line through the pinhole, or a line crossing both slits. It is not a half-line. It has no endpoint and no chosen sense, and it is not a vector: it has a fixed position and no length.
  • Card 1 is the card of the previous plate, with the same zero mark, unit of length, ACROSS and UP.
  • Card 2 is parallel to card 1 and 2 units in front of it. Its zero mark O₂ lies on the line through O₁ square to both cards, and its ACROSS and UP are parallel to those of card 1.
  • The slits are straight here. A curved slit, such as a half-circle cut in a sphere, still costs one number, so the count 4 − 1 − 1 = 2 is unchanged. What changes is that the fixed number can no longer be read as a single ruling on a card, and a general point may lie on more than one ray.
  • The exception, as before. A line whose direction is parallel to the cards has no numbers in this scheme. It misses both cards, or lies in one and misses the other.
