What is drawn

Four points. P₁ and P₂ are the ends of the left red edge, fixed in the floor. P₃ and P₄ are the ends of the right red edge, which the dial twists about the x-axis through the rectangle's centre. The blue pair P₁P₃, P₂P₄ are the diagonals of your sketch; the gold pair P₁P₄, P₂P₃ are the dotted lines you drew beside them. With the two red edges that makes the three pairings of four points into two lines each — the three opposite-edge pairs of a tetrahedron, or the three diagonal points of a quadrangle, depending on the fork.

The eye E sits above the picture plane; its footprint is the black ring in the plan, its height the slider. From E there is exactly one line meeting both members of a pair (the meet of the two planes E spans with them). Those are the three transversals. Each is drawn in the plan from the eye's footprint through the two points where it strikes its pair, and its trace on the picture plane is drawn as a ring in the picture.

The fork

  • theorem One number. With the vertex order fixed as above, det = 8ab²·sin θ. Zero means the four points are coplanar: the complete quadrangle. Nonzero means a tetrahedron. Its sign is the handedness — which blue line passes over the other at the purple point (ledger row 3, checked against the gap's own sign). Its magnitude is six times the tetrahedron's volume, and divides by |d₁×d₂| to give the common perpendicular. Incidence, sign and measure, read off one determinant. The sign is an orientation convention: reverse the vertex order and it flips; what is invariant is that it flips with the crossing.
  • theorem Flat at 0° and 180°, and only there. Your "180° twist → uncross" is the second zero of sin θ. At 180° the blue pair no longer crosses in the plan and the gold pair does: the twist has swapped the roles of the two non-red pairs while fixing red. On the three pairings that is a transposition, (blue gold), in S₃.
  • theorem Fertile versus sterile. When det = 0, the three opposite pairs meet and the net grows — your emerging 2D net. When det ≠ 0 no pair meets; four points, six lines, four planes, closed under join and meet. The figure needs a fifth point, the eye, before anything new appears.
  • theorem The traces are the diagonal points. The transversal to a pair lies in both planes E spans with that pair; those planes cut the picture in the two image lines; so the trace is their meet. Each crossing in the picture is the point view of a cross-slit ray, and one tetrahedron carries three cross-slit rigs — the three pairings, the three transversals. The ledger's residual row is this theorem measured live.
  • theorem The picture cannot see the twist. The image is a planar quadrangle at every θ, so its diagonal lines are cut harmonically, (Di,Dj; X,Y) = −1, flat or twisted. The harmonic −1 lives in the picture and knows nothing of the determinant. That is the flat plane projective assumption of your sheet stated as a limitation: incidence in the picture is incidence through the eye, and the sign the picture can state is not the handedness.
  • rhyme Which sign is "the sign". Whether this handedness is the same sign that Measure and Sign adds to the projective plane is untested. The previous row says the picture's −1 is blind to it, which is evidence against the simplest identification. Test it before using it.

Repairs to the sheet

  • "3 opposing skew edges" → three opposing pairs of skew edges.
  • Flat means all three pairs meet, not only BE and CD. In the plan, with the eye at infinity, your flat rectangle shows one finite meet (purple) and two at infinity (the parallel red and gold pairs); the picture with a finite eye brings the twisted ones home.
  • det = 0 is weaker than the twist defect T = A−B+C−D = 0. A flat trapezoid has det = 0 and T ≠ 0 (check 11). Flat is coplanar; parallelogram is flat with equal halves.
