Compose, Multiply, Sign

Five diagrams, one per claim, on how the sign of a congruence works

Terms as on the One Generator page: a congruence is a distance-preserving map of the plane; a mirror is reflection in a line; the probe figure is an L, chosen because it has a hand. One new term here: to compose maps A and B means to do A, then do B; the result is written BA, read right to left in the order applied. The word multiply is used on this page only for numbers.

1. Mirrors don't multiply; they compose

Composing two maps means doing one, then the other. The result is a single map. Order matters, which is the first sign that this is not arithmetic.

2. The signs multiply

Attach the number −1 to every mirror. The sign of a composition is the product of the numbers attached to its mirrors. The maps compose; the numbers multiply.

3. Why this is not circular, and where the content is

The same congruence can be composed from mirrors in many ways. The sign is well defined only because every way uses the same parity of mirrors. The independent witness is the determinant, which depends on the map alone.

4. "Homomorphism" means this

The sign respects composition: sign(BA) = sign(B) · sign(A). Compose maps on one side, multiply numbers on the other, and the two bookkeepings agree.

5. Discrete, and nothing between

The sign takes one of two values. Along any continuous path of congruences it cannot change, because there is no continuous route from +1 to −1 inside a two-element set. The only way to get from a figure to its mirror image continuously is to pass through maps that are not congruences.

Summary: congruences compose; signs multiply; the sign of a composition is the product of the signs; the sign is a property of the map and not of its recipe; and because a sign is one of two numbers, it cannot drift.
