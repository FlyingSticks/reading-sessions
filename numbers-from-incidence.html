Numbers from Incidence

The plate Beneath the Involution (beneath-the-involution.html) built the harmonic partner with joins and meets and no ruler. This plate repeats one construction of that kind and puts a number on point after point of a line, still with no ruler. Three points must be given first, and they are given names: 0, 1 and ∞. Index: the primer (the-primer.html).

What is given, and what the names mean

  • A line ℓ and three different points on it: O, E and V.
  • Three names: O is named 0, E is named 1, V is named ∞. A name is a label. It is not a measurement, and nothing on this plate is measured to make the marks.
  • On this plate the sign ∞ is only the name of the point V. V may be drawn on the page like any other point. V is a point at infinity of the drawing, in the sense of the plate Level, and Square to the Card, only in the one limiting case where it has no finite place on the line and all lines through it are drawn parallel to ℓ. A point that is merely beyond the edge of the page is not at infinity.
  • Two helpers, chosen freely: a point U that is not on ℓ, and a line k through V that is not ℓ and does not pass through U.

Reading a number back

Write OX for the drawn distance from O to a mark X, counted as positive to the right of O and negative to the left, and write v for the drawn distance from O to V, both in units. Then

number of X = OX · (v − 1) / (v − OX)

When V is at infinity there is no distance v. The fraction (v − 1) / (v − OX) is then replaced by 1, the value it approaches as V is drawn further and further away, and the number of X is simply OX. The formula is the cross-ratio (X, E; O, V), written with the same convention as the −1 of the earlier plate: (A, B; C, D) = (AC · BD) / (AD · BC), each pair of letters standing for a signed stretch.

Turned round, the formula says where mark n is drawn:

drawn distance of mark n = n · v / (v − 1 + n)

If n = 1 − v is a whole number, the bottom of that fraction is 0 for that mark: the mark is at infinity.

Two things follow. First, every mark is tied to its neighbours by the harmonic relation of the earlier plate: (n − 1, n + 1; n, V) = −1, so mark n + 1 is the harmonic partner of mark n − 1 with respect to mark n and V. The whole scale could be built with that one construction. Second, take the signed distance from each mark to V, and then one over it: these change by equal steps from each mark to the next.

What is claimed

What is claimedStanding
7.1 Given O, E and V on a line, the step of figure 1 produces one further point, and it is the same point whatever U and k are chosen.a classical theorem resting on Desargues' theorem; stated, not demonstrated; in the check for four choices of U and k
7.2 Each mark is the harmonic partner of the mark two before it, with respect to the mark between them and V: (n − 1, n + 1; n, V) = −1.classical; in the check
7.3 The number of a mark is the cross-ratio (X, E; O, V) of drawn distances. A perspective view of the whole figure changes the drawn distances and leaves every number alone.classical; in the check, for one view
7.4 Mark n is drawn at distance n · v / (v − 1 + n) from O. With V at infinity the steps are equal. With V at a finite place, one over the signed distance from a mark to V changes by equal steps from each mark to the next.classical; in the check
7.5 The construction of figure 4 gives ½, the harmonic partner of V with respect to 0 and 1. Backward steps give −1, −2 and −3.classical; in the check
7.6 Every fraction, positive, negative or 0, is the number of a point reached by finitely many joins and meets from O, E and V, and no other number is. "Reached" here means that the point comes out the same whatever free helpers are chosen along the way.classical (the net of rationality); stated, not demonstrated, not in the check beyond the whole numbers, ½ and 1½
7.7 The sum and the product of two numbers on the line can each be constructed by joins and meets.classical (von Staudt); stated only: not drawn, not in the check

On 7.6: numbers that are not fractions, such as the square root of 2, are never reached by finitely many joins and meets. Matching them with points of the line needs two further assumptions, neither of which is an incidence statement: that the points of the line have an order, and that this order has no gaps. With both, such a number is matched with a point as the cut between the fractions below it and the fractions above it. Neither assumption is used on this plate.

Toward the book

These pointers were audited on 21 November 2026 against the book's glossary entries, which were quoted to the auditor word for word, and were rewritten afterwards. The auditor did not read the chapters. The chapter numbers are taken from the tags on the glossary entries.

  • Two graduations. The book's entries Depth gauge, Reach and Turn, all of Chapter 2, contrast two graduations of a depth line. Reach has equal steps and keeps the plane at infinity at ∞. Turn sends the plane at infinity to the finite mark 1 and a station to ∞, so that depth steps k become the harmonic progression k/(k + 1). Compare figure 3: its first ruler spaces the marks 0, 1, 2 and onward equally, and its other two space them in a harmonic progression that crowds toward V. There is one difference. Figure 3 keeps the whole-number names on the crowded marks. Turn gives the crowded places new numbers, k/(k + 1).
  • The same progression. The progression k/(k + 1) of the Turn entry matches the drawn places of this plate's marks 0, 1, 2 and onward when V is drawn at twice the distance of the mark named 1, and distances are measured with the stretch O V as the unit: mark n is then drawn at n/(n + 1). On that reading V is the book's plane at infinity, at 1, and the mark named −1 is at infinity, which is where the Turn entry puts the station. This is in the check. It settles the question the earlier wording of this plate left open.
  • Three pins. The book's Pin entry says that three distinguished points fix the three freedoms of a coordinate on a line: "Three pins make a ruler." This plate's three given points O, E and V play the role of three pins. They must be given before a scale of numbers is fixed on the line. From them every fraction follows by joins and meets, and the number of any point of the line is read back as the cross-ratio (X, E; O, V).
  • The point named ∞. Which of the book's points this plate's V stands for depends on how the names of the marks are read. If the names are read as Reach's steps, V is the book's Plane at infinity in all three rulers of figure 3: at infinity in the first, and at a finite place in the other two. If the names are read as Turn's numbers, the point named ∞ is the book's Station, and the plane at infinity is the point named 1. On this plate V is that kind of point only when it is drawn off the page. Struck: the audit found it wrong.
  • A caution about the sign ∞. In the book's Turn and Depth gauge entries the sign ∞ stands in some places for the plane at infinity and in others for the value ∞. On this plate ∞ is only ever a name, the name of V.
  • Not claimed. Nothing here is said about the book's law, its rates, or which graduation belongs to which reading. A page on the reading-sessions shelf, the-gauge.html, reports the same −1 at every tick for the 1984 transfer construction; that page was not re-run for this plate.

Audit. Audited outside on 21 November 2026, in two passes, and amended. The first pass found the mathematics sound. It found a fault in figure 2's drawing code, by which the line U V could vanish or stop short of U and W at some slider settings; the numeral 2 of figure 1 set far from the points O′ and E′ that it marks; the line E O′ of figure 4 called a slant and drawn as one; that claim 7.4 needed "signed distance" and could not say the steps always "go up"; and that claim 7.6 needed 0 and the condition about free helpers. The second pass ruled four statements imprecise: "drawn off the page" said of a point at infinity; "one more assumption" where two are needed for numbers that are not fractions; "Möbius net" said of a net on a line; and the vanishing line credited to the net when it belongs to the helper U. Of the three pointers to the book, it found two to overreach and one wrong. All three are rewritten, the wrong sentence is shown struck, and two pointers that the auditor allowed in weaker wording were added ("The same progression" and "Three pins").

Status. Classical. Every point in the four figures is computed by the drawing code from joins and meets, not typed in; the last column of the table under figure 2 is computed from the drawn distances. A separate script, check-numbers-from-incidence.py, repeats the construction in exact arithmetic on a line in general position, with V at infinity and at three finite places on the line, and confirms claims 7.1 to 7.5 and the pointer "The same progression"; it passes 42 of 42. Claims 7.6 and 7.7 are quoted from the classical theory and are not checked. Built 19 November 2026; amended 21 November 2026 after the audit. Viewed in Chrome, light and dark themes; not in Safari.

Terms

The plain words are the plate's own. The standard name, for looking things up elsewhere, follows each in grey.

ℓ
The line that receives the marks. Standard: the base line of the scale.
O, E, V
The three given points of ℓ. Letters are for points of the drawing. Standard: the fundamental points of the scale.
named 0, 1, ∞
The names given to O, E and V. Numerals are for names. Standard: the coordinates 0, 1 and ∞ of the fundamental points.
U, k
The helper point and the helper line, chosen freely subject to the conditions in the first box. Standard: auxiliary elements of the construction.
rung
A line through U. Standard: a line of the pencil with vertex U.
slant
A line through W. Standard: a line of the pencil with vertex W.
W
The point where the first slant, O E′, meets the line U V. Standard: with U and V, a point of the vanishing line of the helper grid of rungs and slants; in projective terms, the line of centres of the two perspectivities ℓ → k → ℓ. That line depends on the free choice of U. It does not belong to the marks, which stay where they are when U is moved.
O′, E′, 2′
The points where the rungs through O, E and 2 meet k.
step
The five joins and meets of figure 1, started from any two neighbouring marks.
mark
A point of ℓ produced by steps. Standard: a point of the harmonic net, or net of rationality, on a line.
number of a mark
The name the construction gives it. Standard: its projective coordinate with respect to O, E and V.
unit
The drawn stretch from 0 to 1, used only for reading the drawing with a ruler.
OX, v
Drawn distances from O, in units, positive to the right: OX to a mark X, v to V. Standard: signed, or directed, lengths.
read back
To compute the number of a mark from drawn distances. Standard: to evaluate the cross-ratio (X, E; O, V).
scale
The line together with all its marks and their numbers. Standard: a projective scale.
equal steps
Marks drawn the same distance apart; this happens when V is at infinity. Standard: an arithmetic progression; an affine scale.
crowd toward V
Said of the marks when V is at a finite place. Standard: the distances from V form a harmonic progression.
fraction
A whole number divided by a whole number that is not 0. Standard: a rational number.
perspective view
As on the earlier plate: the figure seen from a point and redrawn on another plane. Standard: a central projection; more generally a projective map.
