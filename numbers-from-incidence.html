Plate 7 · bridge to Chapter 2

Numbers from Incidence

The plate Beneath the Involution (beneath-the-involution.html) built the harmonic partner with joins and meets and no ruler. This plate repeats one construction of that kind and puts a number on point after point of a line, still with no ruler. Three points must be given first, and they are given names: 0, 1 and ∞. Index: the primer (the-primer.html).

What is given, and what the names mean

  • A line ℓ and three different points on it: O, E and V.
  • Three names: O is named 0, E is named 1, V is named ∞. A name is a label. It is not a measurement, and nothing on this plate is measured to make the marks.
  • On this plate the sign ∞ is only the name of the point V. V may be drawn on the page like any other point. V is a point at infinity, in the sense of the plate Level, and Square to the Card, only in the one case where it is drawn off the page and lines through it are drawn parallel.
  • Two helpers, chosen freely: a point U that is not on ℓ, and a line k through V that is not ℓ and does not pass through U.

Reading a number back

Write OX for the drawn distance from O to a mark X, counted as positive to the right of O and negative to the left, and write v for the drawn distance from O to V, both in units. Then

number of X = OX · (v − 1) / (v − OX)

When V is off the page the fraction (v − 1) / (v − OX) is 1, and the number of X is simply OX. The formula is the cross-ratio (X, E; O, V), written with the same convention as the −1 of the earlier plate: (A, B; C, D) = (AC · BD) / (AD · BC), each pair of letters standing for a signed stretch.

Turned round, the formula says where mark n is drawn:

drawn distance of mark n = n · v / (v − 1 + n)

Two things follow. First, every mark is tied to its neighbours by the harmonic relation of the earlier plate: (n − 1, n + 1; n, V) = −1, so mark n + 1 is the harmonic partner of mark n − 1 with respect to mark n and V. The whole scale could be built with that one construction. Second, the distances of the marks from V, taken as one over each distance, go up in equal steps.

What is claimed

What is claimedStanding
7.1 Given O, E and V on a line, the step of figure 1 produces one further point, and it is the same point whatever U and k are chosen.a classical theorem resting on Desargues' theorem; stated, not demonstrated; in the check for four choices of U and k
7.2 Each mark is the harmonic partner of the mark two before it, with respect to the mark between them and V: (n − 1, n + 1; n, V) = −1.classical; in the check
7.3 The number of a mark is the cross-ratio (X, E; O, V) of drawn distances. A perspective view of the whole figure changes the drawn distances and leaves every number alone.classical; in the check, for one view
7.4 Mark n is drawn at distance n · v / (v − 1 + n) from O. With V off the page the steps are equal. With V on the page, one over the distance from V goes up in equal steps.classical; in the check
7.5 The construction of figure 4 gives ½, the harmonic partner of V with respect to 0 and 1. Backward steps give −1, −2 and −3.classical; in the check
7.6 Every fraction, positive or negative, is reached by finitely many joins and meets from O, E and V, and no other number is.classical (the net of rationality); stated, not demonstrated, not in the check beyond the whole numbers, ½ and 1½
7.7 The sum and the product of two numbers on the line can each be constructed by joins and meets.classical (von Staudt); stated only: not drawn, not in the check

On 7.6: numbers that are not fractions, such as the square root of 2, need one more assumption about the line, that it has no gaps. That assumption is not an incidence statement and is not used on this plate.

Toward the book

The book's site could not be reached from the session that built this plate. The pointers below were written from the primer's index and concordance and from earlier session records, not from the book's pages, and none of them has been checked against Chapter 2.

  • Two graduations. Chapter 2, Law and gauge, has two ways of graduating a depth line, which the book calls Reach (equal steps) and Turn (harmonic steps). Compare figure 3: its first ruler has equal steps and its other two have harmonic steps. This plate's statement is that the three rulers carry the same numbers and differ only in where V is drawn. Whether the book's Turn is this scale with V at a particular point of the book's rig has not been worked out here.
  • Three named points. The plate The Six on Three Dots read the book's 0, 1 and ∞ as the picture plane, the station and the plane at infinity, from the entry Degenerate orbit. That reading is unaudited. If it holds, a depth line of the book is a line with three named points, and this plate says how every other number on it follows from those three.
  • The point named ∞. Compare the book's Plane at infinity, which the concordance records as defining a point of a line. On this plate V is that kind of point only when it is drawn off the page.
  • Not claimed. Nothing here is said about the book's law, its rates, or which graduation belongs to which reading. A page on the reading-sessions shelf, the-gauge.html, reports the same −1 at every tick for the 1984 transfer construction; that page was not re-run for this plate.

This is the last plate planned for the primer. Back to the primer (the-primer.html).

Terms

The plain words are the plate's own. The standard name, for looking things up elsewhere, follows each in grey.

ℓ
The line that receives the marks. Standard: the base line of the scale.
O, E, V
The three given points of ℓ. Letters are for points of the drawing. Standard: the fundamental points of the scale.
named 0, 1, ∞
The names given to O, E and V. Numerals are for names. Standard: the coordinates 0, 1 and ∞ of the fundamental points.
U, k
The helper point and the helper line, chosen freely subject to the conditions in the first box. Standard: auxiliary elements of the construction.
rung
A line through U. Standard: a line of the pencil with vertex U.
slant
A line through W. Standard: a line of the pencil with vertex W.
W
The point where the first slant, O E′, meets the line U V. Standard: with U and V, a point of the vanishing line of the net.
O′, E′, 2′
The points where the rungs through O, E and 2 meet k.
step
The five joins and meets of figure 1, started from any two neighbouring marks.
mark
A point of ℓ produced by steps. Standard: a point of the harmonic net, or net of rationality, also called a Möbius net.
number of a mark
The name the construction gives it. Standard: its projective coordinate with respect to O, E and V.
unit
The drawn stretch from 0 to 1, used only for reading the drawing with a ruler.
OX, v
Drawn distances from O, in units, positive to the right: OX to a mark X, v to V. Standard: signed, or directed, lengths.
read back
To compute the number of a mark from drawn distances. Standard: to evaluate the cross-ratio (X, E; O, V).
scale
The line together with all its marks and their numbers. Standard: a projective scale.
equal steps
Marks drawn the same distance apart; this happens when V is off the page. Standard: an arithmetic progression; an affine scale.
crowd toward V
Said of the marks when V is on the page. Standard: the distances from V form a harmonic progression.
fraction
A whole number divided by a whole number that is not 0. Standard: a rational number.
perspective view
As on the earlier plate: the figure seen from a point and redrawn on another plane. Standard: a central projection; more generally a projective map.
