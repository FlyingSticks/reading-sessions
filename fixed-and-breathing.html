The fixed square and the breathing square

Drag Q. Its harmonic conjugate S follows, and the two Newton relations ride along as Euclid I.43 — equal complements about a diagonal.

The circle on A–C — center O.  — the fixed square.

The circle on Q–S — center M.  — the breathing square.

Definitions.

Newton product — from the midpoint of one pair, the product of the distances to the two points of the other pair: OQ·OS, measured from O, the midpoint of A–C; MA·MC, measured from M, the midpoint of Q–S. Newton’s relation: the pairs are harmonic — cross-ratio (A,C; Q,S) = −1 — exactly when each Newton product equals the square on its own half-span: OQ·OS = OA² and MA·MC = MQ². Either equation alone carries the whole harmonic condition.

The fixed square — OA² = 3² = 9, the square on the half-span of A–C. A and C never move, so neither does it.

The breathing square — MQ², the square on the half-span of Q–S. As Q slides, S and M follow and the half-span widens and narrows — the square breathes — with MA·MC keeping exact step at every position.
