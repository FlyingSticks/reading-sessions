the glide seats — an architectural construction

The glide construction of the slab, carried one step further into drawing practice: its depth-line fans have seats — the book's word for the point a fan's contraction aims at and where its lines concur (the working sketch marked them VP). Not one seat: the glide's seats lie in scale along the traces — every depth trail, extended backward through the red rectangle, crosses the v trace at λ = u and the u trace at λ = v; lines sharing a height share a seat, lines sharing a width another. The slab's four walls pick out four, and each corner connector threads two of them. Fused, all four slide onto the origin — where the cross-slit keeps its seat welded to its vanishing point, and which is kept as a toggle for comparison. A channel machinery layer runs the parent plate's u-channel construction at the gauge depth — camera pulling in, glide pushing out from one foot, ladder harmonic in and arithmetic out — and its marks Xc · q · Xg land exactly where the three frames cut the trace, Xg·Xc = q². An architectural construction

  
The sketch this plate transcribes is a working drawing: the glide construction of the slab
  with its depth lines ruled in and their concurrences marked — the sketch labels them VP, and
  this page renames them with the book's word, seats. It is derived from the
  four-quadrant plate's depth trails — nothing new is assumed; the trails are simply extended
  backward through the red rectangle until they concur. Read against the book, the whole
  construction is Chapter 7's Type A transfer — marks carried along a fibration, every
  depth station receiving a true copy, the collector its seat — rediscovered from the
  four-quadrant side.


  
Where the glide seats

  
The glide print of the depth line through (X, Y) is
  λ ↦ (X·(1+λ·r_u), Y·(1+λ·r_v)). At λ = u the first
  coordinate is zero whatever X is — so every depth line at height Y passes through the
  point (0, Y·(v−u)/v) on the v trace. Dually, at λ = v
  every depth line of width X passes through (X·(u−v)/u, 0) on the u
  trace. These concurrences are seats in the book's sense — the armature law's
  per-channel contraction aims at them, and each wall's fan concurs at its own. The book keeps
  seat apart from station: the seat is law, where the fan gathers; the station is gauge, the
  point a Turn graduation sends to infinity. These trails run the Reach register, so they have
  seats without a station — nothing here is an image of infinite depth, the chair stays vacant,
  and the family stays open. The cross-slit is the welded case: its
  seat and its vanishing point coincide at the origin, one point for all of depth — and its
  bowing connectors are the book's chains, images of lines skew to both slits, hyperbolas
  with axis-parallel asymptotes, met here as the prints of the slab's depth edges. The
  glide's seats lie in scale along the traces: one per height and one
  per width, in proportion — the slab's four walls select four, and each corner connector,
  belonging to a row and a column at once, threads its two seats on its way out. The two ratios
  (u−v)/u and (v−u)/v carry opposite signs
  whenever the feet differ, so exactly one pair of fans crosses the axis on the way to its
  seats — and both ratios die together at u = v, when all four seats slide onto the
  origin and the glide's fans and the cross-slit's star become one figure: perspective.


  
The channel machinery — where the relation lives

  
The relation between the two constructions is channelwise, and it is the parent plate's
  play verbatim. In the u-plane, the machinery layer runs it at the gauge depth: one depth line
  at height q; the camera line from the foot through the sample point,
  pulling its mark in to Xc = q·m_u; the
  glide stroke from the same foot through the shared footprint, pushing
  out to Xg = q/m_u and carried home; the ladder marking equal depth
  steps — harmonic in, arithmetic out. The three marks stand in geometric progression with the
  corner mark q as mean proportional, Xg·Xc = q², and they are exactly
  where the heavy cross-slit frame, the red rectangle, and the heavy
  glide frame cut the u trace. The frames are nothing but these marks swept around the slab's
  four walls; move λ₀ and the machinery and the heavy frames travel together. The v channel
  runs the same play in its own plane — the frames' other pair of edges.


  
Three planes

  
Behind the drawing, the scene keeps its furniture: the picture plane at λ = 0
  carrying everything drawn; the gauge plane at λ₀, movable by slider, whose section
  square prints to the heavy frame of each family — the frames are the gauge plane's
  signatures, one per depth, which is why the sketch calls them gauge lines; and the
  back plane at λ = 1, the slab's far wall, where the solid part of every
  depth line ends and the open dashes begin.
