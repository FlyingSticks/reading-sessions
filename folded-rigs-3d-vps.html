the glide vanishing points — an architectural construction

The glide construction of the slab, carried one step further into drawing practice: its depth lines have vanishing points. Not one — a scale of them. Every glide depth trail, extended backward through the red rectangle, crosses the v trace at λ = u and the u trace at λ = v; lines sharing a height meet at one point, lines sharing a width at another. The slab's four walls pick out four VPs, and each corner connector threads two of them. Fused, all four slide onto the origin — the cross-slit's single vanishing point, kept as a toggle for comparison. An architectural construction

  
The sketch this plate transcribes is a working drawing: the glide construction of the slab
  with its depth lines ruled in and their vanishing points marked. It is derived from the
  four-quadrant plate's depth trails — nothing new is assumed; the trails are simply extended
  backward through the red rectangle until they concur.


  
Where the glide vanishes

  
The glide print of the depth line through (X, Y) is
  λ ↦ (X·(1+λ·r_u), Y·(1+λ·r_v)). At λ = u the first
  coordinate is zero whatever X is — so every depth line at height Y passes through the
  point (0, Y·(v−u)/v) on the v trace. Dually, at λ = v
  every depth line of width X passes through (X·(u−v)/u, 0) on the u
  trace. The cross-slit spends one vanishing point on all of depth —
  the origin. The glide spends a scale of them: one per height and one
  per width, laid out along the traces in proportion — a vanishing ruler on each axis rather
  than a single point. The slab's four walls select four; each corner connector, belonging to a
  row and a column at once, threads its two VPs on its way out. The two ratios
  (u−v)/u and (v−u)/v carry opposite signs
  whenever the feet differ, so exactly one pair of fans crosses the axis before vanishing — and
  both ratios die together at u = v, when all four VPs slide onto the origin and the
  glide's fans and the cross-slit's star become one figure: perspective.


  
Three planes

  
Behind the drawing, the scene keeps its furniture: the picture plane at λ = 0
  carrying everything drawn; the gauge plane at λ₀, movable by slider, whose section
  square prints to the heavy frame of each family — the frames are the gauge plane's
  signatures, one per depth, which is why the sketch calls them gauge lines; and the
  back plane at λ = 1, the slab's far wall, where the solid part of every
  depth line ends and the open dashes begin.


  
What is checked, what is a reading

  
The constructions run under check-folded-rigs-3d-vps.js — 20 numbered assertions: row and
  column concurrence at the stated points; the corner connectors threading both their VPs; the
  linearity of the vanishing scale along each trace; the algebraic identity
  1+u·r_v = (v−u)/v behind the readout; opposite crossing signs exactly when the feet
  differ; total collapse onto the origin at u = v; the equal-stride frames and
  harmonic room inherited from the slab plate; the cross-slit connectors' hyperbola relation
  and their run to the origin; the gauge-square corners' strikes; area reciprocity at the gauge
  depth; the seam meter's zero set; and containment in all three view presets. The framings —
  the VPs as "a vanishing ruler on each axis," and the construction as architectural practice —
  are readings for the seasoning shelf, not theorems.
