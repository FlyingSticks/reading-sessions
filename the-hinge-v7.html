Reading the plate

The trapezoid costs nothing. Its four sides and both diagonals are panel lines already drawn: the bottom rail O–R is the rectangle's base, the right edge R–N its side, the leg O–M is a side of triangle A's neighbor, the top M–N is the red line's own segment b — and the diagonals O–N and R–M are precisely the two long lines whose crossing X adjudicated D = D. Renaming is the only construction. (The footnote's letters T and P are N and M here.)

X's station is a theorem about ratio, not position: the diagonals of a trapezoid cut each other in the ratio of the parallel sides, OX : XN = RX : XM = c : b, so X stands at height c/(b+c) of the way up — always above the midline, leaning toward the shorter rail. Slide the division point and watch it ride: b shrinking toward 0 lifts X to the red line; b growing toward c would settle it at midheight, a limit it never reaches, since b < c is the sum panel's standing condition.

The parallel through X crosses the trapezoid in a chord of length 2bc/(b+c) — the harmonic mean of the two rails — and X bisects it. In reciprocals: 2/HM = 1/b + 1/c. At the apex, reach adds lengths; at the hinge, the figure adds reciprocals — two additions on one panel, and the second belongs to the harmonic register. The slivers themselves are priced in the same coin: each D equals h·HM/4, so the meter's null reading D = D is, in cash, a quarter of height times harmonic chord.

The parallels between the rails read every length from b to c; three stations are constructible, and the classical means take one each. Midheight reads the arithmetic mean — the midline. The chord that splits the trapezoid into two similar trapezoids reads the geometric mean. The diagonal crossing reads the harmonic mean. Stacked: HM highest, nearest the short rail, then GM, then AM — the classical inequality b < HM < GM < AM < c drawn as three rungs.

The credential is a cross-ratio, not a resemblance. The legs meet at V; the line V–X bisects both rails; and on that line the four points satisfy (m₁, m₀ ; X, V) = −1 — mid-top and mid-bot separated harmonically by hinge and leg-meet. So the point that certifies addition's shear license is simultaneously the panel's own certified −1: the sum panel, the additive register's home ground, carries an anharmonic signature. The means keep turning up at the hinges.

Read the bottom rail as a horizon, and every line takes a perspective office without moving. The sides of M X N V already aim at the rail's ends — X–N and M–V pass through O, M–X and N–V through R — so O and R are the two vanishing points, and the diagonals head for V_D = m₀ and for the point at infinity along M–N. The certificate that the original figure is a parallelogram is the −1 relocated to the horizon: a parallelogram's side and diagonal directions are harmonic on the line at infinity, projection preserves cross-ratio, and the fourth harmonic of ∞ with respect to O and R is their midpoint — where V_D stands. The upgrade to a square costs nothing: the station point S = (c/2, −c/2) sees O and R at right angles — Thales again, on the semicircle below the horizon — with its vertical bisector striking V_D: the canonical 45° set-up. And since O, R, V_D and S never move under the drag, every position of the division point images a genuine square from the same chair; the law is the viewing rig, the event is which square. Last, m₁ — the diagonal meet — is the image of the square's center, so (m₁, m₀ ; X, V) = −1 is the projected sentence "the center bisects the diagonal": the midpoint dies in projection, and this cross-ratio is its receipt.

The tilt handle runs the sheet through the sieve. Tilting the top rail off parallel — the handle at N rotates it about M — brings the fourth vanishing point onto the page: V_D′, where the second diagonal M–N meets the horizon. The four VPs immediately stand in harmonic ratio, (VPₗ, VPᵣ ; V_D, V_D′) = −1 — free, by the complete quadrangle — which in lengths reads 1/OV_D + 1/OV_D′ = 2/c: the base is the harmonic mean of the two diagonal-VP distances. Evicted from inside the figure, the harmonic mean re-enters on the horizon. The station point slides to the meet of the two Thales semicircles — on O–R for the sides, on V_D–V_D′ for the diagonals — and the harmonic relation guarantees the circles cross, so the imaged figure is a square at every tilt; the untilted plate is just the case where the second circle has straightened into the vertical through m₀. What the tilt kills is the affine story — the parallel levels, the means ladder, D = D, the juxtaposed A + B = C — and the plate parks those layers rather than draw falsehoods. What survives untouched: every incidence and every −1. The count survives the turn of the rig; the equal intervals do not.

Glossary

trapezoid O R N M
Bottom rail O–R of length c, top segment M–N of length b, legs O–M and R–N; every side and both diagonals are lines the sum panel had already drawn.
A, B, C — the juxtaposed reading
Five disjoint regions tile the rectangle: A = O L M, D = O M X, B = M X N, D = X N R, and C = O X R (hatched), each letter on its own ground. The diagonal O–N halves the rectangle into A + D + B above and C + D below; cancel D = D and A + B = C holds with nothing counted twice. The overlap lives only in the interpretation, where B and C stand for the classical base-b and base-c triangles under an apex — readings the check file verifies agree with this one.
hinge X
The meet of the diagonals O–N and R–M; the crossing that adjudicated D = D, now read as the trapezoid's harmonic center.
station
The height fraction t at which a parallel crosses; X's station is t = c/(b+c).
diagonal ratio
OX : XN = RX : XM = c : b — trapezoid diagonals cut each other in the ratio of the parallel sides.
harmonic chord
The parallel through X, of length 2bc/(b+c), bisected by X.
harmonic mean, reciprocal addition
HM(b,c) = 2bc/(b+c); equivalently 2/HM = 1/b + 1/c. The hinge adds reciprocals as the apex adds lengths.
sliver price
Each D equals ½bh·c/(b+c) = h·HM/4: the shear license's null reading, denominated in the harmonic mean.
means ladder
Three constructible parallels: AM at midheight (the midline), GM at the similar split, HM at the hinge; b < HM < GM < AM < c.
similar split
The chord of length √(bc) dividing the trapezoid into two similar trapezoids: c : √(bc) = √(bc) : b.
leg-meet V
The point where the extended legs O–M and R–N cross, at height hc/a; it flies upward as the division point moves left.
midline witness
The line V–X, which bisects both rails — the drawn proof that hinge and leg-meet govern the trapezoid's middles.
cross-ratio −1
(m₁, m₀ ; X, V) = −1 on the witness line: mid-top and mid-bot are harmonic conjugates with respect to hinge and leg-meet.
shear license, D = D
Euclid I.37–38: apexes on one rail give equal triangles; in the sum panel this is witnessed by the two equal slivers whose adjudicator is X.
horizon (HL)
The bottom rail read as eye-level: the line carrying the images of the ground plan's points at infinity.
vanishing points VPₗ, VPᵣ
O and R — where the square's two side directions meet the horizon; every side of M X N V passes through one of them.
distance point V_D
Where the depth diagonal vanishes; the fourth harmonic of ∞ with respect to the vanishing points, hence their midpoint m₀.
station point S
The viewer's plan position, (c/2, −c/2): sees O and R at 90° on the Thales semicircle below the horizon, with the vertical bisector striking V_D — the 45° certificate that the imaged figure is a square, fixed under the drag.
image of a square
The quadrilateral M X N V: sides alternately through O and R, diagonals M–N (to ∞) and X–V (to V_D); certified by homography in the check file.
center image m₁
The diagonal meet of M X N V; the −1 along X–V is the center's bisection of the diagonal, surviving projection as cross-ratio.
fourth vanishing point V_D′
Where the tilted second diagonal M–N meets HL; its arrival makes the horizon a complete harmonic four-point range, (VPₗ, VPᵣ ; V_D, V_D′) = −1, equivalently 1/OV_D + 1/OV_D′ = 2/c.
tilt
Rotating the top rail about M (handle at N); in the plan, rotating the square about its near corner. At tilt 0 the second diagonal is parallel to the horizon and V_D′ sits at ∞.
station point S′
The meet of the Thales semicircles on O–R and on V_D–V_D′, below the horizon; sees sides and diagonals each at 90°, so the imaged figure stays a square at every tilt.
parked layers
The affine instruments — harmonic chord, means ladder, midline witness, the juxtaposed A + B = C — all requiring parallel rails; at tilt ≠ 0 the plate parks them rather than draw falsehoods.
