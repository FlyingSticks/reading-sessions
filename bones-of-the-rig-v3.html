The bones of the rig

The fixed square and the breathing square, read as a cross-slit section. Drag Q, or ride the slider. One harmonic quadruple (A,C; Q,S) = −1; two involutions; two circles. With rig labels on, the plate is the depth-axis section of a cross-slit rig: image plane at S, blue slit at A, constructed harmonic plane at Q, red slit at C. Two tangent witnesses, one per circle — each touches directly above the inner point of its mirror pair: T over Q, T′ over A. The slider's right stop is the balance point Q = 6 − 3√2 (gold tick), where MQ = OA and the two circles are congruent.

Q = 1.535

The reading

One quadruple, two involutions. Harmonic separation is mutual: (A,C; Q,S) = −1 is the same statement as (Q,S; A,C) = −1. A harmonic quadruple therefore carries two involutions of the line — one fixing each pair and swapping the other — and each involution is inversion in a circle. The plate draws both circles.

The fixed circle — the construction law. The circle on A–C fixes the slits and swaps S ↔ Q: OQ·OS = OA². Given the apparatus (the two slits) and the image plane, the harmonic plane is produced as the mirror image of the image plane in the slit circle — or anchorlessly, by Euclid I.43: the dashed diagonal through O makes the teal rectangle and the crimson square equal complements. Center and radius are apparatus data; nothing about the reading touches them. The square is fixed. Its witness: the tangent from S touches at T, directly above Q — ST² = SA·SC.

The breathing circle — the channel swap. The circle on Q–S fixes the image plane and the harmonic plane and swaps the slits: MA·MC = MQ². Its radius is the geometric mean of the two slit distances from its center — the anchor law R² = Z₁Z₂, recentred at M. This is the channel swap σ, the apparatus ingredient of the transport δ = σ∘ρ. Its center rides the free point, so the square breathes; the law it obeys never changes. Its witness, symmetric to the first: the tangent from the red slit C touches at T′, directly above the blue slit A — CT′² = CQ·CS. Each circle catches its own swapped pair in the act: the tangent from the outer point of a mirror pair touches directly above the inner point.

Why the plate is admitted. An earlier ruling refused this plate from the book: two centers, not two readings. The rig labels reprice the verdict. The two centers are the two involutions the quadruple carries — one pinned to the apparatus, one riding the probe — and the probe-riding one is σ. The plate is the depth-axis section of the rig, with the construction law and the channel swap in one figure.

The balance point. The slider's right stop is Q = 6 − 3√2, where the two squares agree: MQ = OA, so the circles are congruent, the span Q–S equals the span A–C, OQ = MA, and the two witnesses stand at equal heights. There the half-turn of the line about the midpoint of O and M carries A → Q and C → S — the third involution the quadruple always carries (exchange the two conjugate pairs), turned from projective fact into literal Euclidean mirror. The fixed pair prices itself in silver units: OQ = 3(√2 − 1), OS = 3(√2 + 1), product 9. One law holds at every Q and should not be mistaken for the event: the two circles always cross at right angles — OM² = OA² + MQ² is equivalent to the harmonic condition itself. Orthogonality is the law; congruence is the event.

Glossary

harmonic range
Four collinear points with cross-ratio −1, written (A,C; Q,S) = −1. Q and S are harmonic conjugates with respect to A and C — and, mutually, A and C with respect to Q and S.
cross-ratio
(A,C; Q,S) = (AQ·CS)/(CQ·AS) in signed lengths; the projective invariant of four collinear points.
Newton product / Newton's relation
Measure a conjugate pair from the midpoint of the other pair; the product of the two signed distances is constant: OQ·OS = OA² and MA·MC = MQ². Each relation is equivalent to the harmonic condition.
fixed square
OA² = OC²: side half the slit separation. Pinned by the apparatus; unchanged as Q moves.
breathing square
MQ² = MS²: side the half-span of the conjugate pair Q–S. Its center M rides with Q, so the square swells and shrinks under the fixed law MA·MC = MQ².
involution
A transformation that is its own inverse — applied twice, the identity. On a line, determined by its two fixed points, or equally by one swapped pair.
inversion / mirror circle
The involution z ↦ O + R²/(z−O): points pair off so that the product of their distances from the center is R². The circle itself is the set of self-paired points.
image plane
The screen of the rig — here the plane at S, in front of both slits.
slit (blue, red)
The two line apertures of the cross-slit camera — here the planes at A and C on the depth axis. In section, each slit is a point.
constructed harmonic plane
The plane at Q: the harmonic conjugate of the image plane through the slit pair. Produced by the fixed circle (compass route) or by I.43 complements (anchorless route).
channel swap σ
The involution exchanging the two slits — the u- and v-channels — while fixing the image and harmonic planes. Realized here by the breathing circle.
geometric-mean anchor
The circle whose radius is the geometric mean of the two slit distances from its center (R² = Z₁Z₂). The breathing circle is this anchor, recentred at M.
tangent witness
Each circle carries one. Fixed: the tangent from S touches at T, directly above Q, with ST² = SA·SC = OS² − OA² — the certificate that S and Q are inverse points. Breathing: the tangent from C touches at T′, directly above A, with CT′² = CQ·CS = MC² − MQ² — the certificate that C and A are inverse points. In each, the tangent from the outer point of a mirror pair touches directly above the inner point; T rides the harmonic plane's line, T′ rides the blue slit's.
Euclid I.43
In a parallelogram cut by its diagonal, the two complements about the diagonal are equal in area. The anchorless (straightedge) grammar for both Newton relations.
third involution (pair swap)
The involution exchanging the two conjugate pairs — A ↔ Q and C ↔ S at once. Every harmonic quadruple carries it projectively; at the balance point it becomes the Euclidean half-turn about the midpoint of O and M.
balance point
Q = 6 − 3√2, the slider's right stop: MQ = OA, the two circles congruent, the spans Q–S and A–C equal, the witnesses at equal heights, OQ = 3(√2 − 1) and OS = 3(√2 + 1).
orthogonal circles
The fixed and breathing circles cross at right angles at every Q: OM² = OA² + MQ², equivalent to the harmonic condition. The law behind the plate; congruence at the balance point is the event.
apparatus / probe
Apparatus: what the rig fixes before any reading — the slits, hence O and OA. Probe: the one free point, Q, fourth argument of every cross-ratio. What rides with it — S, M, the breathing circle — is reading, not rig.
