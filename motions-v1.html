Motions of the plane

Four kinds, one primitive. 2 November 2026.

A motion is a rule that moves every point of the plane to a point of the plane so that all distances are kept — equivalently, a way of picking the plane up as a rigid thing and setting it down on itself. This page sorts the motions into their four kinds, shows that all four are built from one of them, and lets the building be watched step by step.

1. Four kinds

Two questions sort every motion. Does it keep handedness — does a small clockwise arrow drawn on the paper stay clockwise? And does it leave any point where it was?

Keeping handedness is the same as being doable without lifting the paper: sliding and spinning on the table never reverse an arrow, and flipping the paper always does. So the top row stays on the table and the bottom row has to be lifted.

Examples as rules on coordinates. Slide: (x, y) ↦ (x + 3, y). Spin: (x, y) ↦ (−y, x), the quarter-turn about 0; (x, y) ↦ (−x, −y), the half-turn. Mirror: (x, y) ↦ (−x, y), in the vertical axis; (x, y) ↦ (y, x), in the diagonal. Glide: (x, y) ↦ (x + 3, −y). Not motions: (x, y) ↦ (2x, 2y), a stretch; (x, y) ↦ (x + y, y), a shear; (x, y) ↦ (0, x), the collapse ×ε; and f ↦ f², squaring, which doubles angles and squares distances. The test in every case: pick two points, measure their separation before and after. A motion never changes that number.

2. The mirror is the primitive

A single mirror reverses handedness and fixes a line. Two mirrors reverse handedness twice, so restore it, and between them fix one point (where the lines cross) or nothing (when they are parallel): those are the rotation and the translation, with the amounts doubled — twice the angle, twice the distance. Three mirrors reverse handedness, so give a reflection or a glide; the glide is the only new thing three can make, and it appears exactly when the last two mirrors slide along the first. Four mirrors collapse back to two. So every motion is a word of length at most three in the single letter "mirror," and the four-kinds table is the mirror count: even count, top row; odd count, bottom row.

3. Watch the mirrors compose

The animation passes the arrow through each line and out the other side; that is a drawing of the flip, not geometry. A mirror has no "during." Only the start and end of each step are the mathematics. Previous positions remain as dashed ghosts; mirrors already used go faint; the arrow turns green when the last mirror is done.

4. Where the fold sits

The pairing of the fold through a point is the half-turn, which is two mirrors at right angles. The pairing of the fold across a line is one mirror. So the two folds of the six-to-three page differ by exactly one mirror — the one that flips the paper. The fold squaring makes is an even fold; the mirror fold is an odd one.

A sign, as defined on that page, needs a motion that undoes itself. Of the four kinds, only two contain such motions: half-turns (one per point) and mirrors (one per line). Other rotations need more than two turns to return; translations and glides never return. A glide done twice is a pure slide, so a glide is a square root of a translation — sign-like in form, but it does not come back.

Glossary

Drawing words and mathematical words are kept apart.

motion
A rule moving every point of the plane to a point of the plane, keeping all distances. Always undoable. Also called an isometry or a rigid motion.
map
Any rule moving every point to a point, distances kept or not. Every motion is a map; the stretch, the shear, the collapse ×ε and squaring are maps that are not motions.
handedness
Whether a small clockwise arrow stays clockwise. Kept by rotations and translations, reversed by reflections and glides. Keeping it is the same as not having to lift the paper.
fixed point
A point a motion leaves where it was. A rotation fixes one point, a reflection fixes a line, a translation and a glide fix nothing.
rotation (spin)
Turning the plane about one fixed point through an angle. The half-turn is the rotation by 180°.
translation (slide)
Moving every point the same distance in the same direction.
reflection (mirror)
Sending each point to the point the same distance away on the other side of a fixed line, at a right angle to it. The line is the mirror line.
glide reflection (glide)
A mirror in a line followed by a slide along that same line. The footprint motion.
primitive
The one kind the others are built from. The mirror is primitive: every motion is one, two or three mirrors.
involution
A motion that undoes itself: done twice, nothing. Half-turns and mirrors are the involutions among the motions.
stroke with a dot (drawing word)
The test figure in the diagrams: a short line with a dot at its head. Its only job is to have a handedness, so that a mirror image can be told from a turned copy.
ghost (drawing word)
A dashed copy of the figure at an earlier position. It records where the figure was; it is not a second figure.
