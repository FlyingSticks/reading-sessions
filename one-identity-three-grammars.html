One identity, three grammars

Xg·Xc = x² proved by areas, by the compass, and by the mirror — with the ancestor figure that held all three before they had names

Thesis. The mirror identity has three proofs, and they are written in three different grammars. Euclid I.43 proves it with areas and a straightedge — the anchorless grammar, no circle drawn, no unit declared. The semicircle altitude proves it with one compass stroke — the anchored grammar, the circle playing exactly the role of the marked-conic anchor (§8.3′): one curve that converts projective data into metric conclusions. The mirror circle proves it inversively — the engine's native reading, where the identity becomes an incidence (a harmonic range) and so survives projection. One identity, three tools; and the split between them is the anchorless/anchored divide of the larger program.

The cast. One scene point, three marks on the picture plane: the glide mark Xg (teal), the perpendicular projection x (black, the corner rig's reading), and the camera mark Xc (purple). The gauges are multiplicative — Xg = x·(1+s), Xc = x/(1+s) — with s = rλ = z/d: the point's depth behind the plane measured in units of the slit's own distance in front of it. One s per channel; this page reads the u channel. One slider drives the first three figures, because the three proofs are functions of the same single variable — watching them move together is watching one identity wear three costumes.

s = 0 — the seam: the three marks fuse at x and every proof below collapses to a tautology. A tautology needs no proof in any grammar.

Grammar I — areas. Euclid I.43, straightedge only

A = A = 1.000·dx   B = B = 0.500·dx
The glide diagonal (teal, from the slit u through x on the plane to Xg at the point's level) is the diameter of one rectangle; the camera diagonal (purple, from u straight to the point through Xc) is the diameter of another. By I.43 the complements about each diagonal are equal: d(Xg − x) = z·x and d(x − Xc) = z·Xc. Divide the two equalities and Xg·Xc = x² falls out. Every move is incidence and area bookkeeping — no compass, no unit. Left of the plane the paired strips sit adjacent; right of it they sit stacked — the same trade the marks make about x in front of the plane.

Grammar II — the compass. Semicircle altitude

Lay Xg and Xc end to end as a diameter and erect the perpendicular at the junction. Thales gives a right angle where it meets the arc; similarity at the altitude gives Xg : x = x : Xc directly. Slide s and watch the invariant this grammar exposes: the diameter redistributes, the altitude never changes length. The proof cannot be run with a straightedge alone — the circle is the added instrument, the anchor that turns a product into a length.

Grammar III — the mirror. Inversion and the tangent witness

(Xg, Xc; x, −x) = −1.000   tangent length = 1.000·x
Xg and Xc are inverse in the mirror circle of radius x about the slits-axis origin. Two witnesses, both incidences. The tangent witness: the tangent from 0 to any circle through the pair touches exactly on the mirror circle, its length locked at x wherever the slider sits (equivalently, that circle is orthogonal to the mirror). The harmonic witness: (Xg, Xc; x, −x) = −1 — the mark and its reflection through the slits axis separate the pair harmonically. The axis acts as mirror, not floor. Grammar II states the identity as a length; this grammar states it as an incidence, which is what survives projection.

Same grammar as Figure 1. Triangle OAD (half the rectangle, cut by the diagonal) and triangle OB2 both have area 1 — the second because B rides a parallel to the base, Euclid I.37. The equal totals share OBP; subtract the shared piece and the theorem is what remains. Decompose the whole two ways, cancel the common part — exactly the move by which Euclid proves I.43 itself. Anchorless: straightedge and area bookkeeping.
Same compass gadget as Figure 2, cast in the dual role. There the altitude is pinned at x while the diameter Xg + Xc breathes — the gadget verifies a conjugate pair. Here the diameter AD is fixed while the split slides with B, so B′ rides the arc and the altitude √(AB·BD) varies — the gadget translates between the reciprocal register above and the √ register below. One anchor, two jobs: certify a product, or exchange currencies between graduations.
