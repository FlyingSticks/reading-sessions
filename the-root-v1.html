The root

What the pair keeps and the congruence forgets. Three planes; mirror lines through 0 only. Version 1, 11 November 2026.

The pair plate found that two mirrors at angle φ make a turn by 2φ. The pair at φ and the pair at φ + 180° are the same two lines and make the same turn. So a pair of lines carries a half-measure, twice over, and the congruence it makes carries only the doubled measure. This plate writes the mirror and the pair in the arithmetic of each plane, the arithmetic of the fold page, and finds that the doubling is a squaring and the half-measure is a square root. It then settles a claim made before this plate was built; section 5 gives the verdict.

1. The arithmetic used

As on the fold page and the three-pairs plate, a point of the plane is a number z = x + g y, with x measured across the page from 0 and y up the page from 0. The three planes differ only in the square of g: g² = −1 (Euclidean), g² = 0 (parabolic), g² = +1 (Minkowski). Numbers are multiplied by the ordinary rules, replacing g² by its value.

Three more words, all in the glossary. The bar of z is z̄ = x − g y; on the page it is the partner of z under the mirror with the horizontal axis as its line. The form of z is z × z̄, which is the form of the three-pairs plate: x² + y², x², or x² − y². The unit locus is the set of numbers whose form is 1. For a number a on the unit locus, a × ā = 1, so dividing by a is the same as multiplying by ā.

A mirror line m through 0 crosses the unit locus at two points, b and −b. Either one names the line.

2. One mirror, in numbers

Statement. The mirror with line m, where m passes through 0 and the unit point b, sends each number z to

z′ = b² × z̄.

Reason. Three short computations show that this rule has the properties that define the mirror.

  1. It fixes every point of m. A point of m is t × b with t an ordinary number. The rule sends it to b² × t × b̄ = t × b × (b × b̄) = t × b.
  2. Applied twice it does nothing. The bar of b² × z̄ is b̄² × z. So the rule applied twice gives b² × b̄² × z = (b × b̄)² × z = z.
  3. It keeps the form. The form of a product is the product of the forms, and the form of b is 1.

A rule that keeps the form, fixes every point of m, undoes itself, and is not the rule that does nothing, is the mirror with line m. (Standard theorem, used here and not proved here; Figure 1 checks it against the drawing rule of the involution plate.)

What to notice. The unit point b enters only as b². Since (−b)² = b², the points b and −b give the same rule, as they must: they name the same line. The line is ±b. The mirror is b².

3. The pair, in numbers

Let line A have unit point a and line B have unit point b. Apply section 2 twice. A sends z to a² × z̄. Then B sends that to b² × (ā² × z). Collect the terms:

A then B sends z to q² × z, where q = b × ā = b ÷ a.

Three things follow.

  • The bar has gone. One mirror has one bar; two mirrors have none. The bar is the sign of the earlier plates: present for an odd product, absent for an even one.
  • The doubling is a squaring. The number q lies on the unit locus, and its measure is the measure from A to B. The congruence multiplies by q², whose measure is twice that.
  • The pair knows q only up to sign. Replace b by −b: the same line B, but q becomes −q. The congruence, q², does not change. So a pair of lines gives two numbers, q and −q, and the congruence gives one, their common square. These are the two roots of the fold page.

4. Where the two roots can be told apart

Set Figure 2 to Euclidean and move the slider through 180°. The line B returns to where it was. The gold L″ returns to where it was. But q has travelled half-way round the circle and now stands where −q stood. The walk was continuous and nothing jumped. So in the Euclidean plane there is no way to say which of q, −q belongs to a pair of lines: either can be walked into the other while the lines end where they began.

Set it to Parabolic or Minkowski. The unit locus now has two separate pieces. The slider moves q along one piece and can never carry it to the other; only the button, which is a jump, puts −q in use. In these two planes the root can be chosen once and for all: take the one on the piece that contains 1.

This is where the square of g acts. With g² = −1 the unit locus is one closed loop, and the two roots are joined. With g² = 0 or +1 it is two pieces, and the two roots are separated.

5. The claim made before this plate

Before this plate was built, the claim was: “each mirror becomes a thing with a square (+1, 0, or −1), and ‘multiply the mirrors’ becomes literal multiplication. This looks like g² = −1, 0, +1.” It was priced as a rhyme. Now checked, it divides into three parts.

PartVerdict
“Multiply the mirrors” is literal multiplication.Holds. The pair is the number q = b ÷ a; the congruence is multiplication by q². Sections 2 and 3.
The square of a mirror is +1, 0 or −1, matching g².Struck. A mirror applied twice does nothing in all three planes; in numbers, b × b̄ = 1 in all three. The square of g is not the square of any mirror.
The square of g has a part in the doubling.Holds, differently. It decides whether the two roots q, −q can be walked into each other. Section 4.

There is a standard algebra (Clifford's) in which each mirror line through 0 is itself an element that can be multiplied, the product of two such elements is the number q or its bar, and the square of the element is the form at that point of the line. It says the same as sections 2 and 3 in another notation. It is not developed here.

8. Glossary

Mathematical words

Number z = x + g y
A point of the plane, written so that it can be multiplied. g² is −1, 0 or +1 according to the plane.
Bar, z̄
x − g y. The standard name is the conjugate.
Form of z
z × z̄ = x² − g² y². The same form as on the three-pairs plate.
Unit locus
The numbers whose form is 1. Euclidean: a circle. Parabolic: the two vertical lines x = 1 and x = −1. Minkowski: the two branches of a hyperbola.
Unit point of a line
A point where a line through 0 crosses the unit locus. Each mirror line of this plate has two, b and −b.
Measure
As on the three-pairs plate: twice the area of the sector from 1 to the point, counted along the unit locus. In the Euclidean plane it is the angle, shown in degrees. In the other two planes it is shown as a plain number, and it is defined only on the piece of the unit locus that contains 1.
q
b ÷ a, which equals b × ā. The number that belongs to the pair A, B once unit points a, b are chosen.
Root
A number whose square is a given number. q and −q are the two roots of q².
Piece (of the unit locus)
A part that can be walked along without leaving the locus and without a jump.

Drawing words

Filled dot
A point that is given or in use.
Ringed dot
A small open circle marking a point that is found. “Ringed” is a drawing word only.
Probe
The black letter L of Figure 2.
Drawing rule
The rule of the involution plate for finding a partner: the mirror line passes through the midpoint, and the segment runs in the across direction. It uses no arithmetic of the plane's numbers.
