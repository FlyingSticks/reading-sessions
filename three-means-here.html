The three means, here

The annotated profile asked three questions at once: where the means stand, why a two-point perspective can be constructed on A B C D, and whether a single equivalent pinhole plane can stand in for the pair of slits. This page answers all three from one figure. Same rig as the sketch: glass at O = 0, vertical slit at Zv = 150, horizontal slit at Zh = 355, probe at 469. Drag A, B, D along the axis; C is never dragged — it is constructed, and the checks below re-run on every move.

The profile of the means sphere, redrawn as the sketch has it. Circle on the slits as diameter; tangent from the glass, length GM; its touch point drops to the axis at C = z* = HM; the centre sits at AM. The red quadrilateral sends its sides to B and D and its diagonals to C — the fourth ray lands on A by itself. The eye E stands where the circle on AC crosses the circle on BD: the two cross at right angles, and E sits on the sphere.

What to look for

Each mean answers a different construction from the same circle. The arithmetic mean is the centre: (a+b)/2 from the glass, the midpoint of the slits, found by a perpendicular bisector and by nothing subtler. The geometric mean is the reach: the tangent from the glass has length √(ab) because the power of the glass with respect to the circle is exactly ab. The harmonic mean is the landing: drop the touch point to the axis and its foot stands at 2ab/(a+b) — the gold mark C, the neutral surface, and, in projective dress, the harmonic conjugate of the glass across the two slits. The sketch labels B the slit-true-length view and D the slit-point view; the means never notice which is which — they are symmetric in a and b — but the view labels swap if the rig turns.

That last reading is what licenses the red construction. Because (B, D; C, A) = −1, the four marks form a harmonic range, and a harmonic range on a horizon is exactly the vanishing data of a parallelogram: sides to one pair, diagonals to the other. Start a quadrilateral anywhere, aim its sides at B and D and one diagonal at C, and the second diagonal finds the glass on its own — that landing is the law, not a coincidence of the drawing. To sharpen parallelogram into square, the eye must see both pairs at right angles, which places it where the circle on AC meets the circle on BD. Harmonic range ⇔ those circles are orthogonal, so they always do meet — and the circle on BD is the sphere's own equator. The eye that squares the range stands on the sphere.

Equivalent pinhole plane?

probe depth 469

Reciprocal channel scale against probe depth. Each channel is a straight line through (O, 1) with slope −1/Z; the gold line of a pinhole at z* = HM is exactly the arithmetic midline of the crimson and purple channels — the black ring (their average) rides the gold dot at every probe depth. Pinholes at AM and GM, dashed, are not the midline.

The answer to the handwritten question is the gold line. For imaging, the equivalent pinhole plane stands at z* = HM, through C — and the mechanism is visible above: reciprocal scale is affine in depth, so the pair of channels averages by averaging slopes, and the arithmetic mean of −1/Zv and −1/Zh is −1/HM. Averaging slopes arithmetically is averaging depths harmonically. That is the whole trick.

There is no single pinhole equivalent for everything. Each mean is the equivalent pinhole for a different question: HM for what the rig does to images; AM for where the middle of the slits is — the sphere's centre, the point the midplane involution trades with the ideal point; GM for the self-dual reading that prices the same in the rate square and the depth square. One rig, three equivalence questions, three pinholes; only the harmonic one images like the pair.

And none of it moves as the probe slides: the alternative probe locations in the sketch change the mark, never the means. That is a count of zero, entered below.

Counts · Laws · Gauges · Defects

A count is an integer fact of incidence, invariant under dragging. A law is an exact equality, closed by the checks. A gauge is a chosen convention — origin, scale, direction — that moves the readouts without moving the facts. A defect is a measured failure to close: a number driven to zero, or a region where a construction leaves the reals.

Counts

  • 2 slits · 3 means · 1 law binding them (AM × HM = GM²).
  • 4 points in the range A B C D · 1 cross-ratio · value −1.
  • 2 tangents from the glass; in the round they fuse into 1 circle of tangency, the gold ring.
  • 2 / 1 / 0 — coordinates a mark settles with both slits, one slit, no slit: a point, a line, nothing. A half-formed image is a line.
  • 0 — the number of these constructions that depend on the probe.
  • 9 live checks in the ledger below.

Laws

  • AM × HM = GM² — GM is itself the geometric mean of the other two; the closing law.
  • Power of the glass = ab = (tangent length)²; equivalently AM² − GM² = R².
  • Polar law: (T − M)·(O − M) = R²; the polar of the glass meets the axis at HM.
  • (B, D; C, A) = −1 — C is the harmonic conjugate of the glass across the slits: the virtual centre.
  • Harmonic range ⇔ orthogonal circles (circle on AC ⟂ circle on BD); their crossing is the eye E, on the sphere.
  • Reciprocation swaps AM ↔ HM and fixes GM.
  • Mean of the channels' reciprocal scales = reciprocal scale of a pinhole at z*, at every probe depth, for every rig.

Gauges

  • Origin at the glass. The means are means of a and b, distances from O, not of absolute depths — drag the glass and all three follow.
  • True depth scale. No compression here; a squeezed circle would fake the tangency it exists to show.
  • Light direction. Scene beyond both slits; the mark inverts; reciprocal scale runs affine in depth, through (O, 1).
  • Which slit is "vertical". The means are symmetric in a and b and do not care; the view labels — slit-point, slit-true-length — swap when the rig turns a quarter.

Defects

  • AM − HM = 2R²/(a+b) — the spread of the range read as one number; zero exactly at the pinhole. Live in the readouts.
  • Glass between the slits: the power ab goes negative, GM leaves the reals, the tangent has nowhere to land — the page stops drawing it rather than fake it.
  • Pinhole limit: R → 0, the range degenerates, the cross-ratio loses its footing (0/0) — while the scales law survives, trivially.

Checks

Re-run on every interaction, and computed from the drawn geometry, not from the closed forms they confirm: T comes from a Thales-circle intersection, C is the foot of that constructed T, the square is built by ruler-and-straightedge intersections, and E is the crossing of the two drawn circles. The closed forms sit on the other side of each residual.

Conventions

Sketch depths by default; the book rig (150 / 250) is one button away. Judgment calls flagged: C is drawn at the foot of the constructed tangency, not at the closed form, so check 2 is honest; the square's starting vertex S is a free choice — the theorem is the fourth ray's landing, not the quadrilateral's shape; the eye E is one certified placement of the station point, not the only one, and the upper crossing is drawn. The level set at z* is a plane here and is called one.

Glossary

glass · image plane · O
The plane the marks land on; the origin of the gauge. Point A of the range.
slit
A line the ray must meet. Vertical slit at Zv (point B), horizontal at Zh (point D).
slit-true-length view
The profile's view of the slit lying parallel to the paper — drawn at true length. Here, B.
slit-point view
The profile's view of the slit pointing at you — collapsed to a point. Here, D.
mark
Where the threaded ray pierces the glass.
probe
The scene point being imaged; slides along a depth line. The means ignore it.
pinhole
The rig with both slits at one depth; where the three means fuse.
power of a point
(distance to centre)² − R²; for the glass it equals ab, and its square root is the tangent length.
polar
The line (in the round, plane) of touch points of tangents from a point; the polar of the glass meets the axis at HM.
neutral surface · z*
The transverse plane at the harmonic mean, where the two channels agree.
virtual centre
The point C: the fourth harmonic of the glass in the range B, D — the centre a two-point perspective on the axis behaves as if it had.
harmonic range
Four collinear points with cross-ratio −1; here A B C D, with C conjugate to A across B, D.
cross-ratio
((C−B)/(C−D)) : ((A−B)/(A−D)); the one projective invariant of four collinear points.
orthogonal circles
Circles crossing at right angles; equivalent to the harmonic separation of their diameters' endpoints.
count · law · gauge · defect
Integer incidence fact · exact equality · chosen convention · measured failure to close.
