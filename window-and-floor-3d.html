Window and floor, in the round

The two panels of the companion page, reassembled into the one scene they were sections of. Drag to orbit; slide the probe; turn the ruler.

One plane, two jobs, one scene. The purple ring is where the sight line lands — an output, the window's mark. The teal dot is where the ruler says to stand — an input, the floor's tick. Turn welds them at every depth; Reach lets the tick walk off the sheet.

What to look for

The green line standing off the plane is the world's depth line: it rises from its footprint at level zero and carries the probe. The camera reads it through the pinhole — each sight line pierces the glass, and the purple ring is the pierce point at the current depth. The draftsman reads the same line by drawing its trace on the plane, from footprint to seat, and laying a ruler along it.

The small purple dots are the camera's marks at equal depth steps 100 to 500 — crowding toward the seat, because that is the law the pinhole welds. The small teal squares are the ruler's ticks at the same depths. Under Turn they sit on the purple dots and the moving pair travels welded. Under Reach the ticks spread equally, the moving tick overtakes the seat near z = 300, and it leaves the sheet along the dashed extension — equal steps leave infinity unmarked, Theorem 0 seen from three-quarter view.

Turn the sight lines on to see why the purple marks crowd: equal steps in the world arrive at the glass through an ever-narrower fan.

Conventions. Rig as in Window and Floor and Chapter 2: pinhole at Zp = 200 behind the plane, r = 1/Zp, mark M(z) = F/(1+rz), Turn tick at μ = z/(1+rz). One drawing decision to flag: the companion page calibrates Reach to agree with Turn at level 1; here Reach is calibrated to agree at the first drawn level, z = 100, so that its equal steps are visible at this sheet scale. The identities checked are unchanged. Depth is compressed 0.5× for display only, and the probe range capped at 800 to keep the whole line on the canvas — an affine scaling, so every straightness and incidence in the scene is honest.
