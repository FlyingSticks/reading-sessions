How It Goes Together

A map of three bodies of work and the joins between them. 19 November 2026. Not audited outside. The file names assume this page sits in the same folder as the plates.

There are three bodies of work: the book, the primer that stands in front of it, and the mirror thread that runs beside both. One object occurs in all three, the involution, with the value −1. The figure shows which joins between them have been shown, which are only a rhyme, and which one was tested and struck.

Terms

involution
A reversible rule that is its own backward rule and is not the identity. Applied twice it returns everything; applied once it moves at least one thing. The book's convention.
harmonic pairing
The pairing of P with P′ on a line through two fixed points A and B, where (P, P′; A, B) = −1.
congruence (book)
A family of lines in space: here, the lines meeting both slits.
congruence (plates)
A distance-preserving map.
shown
Stated on a plate or in a check script and supported there. The note under it says how far the support goes.
rhyme
A likeness of form with no statement that one thing is the other.
struck
Stated, tested, and found false.
open
Not yet stated as a claim.
seam
The book's word for where the drawing reading and the camera reading agree.
