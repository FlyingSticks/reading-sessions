Reading the plate

One point, two dossiers. Under the dictionary O↔O, 2↔R, D↔N, B↔M, the lower figure of the settlement plate (R·T-03) is the hinge configuration of R·T-02: the lines B–2 and O–D are R–M and O–N, and their crossing P is X, with the station heights agreeing verbatim — 2/(4−b) there is hc/(b+c) here, once the two sheets' opposite conventions for b are translated. Two check files had been metering one point from two sides. On this plate it is drawn once and labeled with both names.

The headline: one theorem, two proofs. In the hinge's own coordinates, the Minkowski circle of the product form Q(x, y) = xy on diameter O–M — the gold rectangular hyperbola, null asymptotes, centered at O–M's midpoint — crosses the right edge at exactly one point, P₂ = (c, hc/(b+c)): the hinge's height. The angle O–P₂–M is Q-right, so signed Pythagoras gives Q(OP₂) + Q(P₂M) = Q(OM), legs of opposite type. Now read the squares: the half-square on O–M is the region A vertex for vertex; the half-square on O–P₂ slides along the null rail y = hc/(b+c) — apex P₂ riding to X, Euclid I.37 — into the hatched C; the half-square on P₂–M, whose rectangle owns the corners M and N, slides by the same motion into the green B. The negative square crosses the equals sign and the trade becomes the tiling: A + B = C, the juxtaposed identity, is the uv-Pythagoras wearing region form. What D = D proves by exchange of slivers, the hyperbola proves by signature. Trade versus tiling is (1,1) versus positive-definite — two receipts, one purchase.

Three mountings of one instrument. Every certificate on either sheet is a Thales locus on a diameter. The circle bank hangs it above the rail: the semicircle on L–N with apex M′ over M, where LM′² + M′N² = LN² and the altitude M′M reads √(ab) — the geometric mean, the sum bank's quadratic certificate. The uv bank hangs its conic over the same ground: the hyperbola on O–M with apex P₂, right angle by the null-mirror law, equal and opposite slopes ±h/(b+c). The horizon bank hangs it below: the semicircle on O–R with the station point S, from which O and R subtend 90° and whose vertical strikes V_D — the viewer's right angle. Same sentence, three banks; and each bank keeps a degenerate resting state (a null diameter's circle collapsing to a line pair; the untilted second Thales circle straightened into a vertical).

The means keep their addresses. Inside the figure: HM is the chord through X, bisected by it; GM is the altitude — √(ab) at M′ upstairs, the signed relation Q(P₂F) = Q(OP₂)·Q(P₂M)/Q(OM) in the uv bank; AM is the midline, and the circle bank's radius. Under tilt (R·T-02 v7) the harmonic mean is evicted from the interior and re-hired on the horizon as 1/OV_D + 1/OV_D′ = 2/c. The hinge is where the three registers make change for one another.

What each excursion keeps. The metric-ward sheet (R·T-03) can say why there is a right angle — it owns the forms, its certificates are conics, its −1s implicit. The projective-ward sheet (R·T-02 v7) can say what survives when the forms are taken away — its certificates are cross-ratios, its metric duties exiled to a single point below the page. Bracketed together: one congruence, priced in metric and priced in count, with the cross-ratio as the currency every bank honors. This plate is their common ground drawn as one figure.

The tilt handle then runs the sieve over the banks themselves, and it sorts three strata, not two. Tilting the red line about M parks the rail-borne claims — the five-region tiling, the circle bank (whose diameter was the level rail), the means ladder, and the null slide, since X leaves the rail y = hc/(b+c). But the uv bank stands: the hyperbola's diameter O–M and the apex P₂ never consulted the rail, so the signed ledger ½Q(OM) = ½Q(OP₂) + ½Q(P₂M) reads the same numbers at every tilt — the caption shows them unchanged while the tiling beside them is parked. And the incidence-borne stratum stands with it: the fourth vanishing point arrives on HL, the four VPs lock into (VPₗ, VPᵣ ; V_D, V_D′) = −1 with 1/OV_D + 1/OV_D′ = 2/c, and S′ slides to the meet of the two Thales semicircles, still certifying a square. So the sieve grades finer than affine-versus-projective: claims borne by the rail, claims borne by the form, claims borne by incidence alone — and the equivalence theorem, which needs both the tiling and the form, parks with its weaker partner, exactly as a joint account should.

The progression chip draws the crowding itself. Seed a post on the base, run it up to the red line, slant it to R; where the slant crosses the diagonal O–N, drop the next post; repeat. The composite is a projectivity of the rail into itself — down along the vertical pencil, back through VPᵣ — and its two fixed points collide at N, since the vertical through the rail-diagonal meet is the right edge, which carries R. A double fixed point makes the map parabolic, and a parabolic orbit is the harmonic case between cycling and geometric decay: the reciprocal gaps to N advance by the exact constant 1/c per bounce, each new gap is half the harmonic mean of the old gap and the base, and every consecutive triple closes a −1 with N. This is the drafting-room diagonal method for subdividing a recession, caught being the invariant-count panel: equal steps in the reciprocal register, crowding in the drawn one, the fixed point playing the closed ∞. And because the construction is pure incidence plus one direction, it survives the tilt — the −1s hold and the reciprocal gaps stay arithmetic at every setting; only the step’s value, 1/c, belongs to the level frame. The check file records this as a finding: the first draft asserted the arithmetic law would fail off-level, and the run refused — the progression is harmonic in every frame, and only its rate is furniture.

The matched-altitudes chip prices the horizon's −1 in the circle bank's own coin. Write the harmonic range (O, R ; V_D, V_D′) = −1 in product form: OV_D · RV_D′ = V_DR · OV_D′. Each side is two lengths multiplied, and a product of two lengths is what an ab = H² circle is for: lay the two pieces end to end on HL, raise the Thales semicircle over them, and the altitude at the joint squares to the product. Both products can be laid from the same foot — H = c − OV_D, which is V_D mirrored in m₀ — so the two circles stand on one foot, and the −1 says exactly that their altitudes agree. Two circles, one red altitude, ab = H² twice over: the four vanishing points are harmonic if and only if the altitudes match. In the sketch that prompted the chip the numbers were 3 · 2 = 6 · 1, altitude √6. At level the pair rests: V_D′ is at infinity, the second circle has no far end, and both straighten into the vertical through m₀ — the circle bank's own degenerate state, now on the horizon. This chip is a metric certificate of an incidence-borne claim, so it stands at every tilt.

The prism chip takes the plate's first step off the page's plane. The red quad M X N V is the image of a square at ceiling height; send every vertex straight down to y → −k·y and the four images make a second square, the floor, joined to the first by vertical edges. The map is a vertical homology with HL for its axis, so it fixes every vanishing point: the floor's sides still run to O and R, its diagonals to V_D and V_D′, and its center image still carries its −1 — the floor is a square from the same station S′, verified by homography in the check file. The ratio k is eye height above the floor over ceiling height above the eye, so the eye sits at k/(1+k) of the box; the stepper moves it. Because N and V both stand on the right edge v = c, the face N–V of the box is always edge-on — the plate's box is the right-hand box of the sketch. What the tilt does to the box it does to its ceiling: the −1s hold at every setting.

The free-square chip lets the square off the frame. On the plate the red quad has two corners, N and V, defined as meets with the right edge x = c, so its side N–V lies on the frame's leg by construction, and every version so far has shown a square with one face edge-on. That is an event, not a law. From the four vanishing points alone, one seed corner M′ and one size τ determine a square by joins and meets: the next corner X′ sits on M′–R at fraction τ; N′ is where M′'s line to V_D′ crosses X′'s line to O; V′ closes the figure on N′–R and M′–O. Nothing in the recipe consults x = c, so the edge N′–V′ leans in general, and the chip reads the lean in degrees against a vertical witness through N′. Drag the seed onto M and set τ to the hinge's fraction — the seat-on-the-leg button does it — and N′ lands on the leg, the edge stands vertical, and the free square is the frame's square again. The recipe also shows the −1 doing work: only the diagonal to V_D′ is drawn into the construction, yet the other diagonal X′–V′ arrives at V_D on its own, at every seed, size and tilt, because V_D is V_D′'s harmonic mate with respect to O and R. With the prism chip on, the box is raised on the free square instead. (Since v6 the square is set by two corners, M′ and N′, the second dragged along M′'s line to V_D′; the size stepper is gone.)

The measuring points bring the reach register onto the horizon plate. The picture surrenders true lengths; the measuring points recover them. Swing the eye's distances flat: Mₗ sits on HL at O + |OS′| and Mᵣ at R − |RS′|. Take the seed corner M′ to lie in the picture plane, so the horizontal through it is a ground line on which lengths are true. Then the line from Mᵣ through the far corner X′ cuts the ground line at the true length of the side M′X′, and the line from Mₗ through V′ cuts it at the true length of M′V′. The check file reconstructs the square in three dimensions from those two lengths — eye behind the picture plane at the station's distance, directions taken from the two vanishing points — and projects it back: the corners land on X′, V′ and N′, and the two lengths agree. That agreement is the metric certificate that the drawn figure is a square, the one the horizon bank owed and had only paid by homography. With the prism on, the same ground line gives the box its true height, and k its eye height.

The means come back on the horizon. Level, the ladder HM–GM–AM lives inside the trapezoid and parks with the rail. Tilted, 1/OV_D + 1/OV_D′ = 2/c says that c is the harmonic mean of the two diagonal-VP distances, and the chip draws the classical figure on HL: the two distances laid end to end from O, the semicircle over them split at V_D, the altitude at the split reading GM, the radius reading AM, and the harmonic mean laid from O reaching exactly R. When V_D′ falls left of O the figure reads from R instead, by the same identity reflected. The ladder is not lost by the tilt; it changes address.

Four strata, then. The sieve of v3 sorted claims into rail-borne, form-borne and incidence-borne. The prism, the vertical edge, the eye ratio and the measuring points belong to none of these: they rest on the picture plane being vertical, a hypothesis none of the earlier claims used and none of the tilts disturbs. Call them picture-plane-borne. The free square shows the difference in one figure: its incidences stand at every tilt because they never consult the picture plane; its edge is vertical only when a corner sits in the vertical plane through the eye, and that is picture-plane-borne. The chip groups above the plate are laid out by these four strata, and the rail-borne group dims when the tilt parks it.

Using the sheet. This is a comparison sheet, everything on one ground, kept so the same drag moves every excursion at once. Scenes set a whole configuration in one tap; the ledger under the plate lists each switched-on claim with its value now and whether it stands or is parked; and the page's address carries the entire state, so a configuration worth returning to is a bookmark away.

Glossary

the dictionary
O↔O, 2↔R, D↔N, B↔M between R·T-03's lower figure and R·T-02's trapezoid; under it P = X, station height 2/(4−b) = hc/(b+c).
hinge X = P
The meet of the diagonals O–N and R–M; holder of six offices across the two sheets — chord-bisector, D = D adjudicator, null corner, harmonic center, image point, and slide target.
juxtaposed regions
A = O L M, D = O M X, B = M X N, D = X N R, C = O X R (hatched): five disjoint pieces tiling the rectangle; A + B = C by cancelling D = D across the diagonal O–N.
product form uv
Q(x, y) = xy; null directions are the rectangle's own edges. The hinge plate's frame is the settlement plate's metric.
Minkowski circle on O–M
The Thales locus of Q: the rectangular hyperbola 2xy − hx − ay = 0, null asymptotes, centered at O–M's midpoint, through O and M.
apex P₂
The hyperbola's unique crossing of the right edge, at the hinge's height; the certified Q-right angle, with mirror slopes ±h/(b+c).
half-square
Half the axis rectangle on a segment as diagonal; signed area ½Q. On this plate the three half-squares are A, C and B — the last two after one slide.
null slide
Apex P₂ riding the rail y = hc/(b+c) to X (I.37): both half-squares keep their areas while landing on the tinted regions.
one theorem, two proofs
A + B = C proved by exchange of slivers (D = D) and by signed Pythagoras in uv (the negative square crossing the equals sign); the check file verifies the identities coincide term by term.
circle bank
The semicircle on L–N with apex M′ above M: LM′² + M′N² = LN², altitude M′M = √(ab) — the Euclidean certificate over the same ground.
three mountings
Thales loci above (circle, x² + y²), above (hyperbola, uv), and below (semicircle, the viewer): apex M′, apex P₂, station S.
trade vs tiling
Signature (1,1) signs its squares and trades; positive areas juxtapose and tile. Same identity, two bookkeepings.
horizon bank
HL through O–R: VPₗ, VPᵣ, V_D at m₀, the quad M X N V as the image of a square, S at the 45° seat, and the −1s that survive every projection. The tilt that brings the fourth VP onto the page lives on R·T-02 v7.
bank, price
A quadratic form (or its absence) together with its certificate conic; the same claim priced in metric (right angles, means) or in count (incidences, cross-ratios).
tilt, three strata
Rotating the red line about M (handle at N) sorts the claims: rail-borne (tiling, circle bank, means, the slide) park; form-borne (the hyperbola on O–M and its signed ledger) stand unchanged; incidence-borne (the −1s) stand everywhere.
fourth vanishing point V_D′
Where the tilted red line meets HL; the four VPs then form a complete harmonic range, (VPₗ, VPᵣ ; V_D, V_D′) = −1, equivalently 1/OV_D + 1/OV_D′ = 2/c.
station point S′
At tilt ≠ 0, the meet of the Thales semicircles on O–R and on V_D–V_D′: sides and diagonals each at 90°, so the imaged figure stays a square at every tilt.
parked vs standing
A claim is parked when its hypothesis (parallel rails) fails, standing when its witnesses never used that hypothesis; the equivalence theorem, needing both banks, parks with the weaker one.
the bounce
Post up to the rail, slant to R, next post where the slant crosses the diagonal; as a map of the rail, x → c²/(2c − x) when level.
parabolic projectivity
A projectivity of a line whose two fixed points coincide — here both at N; the case whose orbits crowd harmonically, between cycling (elliptic) and geometric decay (hyperbolic).
harmonic progression
A sequence whose reciprocals are arithmetic; the bounce's reciprocal gaps to N step by exactly 1/c, and each gap is HM(gap, c)/2.
triple −1
(xₙ, xₙ₊₂ ; xₙ₊₁, N) = −1: each post is the harmonic conjugate of N with respect to its neighbors — a chain of −1s sharing one fixed mate, tilt-proof.
matched altitudes
(O, R ; V_D, V_D′) = −1 in product form, OV_D · RV_D′ = V_DR · OV_D′; each side laid end to end on HL from the common foot H = c − OV_D and roofed by its Thales circle, the two altitudes agree. The −1 priced as ab = H², twice.
the foot H
The point of HL where both circles' pieces join: V_D mirrored in m₀. Not the plate's height h; the sketch's letter, kept.
ab = H² circle
Two lengths a, b laid end to end, the Thales circle on a + b, the altitude at the joint: H² = ab. The circle bank's unit, portable to any line.
the prism
The red quad M X N V (ceiling) and its image under y → −k·y (floor), joined by vertical edges: a square box in two-point perspective with the eye on HL.
vertical homology
y → −k·y with HL as axis and the vertical direction as center; it fixes every point of HL, so the floor keeps all four vanishing points and every −1 of the ceiling.
eye ratio k
Eye height above the floor over ceiling height above the eye; the eye is at k/(1+k) of the box's height. k = 1 puts the eye at mid-height and makes the floor the ceiling's reflection.
edge-on face
A face whose plane passes through the eye images as a line. N and V share the vertical v = c, so the face N–V of the plate's box is always edge-on.
grid labels
The sketch's scheme: h₀ for HL and hₙ for the horizontals above it, vₙ for the verticals through O, M, X, R and the vanishing points.
free square
M′ X′ N′ V′, built from the four VPs by joins and meets from a seed corner and a size; the frame's square is the member of the family seated on the leg.
two corners M′, N′
The free square's handles: M′ anywhere, N′ dragged along M′'s line to V_D′; X′ and V′ follow by joins and meets. N′ on the leg recovers the frame's square.
edge-on as event
The edge N′–V′ is vertical exactly when N′ lies on x = c, the vertical plane through the eye toward R; elsewhere it leans. Verticality belongs to the frame, not to the square.
parked free square
A seed from which X′→O runs parallel to M′→V_D′ sends N′ to infinity; the plate parks the square there instead of drawing it wildly. Detected by the sine of the angle between the two lines.
scene
A preset that sets division, tilt, eye ratio, seed and chips at once. "Level" is the v3 headline; "the sketch" is V_D at 3c/4 and V_D′ at 3c/2, the horizon sketch's own setting, where the matched altitudes are legible.
measuring points Mₗ, Mᵣ
The eye's distances to the vanishing points laid flat on HL: Mₗ = O + |OS′|, Mᵣ = R − |RS′|. Lines from them through the far corners cut the ground line at the true side lengths.
ground line
The horizontal through the seed corner M′, taken to lie in the picture plane; lengths along it are true. The choice fixes the square's depth scale, which the picture alone leaves free.
true sides ℓᵣ, ℓₗ
The two side lengths read on the ground line; equal exactly when the figure is a square from S′ — the horizon bank's metric certificate, verified by three-dimensional reconstruction in the check file.
means on the horizon
c = HM(OV_D, OV_D′), with GM and AM beside it on the classical semicircle over the two distances laid from O; the interior ladder re-hired on HL, reading from R when V_D′ is left of O.
picture-plane-borne
The fourth stratum: claims resting on the picture plane being vertical — the prism, the vertical edge, the eye ratio, the measuring points. Untouched by every tilt, unavailable to a general projectivity.
ledger
The table under the plate: one row per switched-on claim, its quantity, its value now, and standing or parked.
permalink
The page's address carries division, tilt, eye ratio, seed, corner and chips; bookmark it to keep a comparison.
the −1 at work
The construction uses V_D′ only; the diagonal X′–V′ reaches V_D unasked, since (O, R ; V_D, V_D′) = −1 — the harmonic mate is what the complete quadrilateral delivers.
