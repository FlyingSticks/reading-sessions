The fold

In the plane the mirror is discrete: no continuous path of congruences of the plane leads from the identity to a mirror, because the sign would have to jump. The wavy fold lines in the sketch already say what changes this. In space, a half-turn about the mirror line — a fold — is continuous, carries a measure (the angle turned, up to 180°), and its trace in the plane, what the plane sees when the fold is complete, is exactly the mirror.

The plane therefore cannot tell whether its mirror is the trace of a mirror in space (odd, one mirror) or of a fold (even, two mirrors). The sign of an ambient map is not determined by its trace. Every mirror in n dimensions is a rotation in n + 1 dimensions, restricted; “discrete” means that the arc lives in a dimension the plane does not have.

Chirality is dimension-relative for the same reason. An L in the plane is chiral: no rotation or translation of the plane superposes it on its mirror image. The same L in space is achiral, because a fold through the spare dimension does superpose it. A figure can be chiral in n dimensions only if it spans all n directions; anything spanning fewer can be turned over through a spare one.

Glossary for this plate

Terms not listed here are as on the One Generator page. Mathematical terms first, then drawing terms.

fold
A half-turn of space about a line lying in the plane. Continuous, even in space, with a measure (the fold angle). Its trace in the plane, when complete, is the mirror in that line. The fold page uses the same word for a related but different thing: counting a point and its half-turn partner as one. The ladder, side plate C, connects the two uses.
trace
What a map of space does to the plane, considered only on the plane. Two different maps of space can have the same trace; in particular a mirror in space and a fold have the same trace, so the trace does not determine the sign of the ambient map.
chiral / achiral
A figure is chiral in a given space if no even congruence of that space superposes it on its mirror image; achiral otherwise. Chirality is relative to the space: a figure can be chiral in n dimensions and achiral in n + 1. A figure is chiral in n dimensions only if it spans all n directions.
hand
The property of a figure that a mirror reverses and an even congruence preserves. “Opposite hand” means mirror image.
pin
Drawing term. A segment with a head at one end, drawn as a black stroke with a red disc. It represents an oriented segment: a vector with a position.
head
Drawing term. The red disc marking which end of a pin is which.
L
Drawing term. The figure used as a probe with a hand: a short bar and a long bar meeting at a right angle, unequal so that its mirror image cannot be rotated onto it.
fold line
Drawing term. The drawn line about which a fold is performed; the same line that the resulting mirror fixes.
mirror line
Drawing term. The drawn line of a mirror; the line of its fixed points.
plan / elevation
Drawing terms. Plan: the plane viewed face-on. Elevation: the same plane viewed edge-on, so that a fold is seen as an arc.
