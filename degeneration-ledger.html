The degeneration ledger

The means page said of the pinhole: one event, seen twice. This ledger sees it fourteen times. One dial — the half-gap ε between the slits and their common limit — is walked to zero, and every object of the figure files its outcome under a verdict: survives, survives trivially, mutates, revives in the blow-up, decays to gauge, or dies — in the spirit of the Euclid's-Five treatment, where the question is never only whether a thing fails but what it turns into. The left panel is the true scale, where the figure fuses; the right panel is the blow-up chart, the same scene with the axis and heights rescaled by ε, where much of what died stands back up. The glass, which owns the true scale, is exiled to infinity in the chart — an echo, in miniature, of the seam's exiled meeting.

What to look for

Three different fates hide inside "everything fuses." The cross-ratio never wavers: (B, D; C, A) = −1 at every ε > 0, and only its points collapse — at ε = 0 the expression is 0/0, and the blow-up regularizes the points so the −1 stands in the chart as (−1, +1; 0, ∞), the conjugate of infinity being the midpoint. The means fuse and then revive one order down: their spreads are second order, and because AM × HM = GM² holds all the way, the geometric mean lands exactly midway — (AM − GM)/(GM − HM) → 1, the three means re-separating in arithmetic progression at scale ε². And orthogonality decays to incidence: the two circles cross at right angles for every ε, and when one of them dies to a point, the right angle has nothing left to measure — what remains is that the dead circle's point lies on the survivor, which is the classical reading of a null circle orthogonal to a live one.

The mirror's fate is the ledger's best mutation. Inversion in the GM circle, viewed in the chart, flattens into the half-turn u ↦ −u about the crease: it still swaps the slits (∓1), still fixes the midpoint where C and GM have both marched, and its fixed circle steepens into the vertical line through the crease. An inversion seen at small scale near its own circle is a reflection — the ledger watches that classical fact arrive. Meanwhile the square's construction splits its verdicts: the incidence law (the fourth ray lands unaided) survives in the chart, while the meeting point of the diagonal and the sliver's tip halt at 1 − t and (1 − t)/(1 + t) — numbers that persist but report only the free starting choice. The limit is where facts and gauges, which the drawing had let look alike, are forced to declare themselves.

A degeneration is not a death; it is a sorting. The pinhole limit sends every entry of the figure to its own address: laws that hold at every ε keep holding; laws about fused points revive one chart down or one order down; right angles migrate to survivors; inversions flatten to reflections; and the numbers that halt at values set by free choices are thereby exposed as gauges. The ledger is the sorting, written out.

Checks

Exact at every ε > 0, re-run on every interaction, computed from the drawn geometry: C is the constructed tangency foot, E the constructed circle crossing, F the constructed perpendicular foot, and both squares are ruler-and-straightedge intersections — one in the true scale, one re-run in the chart. The convergence rates claimed in the ledger's third column are asserted in check_degeneration_ledger.js by comparing deviations across a decade of ε.

Conventions

Judgment calls flagged: the vertex S is pinned to its limit position (0.55 P, 0.5 P) so the collapse is the range's doing, not the vertex's drifting; the blow-up square is the construction re-run in the chart — the true-scale square's image would sit at infinity there, since S is macroscopic; the approach is symmetric (B, D → P at equal rates), and the asymmetric case is asserted in the check script — the chart range becomes (−1, +2; ½, ∞), still harmonic, the midpoint moving where the midpoint should; both frames are fixed rather than auto-fit, because a ledger about convergence must not let the camera chase what converges. The level set at z* is a plane and is called one.

Glossary of verdicts

survives
Holds at every ε and holds with content at the limit. The perpendicular foot F and its right angle; the mirror's fixed points, arriving at ±P.
survives trivially
Holds throughout while its content drains: the scales law, whose two channels agree about everything once they coincide.
mutates
Survives as a different kind of statement: orthogonality → incidence; inversion → half-turn; tangent length → distance to the pinhole.
revives in the blow-up
Dead at true scale, alive in ε-units: the equator as the unit circle, the range as (−1, +1; 0, ∞), the eye at the summit, the square with a horizon-parallel diagonal.
decays to gauge
The number persists but reports a choice: the diagonal's meeting point at 1 − t, the sliver's tip at (1 − t)/(1 + t).
dies
Nothing to inherit: the two tangents as two distinct lines; the crossing angle of the two circles as an angle.
blow-up · chart
The scene in coordinates (x − P)/ε, y/ε: a microscope whose magnification tracks the degeneration exactly.
crease
The mirror's trace in the chart: the vertical line the fixed circle steepens into; the axis of the surviving half-turn.
exiled
Sent to infinity by the chart, as the glass is: present in every finite drawing, unreachable in the limit's own coordinates.
