What was corrected

The four steps of the cross-ratio are A→Q, C→S, A→S, C→Q. The sketches’ long bar ran S→Q — not one of the four — and its 8 : 4 = 2 : 1 held only at Q = 2. Here the outside split is stated as S→A : S→C, which mirrors A→Q : Q→C at every position of Q. The 8 has not been lost: it is M→C, and it belongs to the area picture. The two circles, in coordination

Each circle owns one rectangle-and-square. The circle on A–C (drawn above the line, with the tangent from S and the drop T→Q) enforces OQ · OS = OA²: its square is fixed at 3² = 9, whatever Q does. The circle on Q–S, recorded in the braces below the line, enforces MA · MC = MQ²: its square breathes with Q. In each plate the straight diagonal through the shared corner is Euclid I.43 — complements about the diameter — so straight diagonal and equal areas are the same statement.

The two readings of the axes are the sketch’s own: reach is the rectangle stretching — as Q approaches the midpoint of A–C, S and both rectangles run off toward the infinite (the slider stops at 2.3 to keep them on the page; the identities do not stop). Turn is the tilt of the diagonal, the ratio changing while the product holds. Past the midpoint the whole figure mirrors to the right of C.

The third plate: the quarter-turn

The third plate is the O-plate sent to unit scale. Divide everything by OA = 3: the anchors A, C become A′, C′ = ±1, the factors become n = OS⁄3 and 1⁄n = OQ⁄3, and the fixed square becomes the unit square — n · 1⁄n = 1, whatever Q does. The quarter-turn about the corner (0, 1) then sends the reach mark n to −1⁄n: the same involution with the product flipped to −1, and the fixed points expelled from the line to ±i.

The corner (0, 1) is where ±i touches the paper: every semicircle on [−1⁄n, n] passes through it, whatever n — that right angle is the Thales witness of the turn, and it fails if the minus sign is dropped. The radius is the arithmetic mean of n and 1⁄n; the altitude is their geometric mean, 1, the square’s own side. So the trio reads: real anchors and product +R² (plate one), real anchors and a breathing square (plate two), imaginary anchors and product −1 (plate three). At Q = 2 the radius triangle is 3-4-5 in thirds.
