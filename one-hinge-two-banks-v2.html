Reading the plate

One point, two dossiers. Under the dictionary O↔O, 2↔R, D↔N, B↔M, the lower figure of the settlement plate (R·T-03) is the hinge configuration of R·T-02: the lines B–2 and O–D are R–M and O–N, and their crossing P is X, with the station heights agreeing verbatim — 2/(4−b) there is hc/(b+c) here, once the two sheets' opposite conventions for b are translated. Two check files had been metering one point from two sides. On this plate it is drawn once and labeled with both names.

The headline: one theorem, two proofs. In the hinge's own coordinates, the Minkowski circle of the product form Q(x, y) = xy on diameter O–M — the gold rectangular hyperbola, null asymptotes, centered at O–M's midpoint — crosses the right edge at exactly one point, P₂ = (c, hc/(b+c)): the hinge's height. The angle O–P₂–M is Q-right, so signed Pythagoras gives Q(OP₂) + Q(P₂M) = Q(OM), legs of opposite type. Now read the squares: the half-square on O–M is the region A vertex for vertex; the half-square on O–P₂ slides along the null rail y = hc/(b+c) — apex P₂ riding to X, Euclid I.37 — into the hatched C; the half-square on P₂–M, whose rectangle owns the corners M and N, slides by the same motion into the green B. The negative square crosses the equals sign and the trade becomes the tiling: A + B = C, the juxtaposed identity, is the uv-Pythagoras wearing region form. What D = D proves by exchange of slivers, the hyperbola proves by signature. Trade versus tiling is (1,1) versus positive-definite — two receipts, one purchase.

Three mountings of one instrument. Every certificate on either sheet is a Thales locus on a diameter. The circle bank hangs it above the rail: the semicircle on L–N with apex M′ over M, where LM′² + M′N² = LN² and the altitude M′M reads √(ab) — the geometric mean, the sum bank's quadratic certificate. The uv bank hangs its conic over the same ground: the hyperbola on O–M with apex P₂, right angle by the null-mirror law, equal and opposite slopes ±h/(b+c). The horizon bank hangs it below: the semicircle on O–R with the station point S, from which O and R subtend 90° and whose vertical strikes V_D — the viewer's right angle. Same sentence, three banks; and each bank keeps a degenerate resting state (a null diameter's circle collapsing to a line pair; the untilted second Thales circle straightened into a vertical).

The means keep their addresses. Inside the figure: HM is the chord through X, bisected by it; GM is the altitude — √(ab) at M′ upstairs, the signed relation Q(P₂F) = Q(OP₂)·Q(P₂M)/Q(OM) in the uv bank; AM is the midline, and the circle bank's radius. Under tilt (R·T-02 v7) the harmonic mean is evicted from the interior and re-hired on the horizon as 1/OV_D + 1/OV_D′ = 2/c. The hinge is where the three registers make change for one another.

What each excursion keeps. The metric-ward sheet (R·T-03) can say why there is a right angle — it owns the forms, its certificates are conics, its −1s implicit. The projective-ward sheet (R·T-02 v7) can say what survives when the forms are taken away — its certificates are cross-ratios, its metric duties exiled to a single point below the page. Bracketed together: one congruence, priced in metric and priced in count, with the cross-ratio as the currency every bank honors. This plate is their common ground drawn as one figure.

The tilt handle then runs the sieve over the banks themselves, and it sorts three strata, not two. Tilting the red line about M parks the rail-borne claims — the five-region tiling, the circle bank (whose diameter was the level rail), the means ladder, and the null slide, since X leaves the rail y = hc/(b+c). But the uv bank stands: the hyperbola's diameter O–M and the apex P₂ never consulted the rail, so the signed ledger ½Q(OM) = ½Q(OP₂) + ½Q(P₂M) reads the same numbers at every tilt — the caption shows them unchanged while the tiling beside them is parked. And the incidence-borne stratum stands with it: the fourth vanishing point arrives on HL, the four VPs lock into (VPₗ, VPᵣ ; V_D, V_D′) = −1 with 1/OV_D + 1/OV_D′ = 2/c, and S′ slides to the meet of the two Thales semicircles, still certifying a square. So the sieve grades finer than affine-versus-projective: claims borne by the rail, claims borne by the form, claims borne by incidence alone — and the equivalence theorem, which needs both the tiling and the form, parks with its weaker partner, exactly as a joint account should.

Glossary

the dictionary
O↔O, 2↔R, D↔N, B↔M between R·T-03's lower figure and R·T-02's trapezoid; under it P = X, station height 2/(4−b) = hc/(b+c).
hinge X = P
The meet of the diagonals O–N and R–M; holder of six offices across the two sheets — chord-bisector, D = D adjudicator, null corner, harmonic center, image point, and slide target.
juxtaposed regions
A = O L M, D = O M X, B = M X N, D = X N R, C = O X R (hatched): five disjoint pieces tiling the rectangle; A + B = C by cancelling D = D across the diagonal O–N.
product form uv
Q(x, y) = xy; null directions are the rectangle's own edges. The hinge plate's frame is the settlement plate's metric.
Minkowski circle on O–M
The Thales locus of Q: the rectangular hyperbola 2xy − hx − ay = 0, null asymptotes, centered at O–M's midpoint, through O and M.
apex P₂
The hyperbola's unique crossing of the right edge, at the hinge's height; the certified Q-right angle, with mirror slopes ±h/(b+c).
half-square
Half the axis rectangle on a segment as diagonal; signed area ½Q. On this plate the three half-squares are A, C and B — the last two after one slide.
null slide
Apex P₂ riding the rail y = hc/(b+c) to X (I.37): both half-squares keep their areas while landing on the tinted regions.
one theorem, two proofs
A + B = C proved by exchange of slivers (D = D) and by signed Pythagoras in uv (the negative square crossing the equals sign); the check file verifies the identities coincide term by term.
circle bank
The semicircle on L–N with apex M′ above M: LM′² + M′N² = LN², altitude M′M = √(ab) — the Euclidean certificate over the same ground.
three mountings
Thales loci above (circle, x² + y²), above (hyperbola, uv), and below (semicircle, the viewer): apex M′, apex P₂, station S.
trade vs tiling
Signature (1,1) signs its squares and trades; positive areas juxtapose and tile. Same identity, two bookkeepings.
horizon bank
HL through O–R: VPₗ, VPᵣ, V_D at m₀, the quad M X N V as the image of a square, S at the 45° seat, and the −1s that survive every projection. The tilt that brings the fourth VP onto the page lives on R·T-02 v7.
bank, price
A quadratic form (or its absence) together with its certificate conic; the same claim priced in metric (right angles, means) or in count (incidences, cross-ratios).
tilt, three strata
Rotating the red line about M (handle at N) sorts the claims: rail-borne (tiling, circle bank, means, the slide) park; form-borne (the hyperbola on O–M and its signed ledger) stand unchanged; incidence-borne (the −1s) stand everywhere.
fourth vanishing point V_D′
Where the tilted red line meets HL; the four VPs then form a complete harmonic range, (VPₗ, VPᵣ ; V_D, V_D′) = −1, equivalently 1/OV_D + 1/OV_D′ = 2/c.
station point S′
At tilt ≠ 0, the meet of the Thales semicircles on O–R and on V_D–V_D′: sides and diagonals each at 90°, so the imaged figure stays a square at every tilt.
parked vs standing
A claim is parked when its hypothesis (parallel rails) fails, standing when its witnesses never used that hypothesis; the equivalence theorem, needing both banks, parks with the weaker one.
