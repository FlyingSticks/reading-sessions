The GM mirror

The laws card of the last plate listed seven equalities. This plate says they are one symmetry, seen seven times. Invert in the circle of radius GM centred at the glass — the map x · x′ = ab — and the whole diagram trades partners: the slits swap, AM and HM swap, the glass trades with the ideal point, and GM and the touch point T hold still. Reciprocation on the rate square is this mirror performed in the rig's own unit, since GM² = ab. Drag the marks, drag the probe, then pull the fold slider and watch every conjugate pair ride its semicircle through the mirror at a right angle.

The green dashed circle is the mirror: radius GM, centred at the glass, meeting the axis at the GM mark (and, behind the glass, at −GM). Fold: B rides the equator to D, crossing the mirror at T; HM rides its own semicircle to AM; the draggable ◆ probe rides its pair-circle to ab/x. Every crossing carries a right-angle tick, because every circle through a conjugate pair is orthogonal to the mirror — the closing law AM × HM = GM² is just this power law at one particular pair.

What to look for

The mirror meets the axis at the GM mark — that is what "GM is fixed" looks like as incidence. The tangent's touch point T lies on the mirror too (its distance from the glass is GM), and the equator crosses the mirror exactly there, at right angles: the orthogonality is the law AM² − GM² = R², which the last plate carried as a line in a card and this plate draws as a crossing. Since the equator is orthogonal to the mirror, the mirror carries the equator to itself — the sphere is its own image.

The eye's circle is the one thing that does not come back: it passes through the centre of inversion, so it opens out into a line — the transverse plane through the sphere's centre — and the eye E lands on the summit of the equator. The station point and the sphere's north pole are mirror partners. Meanwhile A trades with the ideal point, which is the degeneration of the last plate seen in reverse: the harmonic range (B, D; C, A) = −1 maps to (D, B; M, ∞) = −1 — the conjugate of infinity is the midpoint — so the glass's harmonic story and the midpoint's harmonic story are the same fact on the two sides of the mirror.

One symmetry, seven shadows. Swap of the slits, swap of AM and HM, fixity of GM, fixity of T, self-duality of GM under reciprocation, orthogonality of equator and mirror, and the closing law AM × HM = GM² are one map — inversion with the power of the glass. The rate square's reciprocal duality is this mirror in the rig's own unit: r = 1/u is x′ = ab/x with ab set to one.

Counts · Laws · Gauges · Defects

Counts

  • 1 mirror · 2 fixed points on the axis, at ±GM from the glass.
  • 2 slits trade in 1 stroke; so do AM and HM; so do glass and ideal point.
  • 1 crossing that matters twice: mirror × equator = T, the tangency.
  • Every conjugate pair rides 1 semicircle; each crosses the mirror in 2 right angles (one drawn).
  • 9 live checks in the ledger below.

Laws

  • x · x′ = GM² — the mirror's definition is the closing law, stated for every pair at once.
  • B ↔ D, HM ↔ AM, A ↔ ∞; GM and T fixed.
  • Cross-ratio survives the mirror; in particular (B, D; C, A) = −1 ↦ (D, B; M, ∞) = −1.
  • Equator ⊥ mirror (AM² − GM² = R²), hence the sphere is self-inverse; the crossing is T.
  • Circle on AC ↦ the plane at AM; E ↦ the summit. The eye and the north pole are partners.
  • Reciprocation = the mirror in the rig's unit, GM² = ab.

Gauges

  • Centred at the glass. Move the glass and the mirror rides along; the power ab is measured from wherever O stands.
  • The fold path is a choice. The map is the two endpoints; the semicircle is one certified road between them (orthogonal all the way). Other roads exist.
  • The unit is the rig's own. GM² = ab is what makes reciprocation land back in depth units instead of rates.

Defects

  • Glass between the slits: the power ab goes negative and the mirror leaves the reals with GM — but the map survives as an anti-mirror (x′ = ab/x with ab < 0), which still swaps the slits while fixing nothing real. The page draws the arrows and not the circle.
  • Pinhole limit: the slits fall onto the mirror and stop trading; AM and HM fuse there too. The mirror survives; its cast of movers does not.

Checks

Re-run on every interaction, from the drawn geometry: T is the Thales-circle construction, E is the crossing of the two drawn circles, the equator–mirror crossing is computed as a crossing and compared to T, and the eye's image is the constructed E pushed through the map — the closed forms sit on the other side of each residual.

Conventions

Judgment calls flagged: the mirror is drawn as an arc and allowed to leave the frame — its far fixed point at −GM sits behind the glass and is brought into view by a toggle rather than by squeezing the scene; the fold animates both partners of each pair, which therefore pass each other mid-arc (at the summit, for B and D); the probe defaults to the sketch's 469, whose partner ab/469 lands just short of the near slit. The level set at z* is a plane and is called one.

Glossary

inversion · mirror
The map sending x to O + ab/(x − O): distance times image-distance equals the power, GM².
power of a point
For the glass and the means circle, ab; positive outside, zero on, negative inside.
conjugate pair
A point and its image, x and ab/x. Slits are one pair; AM and HM another; glass and ideal point a third.
fixed circle
The mirror itself: every point at distance GM from the glass stays put, T included.
orthogonal circle
A circle crossing the mirror at right angles — equivalently, one through a conjugate pair. Carried to itself.
anti-mirror
The same formula with negative power: still an involution swapping the slits, with no real fixed circle.
summit
The top of the equator, over the centre; the mirror image of the eye E.
